## M. The camera model (the AI's proposal, for the human to approve or change)

**One idea: the camera is an eye looking in a direction. Every movement is the eye moving or turning.** No separate "target" and "distance" stored as state (those are what went wrong in the zoom bugs: `in_200`, `in_230`, `in_368`, `in_413`, `in_415`).

| Input | What it does to the eye |
|---|---|
| Wheel, pinch, W / S | Move along a ray: for the wheel and pinch the ray through the pointer, for W / S the ray through the view centre. A step is a fraction of the distance to what is ahead on that ray, so it never overshoots a surface and never gets stuck far away. |
| A / D, middle-drag, trackpad scroll, two fingers | Move across the view (sideways, up and down). |
| Mouse in game mode | Turn about the eye. |
| Left-drag, right-drag, Alt+left-drag | Orbit: turn the eye and move it round a pivot (the nearest object's centre, found when the drag starts, not stored) so the pivot stays where it is on screen. |
| X Y Z buttons, panes | Go to a straight view of a plane: set the direction, back the eye off and narrow the lens, eased. |

**What "what is ahead" means:** the distance to the surface under the ray; else how far in front of the eye (along the view) the nearest object's centre is, so a zoom goes toward it and never past it and the same zoom back returns exactly; else, with everything behind, how far away the nearest object or the origin is. This is the only place the camera needs to look at the scene; everything else is pure maths.

**Scroll zoom and game-mode forward are the same movement** in perspective: the eye going along a ray. They differ only in what drives them (a wheel notch, a key held over time) and which ray (the pointer's, the centre's). Zooming toward the pointer comes free: moving along the pointer's ray keeps the point under the pointer where it is on screen, and the same steps back return to the same place.

**Straight (orthographic) views need no exception, if the lens is part of the state.** A straight view is the same camera with a very narrow lens and the eye far back (that is already how the prototype eases in and out: `mix`). Moving the eye along a ray then zooms it just as in perspective, because the picture's size follows the eye's distance; pan is the same sideways movement. So the state is **the eye, the direction, and the lens** (how wide: normal, or nearly zero). Going to a straight view narrows the lens while the eye backs off so the picture holds its size; leaving it reverses that, in one eased change. Between the two there is no resting state (C9). *Check when built:* that a lens this narrow still looks orthographic and does not z-fight (the prototype's limit was 2 % perspective).

**Not stored:** a target, a distance, a zoom number, a focus, a scale. **Stored (and saved in a file):** the eye, the direction, and the lens.

**Two primitives, one step rule, one lens.** *Move* the eye (along a ray, or across the view) and *turn* about a point; the step is a fraction of the depth ahead; the lens is normal or narrow. Everything in C2 to C7 is built from these.

**Costs and cautions:** the one scene query (depth ahead) must be fast; the eye can be far from the origin, so the drawing is done relative to the eye (as games do), which also removes far-from-origin glitches; the 60 px hold before leaving a straight view stays.

*Confirmed by the human (`in_1399`): game mode turns the camera directly; otherwise it turns about the centre of the nearest object in front of it, or the other defaults above.*

**Rotate, in both modes, is one operation:** turn the camera by a yaw (about world Z) and a pitch (about its own right side), rigidly, **about a point**. It has no roll, so Z stays up. The only difference is the point.
- **Orbit (not game mode):** the point is the pivot, so the eye swings round it and the pivot stays where it is on screen. The pivot is the centre of the nearest object in front of the eye, found when the drag starts and kept for that drag; with no object, a point straight ahead at a default distance (never the origin far behind).
- **Look (game mode):** the point is the eye itself, so the eye stays and the view turns, like turning your head. It is orbit with the pivot at distance zero.
- **Direction:** both turn the camera the same way for the same movement (the mouse or drag to the right turns the camera to the right, and down tilts it down); they differ only in the point, so orbit swings the eye round the pivot and look turns it in place. One angle per pixel; game mode scales it down (0.25) and smooths it, and the smallest movements are less sensitive in both (`in_420`).
- **Limits:** pitch stops straight up and straight down; when it stops, the eye stops too (it is not moved on its own). A straight view is left only after the 60 px hold (`in_193`), and orbit then turns about the focus.

## What this cuts, against the prototype

Measured on the prototype (`app/`): about 1,160 lines in the files that are only the world view and camera (camera maths, the camera intents, the last-seen and reset code, the world drawing, panes, orb, buttons, input recogniser), and the words `straight`, `orthoDebt`, `mixT0` or `mixBase` appear 305 times across the code, because straight views are a special case everywhere.
- **No stored target or distance**, so none of the formulas that keep them honest: the three zoom paths in `zoomAt` (anchor, content, minimum surface), the far-from-origin fix-ups.
- **No separate straight-view logic** in zoom, orbit, in-view tests and walking: the lens does it. The perspective state machine (`mixT0`, `mixBase`, `orthoDebt`) becomes one eased lens value plus the 60 px hold.
- **Orbit and game look are one function** (the point differs); walking and zooming are one function.
- **Fewer settings:** the scroll mode switch (auto, pan, zoom), the zoom-to-cursor switch, the per-second zoom cap. Zoom to the pointer is simply how zoom works; the wheel-or-trackpad decision stays automatic.
- **Left out of this section** (they belong to later ones): the grid of a selected face's plane while sketching (`in_385`), going normal to a sketch and the cut, the hover disc's selection behaviour beyond its look, touch gestures for tools, the trackpad and keyboard settings page (`in_661`).
- **Estimate, to be checked:** perhaps half the code of the prototype for the same behaviour. The first from-scratch camera (`next/`, in git) was about 600 lines without the reset, the pivot, the depth query, the button highlights, the disc or the game-mode centre cursor, so 800 to 900 is the likelier size.
