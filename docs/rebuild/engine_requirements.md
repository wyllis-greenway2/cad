# The engine: the requirements (a draft from six spikes)

Status: **a draft, `ai-unreviewed`**. The human asked for the engine to be thought through (`in_1463` to `in_1465`), for the spikes (`in_1467` to `in_1475`), and for these requirements to be written; he has **not yet reviewed them**, so no row here is a decision. What is decided is only `dec_scope_real_cad` (the product is real CAD: the exact geometry is the model, a mesh is only a picture of it, exchange with other CAD programs) and, from before, `dec_kernel_hybrid` (OCCT now behind a kernel adapter, `inv_kernel_boundary`) and `prin_history_additive`. No code and no tests are written for this section until the human is happy with it (`dec_rebuild_by_requirements`, `in_1444`).

How to read a row: as in `world_view_requirements.md`. **Source** says where it comes from: the human's inputs, and the spike (a throwaway prototype under `prototypes/`, each with a README that says what it did and did not show). A row marked "to settle" is a rule the spikes found but whose exact form is the human's to choose.

## What this section is for
The old app failed in a few ways that came from how it kept the model, not from the screen: a recipe replayed through the kernel with **names that were positions** (a later step could not find its face again), **rounding patches** against slivers, a **whole model going blank** when one step failed or ran past a time limit, a history that **cost the square of its length**, and a **hidden sketch kept beside the body's own edges**. The human asked for an engine in which a line is a line whether it was drawn or is an edge of a body, which keeps fillets and editing an earlier step with the rest following, and which "always works". Honest limit, found by the spikes: **the kernel will sometimes fail on a valid shape** (about one random edit in forty on the human's own saved models, `prototypes/real-models`), and no design prevents that; what the design can promise is that **the model is never lost, a failure stays where it happened and is explained, and a reference never quietly attaches to the wrong thing.**

## The shape of it
- **Truth:** the recipe, a graph of steps. A step has a kind, numbers, and inputs that are other steps or ids of faces and edges. Its result is a body (an exact B-rep solid kept by OCCT), or a sketch.
- **Kernel:** each step, run, returns the new body **and what every face and edge of the old one became** (the kernel's own history, completed by exact rules where the kernel says nothing). That is the **lineage**.
- **Index:** from the body the engine derives one list of **vertices, edges and faces with lasting ids**, the same list for what was drawn and what was made. The interface selects, snaps and constrains through it; it is a cache of the kernel's answer, never stored in history.
- **Failure:** a step is `ok`, `flagged` (it failed, its input passes through) or `orphaned` (a reference it names is gone). The model stays shown.
- Planned places (not contracts until the files exist): `src/engine/` with the kernel adapter, the step graph, the lineage, and the index; the interface layers above it use only these.

## Face pull modes (a draft; each is one intent, E24 to E26, E32)
A pull is a face and a signed distance `d` along the face's normal (outward positive), or a **target** instead of a distance: *up to a face*, *through all*, *to the next*. A target is the way to finish a hole: the tool overshoots the surface it must clear, so the end of the tool never lies on or near the surface it breaks through (the numeric stop of the ring experiment, E25, is what made it fragile). For each mode: where it applies, what the kernel is asked, which ids survive, the volume law, when it refuses, and the edge cases.

**1. Change the original** (edit the step that made the face)
- *Applies* when the face's id names a step role with a number that moves it: an extrusion's cap (depth), a hole's end (depth), a revolve's end (angle), a shell's inner face (thickness), a loft's section. Not for a face made by a split, an import, or a fillet.
- *Kernel* is asked nothing new: the step runs again with its number changed by `d` (E20); every later step follows (E18).
- *Ids* are those the step makes (they are named by role, E6), so later steps keep finding their faces; a chamfer on the cap stays on the cap.
- *Volume law* is the step's own; no promise beyond E14.
- *Refuses* when the number would leave its range (depth to zero or past the far side for a push that must stay blind): the answer names the range. A negative depth is **not** flipped to the other side.
- *Edge cases.* The face is a **piece** of a split face (E8): the edit moves the whole step, so the answer says which other faces move and asks. The face has **two origins** (merged, E9): offer each. The step has **other users** (later steps read its end by id): list them in the trial; some may flag. The face is one of several made by one number (both caps of a symmetric extrude): ask which end.

**2. Push** (extrude the outline)
- *Applies* to a **planar** face. A curved face has no outline-prism (use mode 4).
- *Kernel* is asked for a prism of the face (with its holes) along its normal by `|d|`, joined to the body for `d > 0` and cut from it for `d < 0`; the new step is a `push` with the face's id as input.
- *Ids* of the new side faces are named by the outline's edges; the end face carries the pushed face's id; faces the prism ran onto merge (coplanar merge, E7) and keep both ids (E9).
- *Volume law* is exact: the volume changes by area times `|d|` (E27b); a result off by more than the tolerance is **flagged**, not passed. This is the check that would have caught the 0.023 mm³ of the ring experiment.
- *Refuses* when `d < 0` and the prism leaves the body on the far side by more than the body is thick (it would cut through: asked first, then done as a through cut), or when the outline would run onto a **curved neighbour** at near tangency (E29: the gap is named, a target is offered).
- *Edge cases.* **Chamfers or fillets around the face stay where they are**, so a ledge is left: the person asked for this mode, the preview shows it, and mode 3 is the one that grows them. Neighbours that are walls of the original (`90°`) merge into one wall. A face with **holes** keeps them (the prism is of the face, holes included); a push that is `d < 0` past the end of a hole closes nothing new. If the same face is also the cap of an extrusion, the answer offers mode 1.

**3. Move with neighbours** (the face's plane moves; the faces beside it are extended or cut back to meet it)
- *Applies* when the face and **all faces around it** are planar (chamfers included; a fillet is a cylinder and does not qualify unless mode 4 is used for it). Not offered otherwise, with the reason.
- *Kernel.* There is no single call for this in OCCT. It is **our own region rule (built as a spike, `in_1495`, `prototypes/face-modes`)**: the tool is the slab between the old and the new plane, kept on the side of each neighbour's plane where the face lies, and it is added (outward) or taken (inward); no face is patched. **Measured:** the chamfered block of the human's example pulled +2, +5, +10 and +11.9 mm equals the analytic volume to three decimals, with the chamfers growing and the top shrinking; the collapse distance is found by halving (12.0000 mm); pockets' floors and an L-block's notch wall are exact; a curved neighbour is refused.
- *Ids* of every face stay (the neighbours are the same faces, longer or shorter); a neighbour that shrinks to nothing is **deleted** and its id is lost (E10).
- *Volume law* is the integral of the face's area as it changes; the engine computes it from the new vertices and checks it against the kernel's.
- *Refuses* at the distance `d*` where a neighbour or the face collapses (the chamfers meet in a ridge, a square block ends in a pyramid): the answer gives `d*` and **offers to stop there**. Past `d*` is refused, never continued as a different shape.
- *Edge cases.* Two neighbours parallel to each other and to the move (the walls of a block): they stay, the face just moves (as mode 1 or 2 would). A neighbour that grows into **another feature** (the chamfer reaches a hole): the result is checked for validity and for self-contact, and refused with the pair of faces named. Neighbours not planar: refused with the face named.

**4. Offset** (move the surface itself: a cylinder's radius, a sphere's, a draft)
- *Applies* to cylinders, cones, spheres and planes with **no step behind them** (otherwise mode 1 is the better answer and is offered first).
- *Kernel.* OCCT's offset of one face **is not available** (`BRepOffset_MakeOffset` is not in this build; the offset of a shape moves every face). The mode is **our own region rule, as mode 3** (spike 8): the ring between the old and the new radius, kept on the side of each flat neighbour's plane, added or taken; a wall split at the kernel's seam is moved as one wall. **Measured:** holes (through and blind) and a boss, radius changed up to and past the point where the hole breaks out of the block, give the same part as the one made fresh with that radius (same faces, volume to 1e-11, one extra or fewer seam-split face in the broken-out cases).
- *Ids* as for mode 3; a hole's wall keeps its id when its radius changes.
- *Refuses* when the offset would make the radius zero or negative, or the surface would cross a neighbour's end.
- *Edge cases.* A fillet face: offset is a change of its radius and is offered as an edit of the round step (mode 1). Cones, spheres and tori (the ring becomes a revolved profile) and axes not along Z are **not built**; a neighbour that is not flat is refused.

**Not a mode.** *Extend* (as in the old app) is mode 3 when the neighbours are planar and mode 2 otherwise; naming two things "extend" is the cause of "it was not clear why and when they would work how they did". The old name goes; the four above stay, each with one rule.

## Rounds: where the kernel stops, and why (a draft from the old app's decisions and `dev/round-experiment.ts`)
- **Why a rod-end fillet fails when it is bigger than the rod's radius.** A fillet is the surface of a ball rolling while touching *both* faces. On the end of a rod of radius `r` the flat top is a disc of radius `r`; with a ball of size `s` the ball touches the top at radius `r - s`. At `s = r` the touch is the centre (a dome); above `r` there is no touch point on that top (the disc has gone), so the *definition* has nothing to roll on and the kernel's algorithm stops. The shape itself still makes sense; only that definition ends.
- **The pointed ("gothic") end is a different, well-defined shape, not a bigger fillet.** Keep the top's height `h`; an arc of radius `s` is tangent to the wall at a distance `t = sqrt(2*r*s - r*r)` below the top and ends on the axis at the top point. At `s = r` it is the dome (`t = r`); above `r` it is a pointed arch, getting longer with `s`, until `t` reaches the rod's height (`s = (h*h + r*r) / (2*r)`), which is the limit past which it refuses. It is a **revolve of that profile, cut from the rod**: exact, no rolling ball, so it cannot fail where the kernel's fillet does. (**Built as a spike, `in_1489`:** valid at every size from the dome to the limit, volumes equal to the hand-worked integral to 1e-11, equal to the kernel's fillet at sizes up to the radius; the old app's rim region saturated at the dome and did not make the point, `in_510`.) Whether *continue past the limit* makes a dome (saturate) or the pointed end is the human's to choose (E35).
- **The saddle edge of a rod crossed by another** has an angle between faces that changes all along it (spikes 7B and 7F, `prototypes/round-limits`). For ordinary crossings the kernel's constant round works. It fails in a **band of near-degenerate geometry**: for fillets a shallow crossing (30 degrees and less) with radii close to but not exactly equal, and 45 degrees with radii equal to within 0.001; for chamfers radii within 0.1 of each other at most sizes, and exactly equal radii give an invalid solid. 118 of 200 grid cases failed (a grid chosen to be hard). **Nothing cheap rescues them**: a size nudge of at most 0.01 mm rescued 0, rounding the edges one after the other 0. A size that **follows the wedge angle** turned two invalid constant results into valid ones and did not help the equal-radius case. **The closing by two offsets does not work in this build** (the erosion fails); a **region** (the air within s of both surfaces, minus the body, minus a pipe of radius s round the spine) was taken further (`in_1501`): the pipe is valid in Frenet mode, a pipe of exactly the radius s is tangent to both surfaces and the boolean with it gives garbage, a pipe a hair larger gives a valid solid with **about 50% too much material** (the air beyond the contact curves is counted), and 7 to 8 s: **not a route**. Until a tier-2 construction exists, this case is tier 3: **refused with the limit and the options of E35** (change an angle or a radius, round before the crossing is made, a variable size, or accept a chamfer-free joint).
- **Old lessons kept** (`dec_edits_saturate`): a round is a set operation on regions, saturating rather than failing; the kernel's own round is tried first so sizes that worked give the same body; a cut that took off nothing is detected (a valid solid can be wrong, `in_514`); the handover between the kernel's round and the region must be continuous in volume (`in_505`); a chain of tangent edges is rounded as a whole (the kernel follows it, `in_507`, `in_508`).
- **Not built** (prototype `dec_edits_saturate`'s still-not-built): the exact rolling ball between curved faces; corners of chamfers not at right angles; more than five size points. Every such case is tier 3 (refuse with the limit) until built.
