# What is worked out of a body is worked out once for the body

**Asked (`in_701`, approved `in_726`):** adding a gear should cost what that gear costs, not what the whole model costs.

**Found (measured, six gears of 30 teeth added one after another, Node, each step the new gear's sketch and extrude):** the new body's own work (extrude, its validity and self-crossing checks, its mesh) was about 0.4 s every time; the rest grew with the bodies already there: the fingerprint of every body for the description cache (265 ms at six bodies), naming the edges of the whole final shape by listing the edges of every face (340 ms), the details of every face for naming (`surfacesOf`), the validity check of the whole compound, and, when a gear is pulled out beside the others, a boolean fuse of the new body with all of them (about 75 ms a step, growing).

**Built, each kept by the body's, face's or edge's identity (its identity number, and for a body a quick box; a face or an edge is only taken for the same when the kernel says it is the same shape, as identity numbers can be shared), as the body's exact box already was (`bodies.mjs`):**
1. **The fingerprint of a body** (volume, box, number of faces and edges) is found once for the body (`evaluate.mjs`, `lumpFacts`).
2. **The validity check of a compound** checks each solid on its own (the library checks the parts of a compound separately) and not again for one found valid before (`interpreter.mjs`, `isValidSolid`, `validSolids`); a single solid is checked as before.
3. **The edges of a face and the ends and middle of an edge** are found once (`facenames.mjs`, `edgesOfFace`, `itemOfEdge`); **what a face is** (its kind, direction, place, box) too (`surfacesOf`, `factsOf`). What is given out is a copy, so changing one answer does not change the next.
4. **A piece added whose box meets none of the bodies** (`joined`) is a body of its own: the compound of them, with no boolean on the others. A piece that does meet one is fused as before.
5. A new body is still checked, meshed, named and described in full: that is what it costs.
6. **A body's own solid is kept under what it was made from, not what came before it** (`in_729`; `interpreter.mjs`, `extrude`'s `toolOf`, `prisms`): a step's kept answer is keyed by the steps before it, so a change to an early gear made every body after it be built again (8 gears: 9 steps, 4.0 s). The solid a step makes of its own sketches (the prism, with the roles of its faces by the sketches' own edges, which are replayed when it is found again) is now kept under a key of its sketches' answers, its depth and the named parameters, so the later bodies are the very same solids as before, and everything kept for a solid (validity, no self-crossing, mesh, the details of its faces: all now by the solid, also for a single solid: `isValidSolid`, `crossesItself`) is found again. Only the body that changed is built.

**What is not changed:** the results. The kept answers are the ones working it out again gives (`test/perbody.test.ts`: the names of the edges and faces are the same cold and kept; two gears apart have the volume of one twice; touching gears are one body with the volumes added; a compound of valid solids is valid).

## Measured

Six gears, one after another: **13.8 s to 9.2 s** in all. The last gear's step: fingerprints 265 to 56 ms, the kernel's naming of the final shape 340 to under 100 ms, the fuse gone. The cost of each added gear (ten gears): 1.5 s for the second to 2.7 s for the tenth, a rise of about 0.13 s a gear where it was 0.55 s a gear (0.9 to 3.6 s over six). What still rises is mostly outside the kernel (the page's scene is derived from every part again at each edit, `withDerived`, which grows with the 700 or so parts a gear sketch has) and the work a new body is owed.

7. **Naming the faces and edges is per body** (`in_760`; `facenames.mjs`, `infoOf`, `surfacesOf`, `nameEdges`): the faces of each solid, what each face is, and the names of the solid's edges for the names its faces were given, are kept by the solid (its identity, and only for the same solid), so a step that adds or changes another body no longer looks through the faces and edges of every body. An edge lies between two faces of one body, so each body's edges are named from its own faces and the lists are put together in the order of the bodies; the answer is the one the face by face way gave (`test/perbody.test.ts`, `test/prismkept.test.ts`: the names of faces and edges are the same kept and worked out from nothing). Editing the first of 16 gears: 2.8 s to 1.9 s; the last: 1.83 s to 1.59 s.

8. **A revolve, a loft and a sweep keep their own solid as an extrude does** (`in_763`; `interpreter.mjs`, `ownSolid`): the solid each makes of its own sketches (before it is combined with what is there) is kept under a key made of those sketches' answers, its own settings and the named parameters, with the roles of its faces and, for a sweep, its note about a crease, replayed when it is found again (a twist, a spiral, a loft through its sections with their holes, a swept profile with its holes). Measured (Node, an edit to the first of six helical gears of 30 teeth): **10.0 s to 3.7 s**; the first of three worm wheels of 20 teeth: **20.4 s to 7.8 s**. **Not changed, found:** the first of six worms: 9.7 s both ways, because a worm's threads are added to its core (`add`) and an add is a boolean on the whole existing shape, the other worms included (the next item: a joined or pushed-in step works on the one body it touches). `test/prismkept.test.ts` (4 more tests: a helical gear's revolve, a worm's sweeps and a worm wheel's loft are built once and found again; the answers kept and worked out from nothing have the same volumes, face names and edge names).

9. **A step that adds to or cuts into the body works on the bodies it meets** (`in_764`; `interpreter.mjs`, `touching`, `fuseInto`, `cutFrom`, `cutOrAdd`, `joined`, `keptBoolean`): an extrude, a revolve, a loft or a sweep that adds, cuts, or is pulled out (`cutOrAdd`) did its boolean on the whole shape, every body in it. Now the bodies whose box meets the piece's (with a margin of 0.1 micron) are the part the boolean works on; the others are put back as they are, the very same solids (so what is kept for them is found again). A piece that meets none is a body of its own, with no boolean (as before, now for every kind of step); one that meets all is worked on the whole shape. What each boolean gave is kept under the identity of the bodies and the piece it was made of (64 kept), so a step after an early change finds them as they were. Whether a figure pulled backwards tries a cut at all (`runsInto`) is still decided by a real overlap of boxes, as before (bodies that only share a face are joined, not cut). Measured (Node, an edit to the first of six worms of module 2.06): **9.9 s to 3.8 s** (the threads of the other five were each a fuse with the whole shape of every worm). The answers are the old ones: two worms whose threads run into each other give the same three bodies with the same volumes as the boolean on the whole shape (`test/partialboolean.test.ts`, 3 tests, with the volumes found before the change; and the suite).

**Measured (`in_729`, Node, 30-tooth gears):** an edit to the first gear: 8 gears **4.0 s to 1.5 s**, 16 gears **10.5 s to 3.3 s** (the fingerprints 826 to 89 ms, the checks and the builds of the later bodies gone). Editing the last of 16 is 2.0 s, of which about 0.65 s is the changed body's own work (describe 0.3, self-crossing 0.2, validity 0.1, prism 0.07) and the rest grows with the model, in proportion: naming the faces and edges (0.3), the fingerprints (0.07), the page's parts (0.25). `test/prismkept.test.ts` (3 tests: an early edit builds one body and finds three kept; the kept answer's volumes, face names and edge names are the worked-out answer's; a valid solid is found so again).

10. **What is kept is bounded by weight, not by a count (`in_857`).** Measured on ordinary use (not gears): a model of plain parts added one after another cost 0.1 s a part up to 40 parts, then **1.2 s at 60 and 7.8 s at 100**: the kept answers had counts (40 bodies' descriptions, 64 kept solids and booleans, 96 step answers: a sketch and a push each, so 48 parts) and a model one part past them made every part again at every step. Now: the descriptions are kept by the triangles they hold (`evaluate.mjs` `DESCRIBED_TRIANGLES`, a million), the kept solids and booleans by faces (`interpreter.mjs` `keepWithin`, 20 thousand), the step answers by the faces each adds to its target's (`MEMO_FACES`, 150 thousand), all with a high count as well. **Result:** a part added to a model of 70 parts 2.5 s to 0.16 s; 100 parts 7.8 s to 0.3 s. Test: `perbody.test.ts` (the 61st part takes the steps before it from what was kept). **Left, measured:** a change to the first part of a hundred still takes 0.9 s (each later step's key includes the steps before it, and joining the bodies enumerates them: quadratic); and over 200 parts the process ran out of memory in Node (about 36 scene parts a part, the history's derived scenes are suspected: not looked at).

11. **Heavy bodies side by side (`in_857`, found in the real app).** Six 200 tooth worm wheels added one after another took 12, 38, 33, 43, 55 and 61 s (the sixth refused at the 60 s limit); each should cost about the first's 12 s. Two causes, both in what is worked out for all the bodies at each step: (1) **the exact box of a body of more than 300 faces** (`bodies.mjs` `box`, used to keep bodies' names when there are two or more): about 3 s for a 100 tooth wheel, once for each body that was not found again by its identity; a body of more than 300 faces is held by the quick box (a little roomier), which is all a marker needs; (2) **naming the faces** (`facenames.mjs` `nameFaces`) compared every old face name with every face of the body building arrays for each pair: seconds at five thousand faces; the comparison now stops at the first corner that is too far. **Result:** in the real app, six 200 tooth wheels 12, 13.5, 13.9, 15.2, 17.4, 20.5 s (none refused); in Node, four 100 tooth wheels 6.8, 20.4, 17.0, 18.5 s to 6.8, 7.6, 8.1, 9.0 s. Test: `wheelspeed.test.ts` (wheels side by side stay separate bodies with names of their own).

12. **The cost of editing an early part of a model of many (`in_876`): measured, not built.** Parts added one after another (a sketch and a push each, boxes): changing the first part costs 84 ms at 25 parts, 190 ms at 50 and 740 ms at 100 (quadratic: each later step's key includes the steps before it, so each is made again, and each one's work on the body so far is by the number of bodies); changing the last part 30, 46 and 95 ms. Where the 7 ms a step goes at 100 parts: the bodies' markers (`trackBodies`) 2 ms, naming all the faces again (`nameFaces`) about 2 ms, the validity and self-crossing checks 1 ms, the push itself 1 ms, listing the solids and faces of the whole body (the explorer, about 15 microseconds a solid, several times a step) the rest. **Tried and dropped:** taking the solids with the explorer's own hashing in place of the library's `solids` (no change: its own quadratic part is five thousand questions at 100 bodies), a cheaper count of faces for the answers kept (0.03 s of 0.7). **A model built by a pattern** (the 12 by 12 grid of 144 posts) is not this: one push and one pattern, an edit of the push 1.1 s, which is the 144 bodies' meshes (0.65 s) and the pattern (0.45 s), all of them changed. **What would cut it** (not built, a change to what markers and names are): a step that only adds a body beside the others (a push or revolve in "new" mode) would add one marker and name only the new body's faces instead of looking at all of them again: about half of the 7 ms. **Recommendation (the AI's, not a decision):** not worth it below a hundred separate steps (0.19 s at 50); keep as it is.

## Rationale

The costs that grew with the model were each the same shape: a pure function of one body (or face or edge) worked out again for all of them whenever one was added. Cutting them where they are made, by the identity the kernel already uses to tell bodies apart, loses nothing so long as an identity is never taken for another (hence the same-shape check on a hit) and what is given out is not shared.
