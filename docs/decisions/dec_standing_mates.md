# Standing mates: a mate puts itself back with the change that broke it; a coaxial mate is a pivot and a slider

`in_321` asked to start on option 3 of `q_mate_persistence`. **This is the AI's first slice and the human has not reviewed it.** Choices to veto:

1. **Standing by steps, not by a pose solver.** A mate keeps its record (`params.stand = 1`). After a change that is not stale, the mates that stand and are no longer true are put back by the same corrective turns and move as a hand-made mate (`putMatesBack`), appended **in the same history entry as the change** (one batch), so one undo takes back the change and its corrections. Replays are exact; sketches are carried as before. A pose solver (several mates at once, loops) is **not** built: mates are put back one at a time, up to 8, and if one cannot be put back nothing is corrected and the old "no longer true" message and mark apply.
2. **New mates stand by default; `mate:stand:<id>` turns it off** (back to placed once). Mates made before this keep their old meaning (no `stand`: placed once).
3. **A coaxial mate is already a joint that leaves freedom:** the first body can turn about the shared axis and slide along it. Selecting that body offers **Turn about the mate's axis** (degrees) and **Slide along the mate's axis** (mm); each appends ordinary steps and the mate stays true. Flush + coaxial gives a pin with a shoulder (turn only); a slider is a coaxial or parallel-axes mate plus the slide row.
4. **Limits (`in_322`, AI's reading, for the human to correct):** a joint remembers where it stands (`turn` degrees, `slide` mm, counted from where the mate was made) on the mate record, and can have a lowest and a highest stop for each. Rows: *lowest / highest stop* (a number; refused if the two would cross) and *take the stops away*. A drive amount is **added** to where the joint stands and **stops at the limit** (asked for more, it goes as far as the stop; at the stop it does nothing). The drive values and stops survive a mate being put back. A hand-made turn or move of the body is not counted (it is a placement, not a drive).
5. **Drag handle (`in_323`, AI's reading):** selecting the pinned body adds two handles to its helper: a ring round the shared axis (turn) and an arrow along it past the body's end (slide). Where the arrow lies on a Move arrow's line the joint's handle is the one taken. The drag is drawn live on the graphics card (the body moved by a matrix, as a free body drag is), is held at the stops, and is made as one drive step when let go (the same step as a typed amount). Sketches on the body do not move in the picture until it is let go.
6. **Not built yet:** standing correction while the kernel is still answering (browser, stale result: it is corrected on the next change), planar joints, several mates on one body solved together.

## Rationale

Keeping standing mates as appended steps reuses everything already tested (carrying sketches, anchors, undo, save/open) and needs no new solver; the cost is that a body with competing mates is corrected greedily rather than solved. Grouping with the change in one entry keeps undo honest. A real pose solver should be added only if competing mates turn out to matter.

7. **Browser pass (`in_328`).** Looked at in Chromium: the mate mark, the joint's ring and arrow, a slide drag (shown moving, made as one step, position kept on the mate), a turn drag of the ring, the axis-perpendicular-to-a-face mate, an angle of 120, the number column on a tall and a short page. **Found and fixed:** (a) a mate just made read as "no longer true" while the kernel's answer was awaited (it was checked against the older faces): the check now waits for the answer; (b) a standing mate that a change broke while the answer was awaited was never put back or reported: when the answer arrives it is put back in the same step as the change (`refresh`), or, if it cannot be (two mates at odds: an angle of 120 turns the body away from its coaxial mate and the face is lost), it is said. **Seen, not changed:** the joint's ring and arrow are drawn like the Move arrows (charcoal, faint) and are easy to miss; the mate-number bubbles sit on the page's left outside the view when their faces are out of view.
8. **Competing mates solved together (`in_329`, AI's reading, for the human to correct).** When a change leaves standing mates not true, they are solved as one least-squares problem over the bodies' poses (`operation/matesolve.ts`, damped Gauss-Newton with a numeric Jacobian; each mode's residuals are zero when it holds). Free bodies: the first body of any standing mate; of bodies that only move each other, the one made first stays. The step taken is the smallest change from where bodies are, so a mate that leaves freedom (a pivot, a slider) leaves the body where it is in that freedom. The result is made as turns and moves about each body's middle, with sketches carried, in the same step as the change (`solveStanding` in `interaction/mate.ts`); the old one-at-a-time put-back is kept as a fallback when the check afterwards still finds one off. **At odds:** if no pose makes every standing mate true, nothing is moved, and the message names the mates that cannot hold (`standing mates cannot all hold at once`). The change the user made stays. **Limits:** only mates whose faces are still found take part (a mate whose face is gone is 'lost' and is only reported); the solve is local (it starts from where the bodies are: a pose far off may fail or find another); the first body of a mate is the one that moves, whichever is 'more fixed' is not asked; no priority between mates (a conflict leaves all of them as they are rather than the newest winning); the problem is small (a few bodies), with no sparse or large-scale handling.

9. **A conflict must be resolved, not ranked (`in_330`, approved in `in_331`).** A change that leaves standing mates that held with no placement where they all hold is **refused**: nothing is made (the history is unchanged), the mates that cannot all hold are named in the message and flash, and the user must take one away or change it first. There is no priority between mates and the newest does not win. This replaces item 8's "the change stays". A conflict that was already in the file does not block unrelated changes (only mates that held before the change, or the one just made, count). When the kernel's answer comes late (browser) the change is shown first and taken back when the answer shows the conflict.
10. **Browser pass over the refusal (`in_332`).** In Chromium, a second flush mate that cannot hold with the first was refused: the message showed at the bottom, the bodies and the one mate were as they were, the mates flashed briefly, no page errors. **Found and fixed:** the refusal that comes when the kernel's answer is late took the change back with undo and left it in the redo tail, so redo could make the conflict; the tail is now dropped (the earlier "a step the kernel could not make is taken back" path was not changed and may do the same). The message now reads "…could not all hold at once (flush, flush). That cannot be done: take one of them away or change it first".
11. **Browser pass over the widened-body refusal (`in_333`).** In Chromium, with a body B between A and C, flush-mated to both, widening A was refused two ways: dragging the push / pull arrow of A's side face (the picture showed the pull; on release it was refused, the bodies and both mates stayed, the message named the mates, no redo tail) and moving A's corners one at a time (the first corner already refused). No page errors. **Seen, not changed:** the mark of a mate stands on the middle of the face's push / pull arrow, so a press on the middle of that arrow hits the mark, not the arrow (the ends of the arrow work); a refused push / pull says the mates could not all hold but not that the face being pulled is what changed; the single-corner move is refused even though it only tilts the face.
12. **The mate's mark keeps off the helper's handles (`in_334`).** A mark that would stand on an arrow or ring of the helper stands beside it instead (`clearOfLines` in `bubbleplace.ts`: the nearest clear place in a ring round it, above first; where it was if none is clear), so a press in the middle of a face's push / pull arrow reaches the arrow. Checked in Chromium on the file of `in_333`: the mark stood 25 px above the arrow's middle, and a press on the middle took the arrow. The mark still belongs to its face by being near it; it is not joined to it by a line.
13. **The refusal says which change it is (`in_335`).** The message now reads, for example, "That push / pull of a face of Body 1 would leave these standing mates unable to hold at once: flush between Body 2 and Body 1; flush between Body 2 and Body 3. Take one of them away or change it first, or change something else". The change is named by `changeWords` (`interaction/changewords.ts`: a push / pull, a move or turn of a body, a mate, a move of a point of a drawing, a fillet, a delete, …) with the body the face or body belongs to, and the mates by their kind and the two bodies they join, numbered as the Bodies list numbers them. Checked in Chromium on the file of `in_333` for the pull and for a corner move. The face itself is named by its body only (a body has no names for its faces).
14. **Browser pass with touch (`in_336`).** Chromium with touch emulation (390 x 780, CDP touch events): a tap on the push / pull arrow of a mated face chooses it, its drag icon drags it, and on release the change is refused with the message (bodies and mates as before); a tap on the joint's slide arrow chooses it and its drag icon slid the pin by 5.6 mm (one step, the position kept on the mate). **Found and fixed:** (a) the mate's mark stood where the drag icon of the finger stands, so the press went to the mark (which swallows it) and the drag became a scroll that the browser cancelled: marks now keep off the drag icons (`clearOfLines` takes discs); (b) a tap near a mark was taken as a tap on the mark (the browser's touch adjustment reaches about 30 px): for a finger a mark keeps 30 px (not 13) clear of the helper's arrows and rings; (c) a long message was under the undo bar and the X Y Z buttons: for a finger it stands above the bar and clear of the buttons. **Not looked at:** the turn ring by touch; the number bubbles of a mate pair by touch.
15. **The turn ring by touch (`in_337`).** In Chromium with touch emulation (390 x 780): a tap on the ring chose it (`joint:turn`) and gave it a drag icon, the icon dragged along the ring turned the pin (the ring lit, the amount followed the finger, -24 degrees for the drag made), and on release it was one drive step with the turn kept on the mate; the mate's mark stood clear of the ring and the icon; no page errors. A pin turned about its own axis does not look different, so the picture shows only the ring and the amount. **Seen, not changed:** the Move arrows' X number bubble ("X 0 ±") stays up on the left while the joint is dragged and has nothing to do with it; no number says how far the joint has turned while the ring is dragged.
