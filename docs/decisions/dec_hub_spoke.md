# Hub and spoke tool: the Wheel model (the human's recipe)

**Asked (`in_1214`):** a tool that makes hubs and spokes for wheels, pulleys or any round object; alone, or on a selected round object so it is made into that object. **Chosen (`in_1216`):** a selected round object is **filled**. The first four spoke styles (tapered, flat taper, straight bars, web with round holes, built after `in_1222`) were **replaced** when the human wrote the recipe below (`in_1236`: "lets just do it ourselves"), after the casting research (sources README) showed no ready drawings.

## The recipe (human-authored, `in_1236`): the only attributes
1. **The web.** The plainest wheel is a web with no spokes: a **web (spoke) thickness**, the **rim** size (outside diameter, width, radial thickness) and the **hub** size (diameter, length, bore). The web meets the rim and the hub in **fillets or chamfers of a set size** (four of them: both faces, rim and hub).
2. **The spokes are cutouts in the web**: **count** (0 is the plain web), the **rim and hub offsets** (how far a cutout stops short of the rim and of the hub: 0 and the spokes run all the way into them; more and they join a web that joins the rim or hub), a **fillet of x size in every corner of a cutout** (no corner is ever left square: not machinable or castable), the **spoke width** and **taper**, and a **spiral**.
3. **Edge of the cutouts (`in_1243`):** a **fillet or chamfer** (kind and size) on the edges where the cutouts meet the web's two faces.
3. **Draft (`in_1238`):** the walls have a **draft** angle as one more number: 0 for a machined wheel (no draft), some for a cast one.
4. **Later (said by the human, not built):** true rod spokes, assembled hubs not made from one piece.

## What is built (mine, from that recipe)
`operation/modelwheel.ts` and the Models item "Wheel" (fields in `modelkinds.ts`): the section (rim, web, hub) is turned about the axis as one profile with the four joins (a fillet, or a chamfer, `join` mm); the cutouts (one outline each, repeated round) are cut through the web. A cutout is bounded by two spoke edges (straight; with a taper they lean; with a spiral they curve), an outer and an inner circular arc, and four corner fillets of `corner` mm (tangent circular arcs, worked out from each corner's angle). Offsets are measured from the hub's and the rim's walls: **0 runs into the wall** (the cutout reaches 0.05 mm into it; a cut that ends exactly on a face is not a solid the kernel makes); a cutout that ends within 0.4 mm of where a join ends is refused (a sliver); less than the join size and the cutout cuts into the fillet. **Refusals:** a fillet or chamfer or corner fillet under 0.8 mm (0.030 in, the Batesville guide's least), rim or web under 1.2 mm, hub wall under 2 mm, the rim and the hub must stand proud of the web by the join size, spokes narrower than 1.2 mm, spokes too wide for their number, corner fillets too large for the cutout (a strong spiral makes one corner sharp and is refused). **Fill:** with a drawn circle, a round face or a circular edge selected the tool starts centred on it, along its axis, as wide as it (`in_1216`). Typing a number that is 0 (an offset of none) sets no scale for the rest.
**Draft (built):** `draft` degrees (0 to 10, start 2, the sand-casting standard of 2° with 1° least in the Engineers Edge guide): the walls of the rim and the hub lean away from the middle plane, as a pattern split there needs: the rim's outside is smaller at the faces, its inside bigger, the hub's outside smaller; the nominal diameters are those at the middle plane (the walls beside the web: at the web's faces); the fillets or chamfers follow the leaning walls; the bore, the web's faces and the cutout walls stay straight. Refused: a rim or hub thinner than 1.2 mm or 2 mm at its faces once leaned.
**Never fail, manufacturing agnostic (`in_1249`):** the wheel refuses only what is no solid at all (a size that is 0 or less, a hub bigger than the rim or smaller than the bore, a web thicker than the rim or hub, the spoke count 1 or 2, a draft over 15 degrees, a rim past a gear's roots) and **never a size that is hard to make**: every radius (rim join, hub join, spoke join, spoke, obtuse) may be **0** (square) and the old 0.8 mm and 1.2 mm minimums are gone. Where a number is too big for the room the wheel is made with the biggest the room allows (`effective()`, and in `cutout()` a **share of all the corner radii found by halving**, so the spoke join radius moves up to what fits between the tangents of the spoke lines): the joins no bigger than the rim and hub stand proud of the web and together than the web is wide; a cutout within 0.4 mm of a join's end is moved off it; the edge round (the **Spoke radius**) no bigger than 0.45 of the spoke thickness or 0.4 of the spoke's width, and none where a cutout runs into the hub or the rim, ends in a point or is narrow (`edgeMade`: the kernel cannot round those; the box's note says so). **Spokes merge:** where spokes run into each other before the hub or the rim the cutout ends there in a **point** (rounded as the other corners), and where they fill the web there is no cutout, which is no failure (a cutout is open where it is more than 0.4 mm wide; the longest open run is the cutout; the outline is checked for crossing itself, and none is made if it does). Pieces of the outline under 0.15 mm make the radii smaller. **Names swapped as meant:** **Spoke radius** is the round on the spoke's edges (the old Spoke join radius); **Spoke join radius** is where a cutout's sides meet the hub and the rim (the old Spoke radius). **Spoke obtuse radius:** a new number, shown only while there is a spiral, for the blunter corners of a cutout (a spiral makes two sharp and two blunt); while it is not typed it follows the spoke join radius and grows with the spiral (join radius x (1 + spiral / 20)); both kinds use the one fillet or chamfer toggle of the spoke join radius. A cutout's two spoke edges are one circular arc each (or a line); the polygon's closing piece no longer loses the first side's bulge when a radius is 0 (a bug found on the way). Tests: `test/wheel.test.ts` (nothing refuses but the non-solids; merging; the obtuse radius).
**Cut spokes (`in_1247`):** a variation of the wheel, Models → **Cut spokes**: with a round body that is there already selected (a gear, a disc, a ring; a face or an edge of it will do) the wheel is **cut into it** instead of made new. `operation/roundbody.ts` reads the body: its axis and middle, the thickness, the **outside** (the biggest circle about the axis; for a gear the tip radius from its gear record, since a gear body has no tooth edges), the **bore** (a whole circle inside the outside: none when there is none) and, for a gear, the **roots**. The outside, the bore and the thickness are kept and not the person's to type (the box has no Size, Bore, thickness or Draft; the box's numbers are the rim diameter, hub diameter, spoke thickness and the spoke numbers, with the same repair and take-back as the wheel). The rim diameter is held inside the roots of a gear's teeth (`rimMax`, 1.2 mm of wall). Parts, made as the kernel answers: **Pockets** (the annulus between the hub and the rim cut down from both faces to the spoke thickness), **Joins** (the hub join and the rim join radius or chamfer on the pocket floors, picked by where the edges are), **Cutouts** (through the web) and **Edge rounds**. It cuts the selected body: the bodies it touches (it says to select a round body when nothing round is selected). No draft (a body is cut as it is). Tests: `test/wheelcut.test.ts` (a disc with a bore is read; a cut disc keeps its outside and bore and loses the metal the pockets and cutouts hold, within 8 %; a gear is read from its teeth; a cut gear keeps its teeth).
**Names, order and kinds (`in_1245`):** the box lists, in this order, the **diameters** (Outer diameter (the Size), **Rim diameter** (the rim's inside diameter: the rim is (outer - rim diameter) / 2 thick across, replacing the old rim thickness), Hub diameter, Bore diameter), the **thicknesses**, all along the axis (Rim thickness (was the rim width), Spoke thickness (the web), Hub thickness (was the hub length)), and the **spoke numbers** (Spoke count, Spoke width, Spoke taper, Spoke spiral, Spoke rim offset, Spoke hub offset, **Spoke radius** (the corner of a cutout), **Spoke join radius** (the round on the cutouts' edges), **Rim join radius**, **Hub join radius** (the web's two joins, now separate)), then Draft. **Every radius has a little toggle** beside it (F | C) to make it a chamfer of that size (`ModelField.toggle` holds the key of a hidden fillet-or-chamfer number; the box's toggle types it); the cutout's corner chamfer is a straight cut with legs of that size. **Turn is gone from every model's box** (a model is placed along the surface; turn it afterwards).
**Edge of the cutouts (built, `in_1243`):** fields Spoke edge (fillet or chamfer) and Spoke edge size (0 for none, at least 0.3 mm). It is a **second part, "Edge rounds"**, made once the kernel has the wheel: the edges of every cutout lying in the web's two faces (and where a cutout reaches into a join) are picked by where they are (their names are coordinates) and rounded with the kernel's fillet or chamfer step (`Builder.roundEdges`). Refused (the numbers not typed follow): an edge over 0.45 of the web, over what leaves a spoke 0.4 mm of flat top, and **offsets under the edge's size + 0.4 mm** (spokes that run into the hub or the rim, offset 0, take no edge round: the kernel cannot fillet there). A spiral's edge is now a **circular arc** through three points of the curve (a few smooth faces, not a dozen straight ones), and each corner fillet of a cutout is the **exact** tangent circle between its two sides (line or circle to circle), so the edge round can follow the outline.
**Typed numbers (`in_1240`):** the Size and the Outside diameter were the same number twice, and typing any length made the others follow *that* number's ratio, so a typed value could break the rest. Now: the **Size is the outside diameter** (one box field; the other is gone). The numbers not typed **follow the ones that are** (`repairWheel`): the hub is 2.75 bores across and 2.25 long while it is not typed (a small bore gives a small hub, on a small or a big wheel), a bigger hub gives a bigger bore, the join is 3/4 of the web; and they are moved as far as they must for the wheel to be possible (the rim and hub stand proud of the web, room for the joins and the cutouts, draft thin, spokes too wide for their number, a fillet too big for a cutout, a wheel too small for cutouts is a plain web). Numbers typed are never moved by that. When typed numbers cannot all be met, the number being typed is **taken back to the nearest value that works** (as the gear box's, `gearFix`: halving between the value without it and the one typed), the field is marked red and the reason said, on Enter (the next Enter makes the wheel) and while typing (the picture goes red). Several numbers that spoil it together are given up one at a time. The least wheel is about 13 mm across. **One way for every model (`in_1243`):** the same handling is now in every model's box, in one code: `operation/modelfit.ts` (`fitNumbers`: the numbers not typed are moved, as little as they can be, until the model's own `problem` says nothing: one number at a time, or all the sizes together, whole numbers a step at a time; and the same search when the first move only changes what is wrong), `Def.repair` for a model's own following (the wheel's hub follows its bore), `modelFix` and `modelGuard` (the typed number taken back and marked red, on Enter and while typing). Bearings, gearbox, engine, differential and cycloidal now use it with no code of their own; a field's `whole` and `zero` flags say which numbers count things. Test: `test/modelfit.test.ts` (all models: any size is a model; a number typed as it is changes nothing; random numbers typed end as a model, met by the others or taken back and marked).
**Limits (honest):** draft is on the rim and hub walls only (the cutout walls are straight: the web is only a few mm thick, and the kernel's cut has no taper); a pattern split off the middle plane would be different; the corner fillets in the plan are exact circles only where the two sides are straight (a spiral edge is a polyline of 14 pieces, so its fillet is a close arc, not tangent to a curve); the spoke section is a rectangle; Fill makes a **new body** and does not cut away the disc it was selected on; no keyway or set screws, no pulley grooves (`q_timing_pulleys`); sizes are the human's, nothing standard.

## Rationale
The human first approved a proposal (`in_1222`) and then replaced it with their own complete list of attributes (`in_1236`), which is the authority for what the tool has; the choices under "What is built" are mine, listed so they can be changed.
