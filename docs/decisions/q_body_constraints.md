# Constraints and suggestions between bodies, as there are between sketch parts

**Status after `in_308`:** steps A and B for circular edges are built and accepted as `dec_anchor_geometry` (anchors in the table, the solver, the hints and the suggestions). Stable naming of a circular edge (by how it looks, with a clear-winner rule) is built too (`in_309`). Straight edges and corners as anchors, with the same naming, are built (`in_310`). Edges that are not in the sketch's plane, by their projection, are built (`in_311`). Hidden edges are told from visible ones (`in_312`). Faces (flat: their trace in the plane; round: their circle) are built too (`in_313`). What is left here is open: body-to-body placement.

## What the human asked next (`in_305`, model kept as `app/test/fixtures/circle-on-body-face-in_305.cad.json`) and my answer, still a proposal

**Two different things were being called "body constraints".**
- **A sketch part tied to the geometry of a body** (the human's example: a circle drawn on the top face, concentric with the body's curved edge). The unknowns are still the drawn circle's own numbers; the body's edge is **read-only**, a reference, as "project / include geometry" is in other modelers. This is the part to do first and it needs no new solver, only that the solver and the suggestions can *see* the body's edges, corners and arcs as fixed geometry. Today they cannot: a body's curved edge is a `line` with a `mid` and no centre part, relations and `nearItems` / `pointHint` / `lineHint` / `circleRadiusHint` only look at drawn parts (checked in the human's file: the circle on the top face is offered only Diameter and Delete, and no suggestions).
- **A body tied to another body** (flush faces, a distance, centres lined up). Here the unknowns are a body's **placement** (3 moves, 3 turns; a body never deforms) and the thing that moves is a step, so it needs a 3D placement solver and a decision about how the answer is stored (a "place" step that is recomputed). That is the second, larger tier.

**The difference from a sketch constraint, in a line:** a sketch constraint is between 2D parts the solver may move (inputs of the history); a constraint to a body is to a part the kernel produced (an output), so it can only be *held to*, never moved, unless it is a placement of the whole body.

**One system for all** (the proposal): every part has a *role* for a solve, whatever it belongs to: **free** (the solver may move it: the drawn parts of the sketch being solved), **anchor** (fixed, read: body corners, edges and arcs on or projected onto the sketch's plane, other sketches' parts, the world axes), and later **placed** (a body's position). A relation names parts by `Ref` and does not care which kind owns them; the solver takes anchors as fixed points and circles (it already has `fixed`), and the suggestion code runs one proximity rule (`in_294`) over *all* geometry on the plane, with anchors lit amber like anything else. The edge named in a relation must be **stable**: a body edge named by position breaks when the body changes, so an edge that comes from a sketch (the arc of a circle that was extruded) is named by that source (`edgesource.ts` already knows it; the human's circle would be concentric with the source circle's arc) and only an edge with no source (a chamfer's) is named by where it is, and is repaired or reported broken. Rule that avoids cycles: a relation may only name things **earlier in the history** than the sketch it is in.

## One system, and assemblies (`in_306`), still a proposal

**Yes, one system, layered; what is shared and what is not.** Shared: (L0) **primitives and frames**: a point, an axis, a circle, a plane, and a *frame* (origin, axis, direction) taken from any owner: a drawn circle, a body's circular edge, a hole's wall, a pin, a face; (L1) the **relation catalogue** written as residual equations over primitives (coincident, on, tangent, concentric / coaxial, parallel, perpendicular, distance, angle, equal, fixed), not tied to 2D or to a kind of owner; (L3) **one solver core** with a count of what is left free (degrees of freedom) and what is over-constrained; (L4) **one suggestion engine** (proximity of primitives gives a candidate relation). Different on purpose: (L2) the **variables**: a sketch's unknowns are 2D coordinates in its plane; a body's unknowns are its **pose** (3 moves, 3 turns; a point of the body is `R p + t`), and a number such as a dimension or a hinge angle is a parameter. A sketch constraint is then the special case where the poses are fixed. That is how the mates of the assembly modelers work (a mate is a few relations between two frames), so the same relation "concentric" is a sketch's concentric and an assembly's coaxial, in 2D and in 3D.

**Assemblies on it (L5, built later, on top, not beside):** a **mate** is a named bundle of L1 relations between two frames that leaves some freedom and may limit it: *fastened* (frames coincide: no freedom: surfaces glued), *revolute* (pin in a hole: axes coaxial and a face contact: one turn, optional angle limits), *cylindrical* (coaxial only: turn and slide), *slider* (axes parallel, no turn: one slide, limits), *planar* (a face on a face: slides in two directions and turns about the normal, cannot separate), *ball*, *pin-slot*. The turn or slide that is left is a **driven value** like any dimension (type it, drag it, animate it). **Collision** is not an equation: it is a **limit** (distance between two solids not below zero) and an **interference check**; both are queries to the kernel (`inv_kernel_boundary`: the constraint code gets them as data), first as a check shown when a part is let go, later as a limit while dragging. A relation therefore has a kind, `equal` or `limit`.

**The human leans to one model (`in_307`): an assembly is not a special thing, it is a model whose bodies are in constraint relationships; not yet a decision (the entry stays open until they say so). My view: sound, and it gives what assemblies are normally used for except the four things below. For free: editing a part in place (its own steps are in the same history), top-down design (a sketch tied to a body, a body tied to a sketch), one undo and one file. What an assembly user also expects and what it needs: **(a) many copies of one part** (four of the same screw): the copies must stay linked to the one that was made (`dec_repeat_parameters`: copies are made from the original's steps, so editing it changes all); **(b) parts from a library or another file** (a bolt, a bearing): an "insert" of a file as a body, a copy at first, a link later; **(c) sub-assemblies and names**: groups of bodies that move as one, a name that the user can set (bodies are named by their step now: `x_s_005`), a body marked **grounded** (the one that does not move); **(d) size**: the history is replayed, so a hundred bodies are slow unless each body's steps are cached separately, which they can be (a body's steps depend only on its own). Also needed later and not in the way: exploded views, interference, a bill of materials (queries over the bodies), and **variants** of one model (the parameter sets that the variable system already has). One thing to watch: the sketch solve and the pose solve must run in an order (sketches first, then poses, following the history), so a mate can never feed back into the sketch that made the body.

**What this needs decided before it is built (not decided here):** (1) are the parts of an assembly the bodies of the one model (a mate is a step between bodies; each body's pose is *derived* from the mates after its own steps, so history stays additive, `prin_history_additive`) or separate documents with instances; (2) the solver: the sketch solver is small on purpose (`dec_constraint_solver_small`); several bodies with mates need a general 3D solve (Newton or Levenberg-Marquardt over poses, with the freedom count), which is a new, tested module, not an extension of the sketch one; (3) when, relative to the rest of the work. What is cheap now and keeps the door open: steps A and B above written against primitives, frames and roles (no `origin === "drawn"` tests in the new code).

Order I would build it, if the human agrees: (A) anchors in the solver and the selection's Constraints list for edges that come from a sketch; (B) the same anchors in the suggestions and the drawing hints; (C) edges with no source; (D) body-to-body placement (below).

The human asked (`in_293`) for "the same constraints and suggestions on bodies that we do on sketches". Nothing is built. What is true today: relations (`relations` in the scene) are solved in 2D, one plane at a time, over drawn corners and circles; a body is moved by **steps** (move, turn, scale), not by solver variables, so a constraint between two bodies has nothing for the solver to move. My proposal, in the order I would build it:

1. **Suggestions while a body is dragged free or with an arrow** (no new constraint kind yet): when a face, edge, or centre of the dragged body comes within a few millimetres of lining up with one of another body (faces flush, edges in line, centres aligned, touching), the body snaps to it and says so (the hint icons and the part lit amber, as for a sketch point), and a release keeps it as an ordinary move. This is the part of "suggestions" that has no constraint behind it and needs no new machinery beyond the free drag.
2. **Constraints that stay true afterwards** (flush faces, a distance between two faces, centres aligned, touching): each would be a relation between two body parts, kept true by **a move step that is recomputed** when something it depends on changes (a solver over the bodies' positions, 3D, separate from the sketch solver). That is a new subsystem: it needs a decision (what is solved, in what order, what happens when two constraints disagree) before any code.
3. **Fixed in place** for a body (it refuses to be dragged or moved), which is small.

Questions for the human: is (1) enough for now, with (2) kept for later? Which relations first: flush faces, a distance between faces, centres aligned?

## Rationale

The sketch constraints work because the sketch solver owns the positions; bodies do not have that. Saying so, and splitting the request into what needs no new subsystem and what does, is what the human needs to decide; nothing depends on this entry.
