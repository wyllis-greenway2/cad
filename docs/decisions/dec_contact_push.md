# Contact push: a played body pushes the bodies it meets, which yield by their own joints

**Accepted at `in_1017`** (the human took the AI's recommendations for the open questions, below; the model itself was built and tried over `in_896` to `in_910` and the human asked for each step). It comes from the human's brainstorm (`in_889` to `in_893`): most of a mechanism is solved with joints and couplings, contact is for the key parts, one body is set to move, and the rest follows by constraints and collision.

**A correction first.** The AI had said the hard bodies were checked with the kernel's booleans and were too slow to use live. That is wrong: `dec_hard_bodies` is already built on the kernel's **mesh** (`operation/collide.ts`: a tree of boxes over the triangles, `collidesMoving`, `contactNormals`, `freePart`), the exact boolean is kept for the final answer only, a hard body already treats every body it meets as hard (`meets`, `in_490`), and a dragged body already stops and slides. What is not there is the next level that `dec_hard_bodies` names as not built: **pushing**.

## The model

1. **Layers.** Joints and couplings first (cheap, exact, nothing to tune); contact only for what is marked, as the last layer. With nothing marked, nothing changes from today.
2. **The push rule.** The played joint moves by a small step. If the moved body overlaps a body that is in the contact set, that body **yields**: it is moved along its own joint (a pin turns, a rod slides, within its stops) by the **smallest amount that clears the overlap** (the way it was going first, then the other way; found by doubling and halving). If it cannot clear (a stop, a wall, a body with no joint), the played joint stops where it was: a **jam**, said in words with the two bodies named. A joint may have a **rest value** it goes back toward when nothing holds it away (a spring, a pawl dropping into the next tooth).
3. **Who is in the contact set.** Three states for a body: **soft** (as now), **hard** (`dec_hard_bodies`), and **ghost** (never solid, for what is meant to pass through). A hard body that is pushed **passes its hardness on** to the soft bodies it touches, to a depth (open: 2 or 3 by default), so one marked body is enough for a simple mechanism and more are marked only where a chain is longer. Bodies joined by a mate are never counted as touching each other (a pin in its hole is in contact by design).
4. **A budget.** A cap on the depth and on the passes, and a time budget for a frame: when it is hit the motion stops with a message, never silently (a silent stop would make a wrong animation look right).
5. **Layered mechanisms.** Two layers of a mechanism rest face to face (the crank's plate under the wheel). The mesh test counts a face resting on a face as a hit (the nudge it uses to break ties goes one way), so only what stands in the same layer may be tested against each other: bodies are compared on the part of them that overlaps in height by more than a tolerance.
6. **Not physics:** no mass, momentum, friction or forces; things stop and start at once; a free body falling or sliding down a ramp is not covered.

## What the prototype showed (`prototypes/contact-push`)

The Geneva drive's wheel, with **no formula**, moved by the rule alone (the pin and the locking disc tested against the wheel with the existing mesh test, 1 degree of crank a step, the wheel yielding by the smallest clearing angle): for 4, 5, 6 and 8 slots, three turns each, no jam; the wheel makes a slot's step for each turn and is within about 3, 1.2, 0.9 and 0.4 degrees of the formula's value (the difference is the clearance each side of the pin: the pin presses one wall and the wheel follows it). Cost in Node on a shared sandbox CPU: about 2 to 5 ms a step on average with about 11 collision tests a step; the slowest steps were 13 to 80 ms (probably the first test of a body and garbage collection, not examined). One thing that did not work first: the crank's plate resting under the wheel was reported as a hit all the time (point 5).

## Built (`in_904`, the first slice; the entry stays provisional until the human approves it)

`interaction/contact.ts` (`startContact`, `solveContact`), called by `startMotion` when any body is hard: **mark one body of a mechanism hard (Shape > Hard body) and play it.** What yields: the wheel of a Geneva drive (its formula is then not used) and the first body of every coaxial mate that nothing else here moves (**turning only** for now: a rod that should slide is not pushed). Who is tested: the bodies that move (the driver, what its couplings carry) and those that yield are all tested against each other when any of them is hard; every other body is a wall, tested when either of the pair is hard (`meets`); a pair a mate joins is left out; a pair already overlapping where the motion begins is left out. The pair is tested only on the part of each body that overlaps the other in height along the joint's axis (the stacked layers of a mechanism). Worked in steps of a degree (half a millimetre), so a scrub of many degrees is still followed. A body that yields is moved by the smallest angle that clears it (the way it last went first); a moving body that meets a wall, or a yielding body with nowhere to go, stops the motion at the last clear amount, and the bar says `stopped: A against B`. Keep pose is as for the motion (a Geneva still cannot keep a pose). Tests `contact.test.ts` (4): the Geneva follows its formula within 3 degrees over two turns with no jam, a scrub of 300 degrees, a hard post in the pin's way stops it with the message, and a Geneva with nothing hard is played by its formula as before. Measured in Node: 1 ms a degree median, 5.6 ms at the 95th percentile, 18 ms at the slowest (720 degrees of the Geneva). Chromium: the wheel stepped to -71.4 degrees (a slot is 72) and on, no page errors. **Not built:** passing hardness on to a depth (the moving set is tested as a whole when any member is hard, which is a simpler rule), the time budget, rest values (springs), sliding bodies that yield, the ghost state; the three open questions stand.

## Built, second slice (`in_906`: the cases tried in the suggested order)

**A gear pair** (20 and 30 teeth, module 2, backlash 0.15): with the driver hard, the driven gear yields to the teeth alone and follows the ratio of the teeth within 0.3 degrees for a whole turn (0.9 degrees with 20 and 20 teeth at backlash 0.3; 12 and 36, and 16 and 24 teeth, also under 0.5), no jam, 5 to 7 ms a degree in Node (gears of 1,300 and 1,900 triangles). **A cam and its follower rod:** with the cam hard the rod is a *sliding* body that yields to the cam's edge, and has a **rest value** (it goes back toward the cam as far as it is clear to, at most a millimetre a step, so it cannot jump through anything): it travels the cam's lift (10 mm for an offset of 5) and is back where it began after a turn, with no table. Built for it: sliding yielders (`Pushable.kind`), rest values, and what the cases showed was missing:
- **The face meshes of a body leave out the walls of a gear's teeth** (`meshOf` reads the face planes of the scene, and a gear body has two: its top and bottom). A pair of gears therefore never met in the existing hard-body test either (a gear body in `hardCollision` is tested on its caps only). The contact push reads the kernel's own mesh by the faces' ranges (`bodyMesh`, from the session's outcome; the face planes are the fallback). **The hard-body drag still uses the face planes**; reading the kernel's mesh there too is the same change and is open.
- **Touching counts as a hit** in the mesh test (a tangent line or a face on a face, by the direction of its nudge), so a pair touching where the motion begins is a *touch*, not an *overlap*: only a pair that still hits when either body is moved a hair either way is left out as already overlapping.
- **The height of a layer is along a turning body's axis** (a slider moves across the layers: its own axis would make a rod and a cam never share a height).
- **A rest value moves a step at a time**: jumping to a far rest passed through the cam (the target was clear on the far side).
- Keep pose for a pose found by contact is kept (`in_1018`) when every body that yields is a mate's first body, a cam's follower rod or a pivoted body (not the wheel of a Geneva drive). **Not tried:** a ratchet (the ratchet comes as two sketches, a wheel and a pawl; it has no 3D model layout with the pawl on a pivot as the Geneva has, so it needs that first), passing hardness on to a depth, the time budget.

## Built, third slice (`in_937`: chains, and what it costs)

A body that yields is now cleared of the driver and the walls and of the body that pushed it only; the others that yield are dealt with when their turn comes, so **a chain gives way link by link** (up to 12 passes a step; when the passes run out with bodies still overlapping the motion stops and says `the chain is too long to settle`, instead of leaving them in each other). The search for room is fine near the body (a tenth of a degree, 0.07 mm: the room between two teeth is a fraction of a degree wide) and coarser further out. The pair test is made both ways round (a touch is a hit or not by the direction the test goes). **Measured (Node, shared sandbox CPU, `prototypes/contact-push`):** a Geneva about 3 to 5 ms a degree; a gear pair 7 to 11 ms; a train of 3, 4, 5, 6 gears 21, 37, 65, 77 ms a degree with every gear within 0.7 to 1.7 degrees of its ratio. So contact is practical for the key interface (a Geneva, a pair, a cam and its rod) and **not for a long train**: that is what the couplings are for (a gear train by couplings costs nothing).

## Built, fourth slice (`in_938`: faster)

A profile of the gear trains showed the cost: the test for a body wholly inside another was a third of it (now left out for the push: `collidesMoving`'s `surfaceOnly`, one optional argument), the pairs' filtered meshes were worked out again at every call (now kept, a miss too), and the search for room went both ways at every distance (now the way it went last first). The Geneva in the app is 0.4 ms a degree median (was 1.4), a gear pair 3 ms (was 11), a train of six gears 20 ms (was 77), with the same accuracy. Left in the profile: the two trees' walk and placing the moving triangles. Not done: a coarser mesh for animation, a warm start from the last step's contacts, testing only pairs that are close by a cheaper box test.

## Built, fifth slice (`in_939`: faster still)

A cheaper box pre-check for pairs far apart; the search for room begins at the last push; only bodies that moved are tested again (a chain is a walk, not a test of every body at every link); the halving is 5 steps. **A coarser mesh for animation was tried and is not worth it** (merging the triangles into grid cells of 0.1 to 0.4 mm changed no times beyond the noise: the tree already limits a test to the leaves that meet). About 30 per cent faster together (a train of 4 gears 6.6 ms a degree, 8 gears 18 to 22, the Geneva 0.36), same accuracy; a test of a train of three gears in `contact.test.ts`. What is left is the narrow phase (about 90 per cent of the time): more speed needs fewer tests (a step worked out from the contact's geometry) or a cheaper test.

## Built, sixth slice (`in_1019`: the depth rule)

**Hardness is passed on to three links** (`solidSet` in `contact.ts`, `DEPTH = 3`): at each step the bodies that move or yield are given a level: 0 for a hard one, then 1, 2, 3 for those whose boxes (each where its motion puts it) touch a body of the level before; a pair is tested when **either** of the two is solid (level 3 or less), so a hard body's push is carried by three solid bodies and one more that is pushed and does not push. A pair of bodies that are neither solid passes through. When the chain goes on past the last solid body (the body it pushes touches another that is not solid) the bar says so: `A touches B, past the depth of 3, and the chain goes on: mark a body in the middle hard to carry the push on`. Measured (Node, a train of gears, `prototypes/contact-push/chain.ts`): with the first gear hard, gears two to five are driven within 2 degrees of their ratios (8 ms a degree for five gears, less than before: the pairs beyond the depth are not tested) and the sixth stays, with the note; with the first and the fourth hard all eight are driven (16 ms a degree). Test: a six gear train in `contact.test.ts`. Not built: the time budget, a settable rest value on a joint, the switch of the hard bodies' drag to the mesh test.

## Built, seventh slice (`in_1020`: the three that were not built)

- **The hard bodies' tests read the kernel's mesh** (`meshOf` in `hard.ts`, from the outcome the scene came from: `outcomeOfScene`, `registerOutcome` in `compile.ts`): every face of a body, the walls of a gear's teeth too; the face planes of the scene (a gear body has two) are the fallback, and for a model of one body, which has no name. The drag, the stop search, the joint's limits against a hard body and the contact push all use it; the existing hard-body tests pass unchanged, and a test (`hardgear.test.ts`) shows two hard gears meeting by their teeth, which the old source could not. The kernel's boolean is still the final answer (a pose kept, the overlap report).
- **A time budget** (`BUDGET_MS = 20` for a call of `solveContact`): past it the steps left wait for the next frame; the amount stops where it got to and the motion starts again from there (a play goes slower, not frozen), a scrub remembers where it was asked to go (`ContactState.target`; `main.ts` goes on to it, a part each frame), and the bar says `this push is heavy: it plays slower than real time`.
- **A settable rest on a joint** (degrees from where the body is drawn; none by default): `Pivot.rest` (the `pivot` command takes `rest`, null takes it away) and a coaxial mate's `turnRest` (the joint's own number, `restCommand` in `mate.ts`; counted from the joint's zero). The bar shows, with contact on, a field for each body that yields and turns on a pivot or a mate: `name back to [ ] °`, empty for none (`Frame.motionBar.springs`, the intent `motionRest`, `doMotionRest`). A body that has one goes back toward it a step at a time as far as it is clear to, once nothing holds it away. The cam's rod still takes its rest from the cam. Tests in `pivot.test.ts` (a body pushed by another goes back with a rest of 0, stays with none; the bar's field sets and clears it) and `contact.test.ts` (the budget).

## Answers (`in_1017`: the human took the AI's recommendations)

- **Depth of passed-on hardness: 3.** A hard body that pushes makes the soft bodies it touches solid for their own pushing, to three links from the hard body; marking one body is enough for a mechanism of up to three links, and a longer chain marks a body in the middle as hard. A **time budget** for a frame and the jam message `the chain is too long to settle` (built) stand behind it. Built at `in_1019` (above).
- **Ghost:** not built, and not a state until a case needs it (a body that must pass through everything). If a case comes, it is a setting of the body, not of the joint.
- **The rest value is a number on the joint** (the joint of a pivot, a coaxial mate or a cam's follower; degrees or mm, "return to"; none by default). A cam's follower rod gets it from the cam today (`rest` toward the cam, a millimetre a step); a settable one is not built yet.
- **The mesh test is for interaction, the exact boolean for the final answer**, in steps: the hard bodies' drag, scrub and the animation use the mesh test (`operation/collide.ts` with the kernel's mesh, `contact.ts`); the kernel's boolean stays for what is kept (a pose kept, an interference report). `hardCollision` is the seam: the old and new tests are run side by side on the existing hard-body tests before the drag is switched. **One finding to carry into that step:** the face planes the drag reads (`meshOf`) leave out the walls of a gear's teeth, so a hard gear is tested on its caps only; the contact push reads the kernel's mesh by the faces' ranges (`bodyMesh`) and the drag should too.

## Free push (`in_1048`, approved at `in_1049`: the AI's recommendations)

A hard body with **no joint** (no pivot, no coaxial mate, no rod) that the played body meets is no longer a wall: it **yields by moving** (translation only to begin with) by the smallest amount along the contact that clears the overlap, **as long as no standing mate or constraint is broken**. The move goes through the same projection onto the standing mates that a free drag uses (`projectOnMates`), so a body with a coaxial mate still only turns or slides on it. If the move cannot be made without breaking a constraint, the motion stops and says which (the refusal rule: it names it, flashes it, offers to delete it).

Choices (the AI's recommendations, taken at `in_1049`): **a default** for every hard body that has no joint; **a cap** on how far a pushed body may travel in one play (the depth limit and the time budget stay); **a body that is fixed (locked) or has a mate stays a wall** as before; a setting **"stays where it ends up"** beside Keep pose (a play ends with the body returned unless it is chosen). **Not physics:** no turning from the contact normal (the lever slides, it does not swing: a lever that should swing needs a pivot), no gravity or friction; a pushed body stops where it clears.

**Built (`in_1049`).** `contact.ts` `yieldFree`: a `Pushable` with `free` (a move from where the body is drawn); a step of the driver pushes it by the smallest clear move in 64 ways round the plane across the turning axis (the way it was last pushed first, then the ones near it), no more than 2.5 mm a step (more would throw it through what pushes it, clear on the far side of a pin that is inside it: the mesh test sees surfaces, not containment) and 60 mm in all; it goes back a millimetre a step when nothing holds it away. When it cannot go on it says what it is against: "x_s_002 pushes x_s_084 against x_s_083". Who is free (`motion.ts`): a hard body that is not the driver, not carried by a coupling, with no mate, no pivot, and not **fixed**. **Fixed body** (`Scene.fixed`, command `setFixed`, ring leaf Fixed body / Free body beside Hard body, `isFixed`) is the wall. **Turning (`in_1070`, built):** a body with no joint has the three freedoms of a body in a plane (two slides and a turn about its own middle, `Pushable.spin`, `reach`), searched together: 64 ways round at five heights toward the turn, a degree of turn counted as a slide of `reach` times a degree, no more than 2.5 mm-equivalent a step and 120 degrees in all; a free body against a body that yields and cannot get out of its way goes out of that body's way itself. Tried on the two files of the jam reports: the lever swings out of the bar's way and the gear makes the whole turn with no mate and no pivot. Not built: the setting "stays where it ends up" beside Keep pose (a pose found by contact is still kept only for joints), a cap setting (60 mm is fixed in the code).

## Rationale

The human wants "set one body to move and the rest follows" without a physics engine. Most of it is already there (joints, couplings, the mesh test, the sliding); the missing part is a push rule that is bounded and explainable. The prototype shows the rule alone reproduces the one mechanism that had a hand-made formula, at a cost that looks affordable, which is the evidence for asking for approval. Nothing is built in the app.
