# Gear finishing: spline flank (prototype done, `in_792`; arcs kept, `in_797`); tooth chamfer, worm wheel land and helical rack (built, `in_783`, `in_790`, `in_791`)

**Tooth chamfer built (`in_791`), then made 3 to 6 times quicker (`in_798`):** `GearSpec.chamfer` (modules, 0 to 1, the Chamfer field, last in the box of a spur gear and a ring; needs a thickness or a helix, and less than half of it). First made as a turned cone cut by a ring-shaped cutter, which cost 4 to 9 times the build time; the cost was the cone meeting the 480 cylindrical flank faces (`prototypes/chamfer-cut`). Now a step of its own (`toothChamfer`; `chamferOf` in `gear.ts`, `chamferSteps` in `gearscene.ts`, the kernel op in `interpreter.mjs`) that cuts one **flat** 45 degree wedge off each tooth tip at each face in one boolean: the bevel is flat across a tooth tip, not conical (a difference of under 0.01 mm over the width of a tip, the same material removed to a cubic millimetre). **Cost now, measured (8 mm thick, module 2, bore 5): 40 teeth 1.6 s plain and 1.85 s with a chamfer (was 5.9 s), 100 teeth 2.55 s and 4.65 s (was 13.8 s), a 40 tooth ring 1.35 s and 1.75 s; a helical gear 1.4 s and 5.8 s (was 11 s): still slow, the twisted flanks are not cylinders.** Works for spur, helical and ring gears; not for racks, pulleys, sprockets, clock gears, worms, wheels. Checked: one body, the same box, less material, taken off again by `setGear`. Not looked at in the app, not printed.

**Built (`in_783`, approved: "Yes, build the helical rack and the wheel land"):** (1) **Helical rack** (`GearSpec.helix` and `width` on a rack; fields Helix and Thickness after Clearance; `rackOutline`, `helixShear` in `gear.ts`): the outline is the section across the rack (module mn / cos(helix), pressure angle atan(tan an / cos(helix)), addendum and dedendum the normal ones), and the body is one `extrude` step with `slantx` and `slanty` (new step numbers: the sideways slide for each mm of depth, in the plane; the kernel's `op.slant`, an oblique prism) so each layer is slid along the rack by tan(helix). Mesh rack from a helical gear and Mesh gear from a helical rack work; the rack takes the gear's helix number as it is (a rack and a gear that mesh have the same sign here, an outside gear pair have opposite signs): `test/helicalrack.test.ts` checks it in the kernel (the right hand rack overlaps the gear by under 8 mm3, the other hand by a good deal more). (2) **Worm wheel land** (`wheel.land`, the Land field; none by default): first built as a round shoulder (`in_783`); the human then asked for the toothed land cut by the worm's envelope (`in_790`) and that replaced it. The end sections are carried straight out to the faces (two `extrude` steps, each a piece of its own), the worm's envelope (the cylinder of the thread's tip circle plus a tenth of a module about its axis, as far as it lies past each face: a circular segment drawn across the worm's axis and carried along it, `envelope` in `wormscene.ts`) is cut out of each (a `combine` subtract), and the three pieces are joined (a `combine` add with `glue`). A step can now name the body another step of its gear made (`@s1`, put right by `resolveOwnRefs` in `gearscene.ts`) because a cut by a step acts on every body made so far, the worm's included. **Cost: the wheel takes about 23 s to make with a land against about 5.5 s without (the join of the land with the throated part is most of it, 38 s without `glue`), so the default is no land;** the tool and Mesh gear take a Land typed. Checked in the kernel: one body, width + 2 land thick, and the worm and the wheel with a land overlap by under a cubic millimetre. Not looked at in the app.

**Asked (`in_770`):** are our worm wheels made as real ones are, should the top and bottom of the teeth be chamfered and what would it cost, would a richer curve for the tooth flank be more exact or quicker than 4 arcs, and why can a helical gear not mesh with a rack. Nothing is built for these; this records what was found (from the code and general gear knowledge, **not measured** here) and what to decide.

**Worm wheel.** A real wheel is a helical-toothed blank with a **throat**: its rim is hollowed by a circle about the worm's axis (radius the worm's outside radius plus clearance) so it wraps the worm; the blank has a straight cylindrical **land** at each face (the face width is limited, about 0.75 of the worm's tip diameter, and the corners are often broken), cut by a hob that is the worm. The human's picture (a straight sided helical gear with the curved seat cut from it) is how it is made. Ours (`wheelsections.ts`, `worm.ts`) is the whole of what the worm leaves of the blank, to the throat arc, with a width up to 1.26 times the throat: the tooth ends where the throat arc meets the face, so the end of a tooth is a thin wedge. Option: a **land** of a chosen width (default about one module) at each face: the outline is the sections already worked out, with the first and last section repeated out to the faces (a straight loft piece), so the tooth keeps its section to the face; no new maths, five sections become seven. Not tried; needs the check that the wheel still meshes (kernel intersection with the worm, as the tests do).

**Tooth chamfer.** Chamfering every tooth edge at both faces is hundreds of edge operations (about 20 edges a tooth, a fillet or chamfer each) and the kernel is slow and fragile on that; do not do it edge by edge. Cheaper and how machinists do it: **one cut with a revolved profile** (the blank's envelope with a 45 degree cone at each end, a chamfer of c modules), which breaks every tooth tip at once. It adds about one conical face a tooth at each end; with `dec_gear_feature` those are not parts, so the page does not slow down; the kernel adds one boolean. Printing note: a 0.2 to 0.4 mm chamfer on the bottom face also helps the first layer; this is the human's call. To measure before deciding: a prototype with the 40 tooth gear, time with and without.

**A richer curve for the flank: prototype done (`in_792`, `prototypes/gear-spline-flank`, its README has the table).** One cubic B-spline a flank matches four arcs for accuracy (0.0004 to 0.0007 mm at 40 teeth) and makes half the faces (243 against 483) and builds its outline 3 to 4 times faster; **not a sure win for triangles** (4 times more at 40 teeth, fewer at 100) **or booleans** (no consistent difference, about 1.8 times faster at 100 teeth, one run each), and it is touchy: the first settings tried gave 0.22 mm and 870 000 triangles; only cubic with tolerance 1e-3 to 1e-4 works well. Recommendation (the AI's, not decided): do not change the default; a spline `detail` option is worth building only if the face count matters.

**A helical rack, and a rack with a helix field.** `meshWith` refuses a helical gear against a rack ("a helical gear does not mesh with a rack yet") and `canHelix` excludes racks, so the rack has no Helix field. A helical rack is the rack's section (transverse module mn / cos(helix), the same trapezoid) **sheared** along the rack as it is extruded: each layer slid sideways by height x tan(helix). The body is an oblique extrusion (the kernel's prism along a slanted vector) rather than the twist used for a gear, so it needs a step that takes a direction not along the normal, then Helix on the rack (opposite hand to the gear it meshes with), and the mesh placement shifted by the shear. Medium work; not started.

**Answered (`in_797`): leave the arcs as they are** ("Yes, leave the arcs as they are", to the recommendation not to build a spline option); the prototype stays as the record. **Question for the human, still open:** the helical gear's chamfer still adds 4.4 s: wedges following the helix were asked for (`in_799`) and tried in the form of narrower wedges, which changed nothing (the cost is a plane meeting a twisted flank by marching, not how many faces the wedge reaches; `prototypes/chamfer-cut`), so nothing was built; is 4.4 s acceptable, or should the chamfered tooth be built on one tooth block and copied round (see the end of this entry), which is a larger change? (3) is the wheel land worth a default of one module at about four times the build time (now off)?

## Rationale

The record keeps these as open questions because each is a choice of shape or cost the human has not decided, and none was measured.

**Helical gears made faster: tried, little to gain (`in_800`, `prototypes/helical-fast`).** A plain helical gear is not slower than a spur gear of the same size (40 teeth 1.4 s against 1.8 s); its time is the twisted B-spline flanks (sweep, sewing, validity, volume, mesh: 100 teeth 4.7 s against 2.5 s for a spur gear). Sweeping one tooth and copying it round, and a shell built by hand with no sewing, gave no gain (the second was also invalid); keeping one volume measurement instead of two was lost in the noise. Nothing changed in the app. The one real gain found is a chamfered tooth made on one tooth block and copied round (the wedge cut on a fortieth of the gear), which needs the chamfer made part of the helical body's own step: not built, asked below.
