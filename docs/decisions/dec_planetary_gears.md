# A planetary set that keeps its numbers

**Approved (`in_762`, after `in_761`):** the human asked for a planetary gear maker and answered the design questions: either the teeth or the ratio is given and the other follows; the carrier plate is optional; the set is a feature that keeps its numbers and Edit gear edits the whole of it as it was made; spur and helical; the ratio is shown for each part held; the carrier is a plate with a pin at each planet (thickness, pin diameter and clearance set by the person); each planet is a body of its own.

**Built (`operation/planetary.ts`, commands `planetary`, `setPlanetary`, `deletePlanetary`, `Scene.planetaries`, saved in the file):**
- A set is a record (plane, centre, turn, numbers) that ties together gear records `<id>s` (sun), `<id>p1..N` (planets) and `<id>r` (ring): ordinary gears, so every body is a separate body with its own steps, and everything that works on a gear (selecting, extruding, meshing, export) works on them. Editing the set sets each gear again (`setGear`), adds or takes away planets, and makes the carrier again.
- **Numbers:** sun and planet teeth, number of planets, module, width (every gear's thickness), pressure, backlash, clearance, helix (the sun's), sun bore, ring wall, pin diameter, pin gap, carrier thickness. The ring has `sun + 2 planet` teeth. The planet's bore is the pin plus the gap all round.
- **Checks (`problemsOf`):** whole numbers, `(sun + ring) / planets` whole (the planets spaced evenly all mesh), neighbouring planets' tips apart (in the transverse module for a helical set), a width; few teeth are warned as undercut.
- **Ratio to teeth (`fromRatio`):** for a wanted ratio with the ring, carrier or sun held, every sun and planet count that passes the checks, nearest first; when none is exact the nearest is said (four planets cannot reach 7.3).
- **Ratios shown (`ratios`):** ring held 1 + Zr/Zs; carrier held -Zr/Zs; sun held 1 + Zs/Zr (input over output).
- **Layout:** the planets are placed with `meshWith` on the sun, `360 / N` apart; the ring is placed with `meshWith` round the first planet (its phase then fits the others by the whole-number rule).
- **Hands (helical):** the sun one hand, the planets the other, **the ring the planets' hand**. The AI said in the chat that the ring takes the sun's hand: that was wrong; an internal pair has the same hand (the contact point has both circles' centres on the same side, so their counter-clockwise tangents agree), and `meshWith` already does it that way. The human approved the AI's sentence; this is the correction, to be told to them.
- **Carrier:** one body: an extrude with the plate (radius the ring's pitch radius, below the gears' plane, thickness as set, a hole for the sun's shaft: the sun's bore plus the gap) and a pin at each planet (its length the gears' width), each figure with its own depth (`alsoDepth`). Its parts are ordinary drawn circles named `d/<id>.*`.
- **Checked (`test/planetary.test.ts`, 13 tests; the whole suite 1357 tests, typecheck, layers, boundaries, unused pass; looked at in a browser, see below):** the kernel makes the bodies (6, or 7 with the carrier, whose volume is the plate less the hole plus the pins to 1%); no body of a spur set runs into another (0 mm3 in every pair tried), a helical set under 0.01 mm3; a planet turned half a tooth overlaps by more than 5 mm3 (the check sees a bad fit); edit (planets 4 to 2 to 5, carrier on and off), refused numbers leave the scene alone, delete takes everything, save and open.

**The tool (built, `interaction/tools.ts` `planetaryMade`, `gearhelp.ts`, `ui/gearbox.ts`, `interaction/control/`):** **Planetary set** in the Gear submenu (a click for the centre, then Enter or a second click makes it). Box fields: Sun teeth, Planet teeth, Planets, Ratio, Module, Width, Helix, Pressure, Backlash, Clearance, Sun bore, Wall, Pin, Pin gap, Carrier (0 or empty: no carrier). Either way round: type the teeth and the ratio is shown, or type the ratio (ring held, sun in, carrier out) and the nearest whole teeth are chosen, keeping a sun or planet count that is typed too; when no whole teeth reach it the box says so. A blue line in the box shows the ring's teeth and the ratio with the ring, the carrier and the sun held, and warnings (undercut). The preview draws every gear, the plate and the pins. Numbers that cannot make a set are taken back to the nearest that can and flash, as for a gear. **Edit gear** on any gear of the set (or its chip) opens the whole set with its numbers; **Delete** on a gear takes the whole set; **Move** and **Rotate** of a gear's sketch move and turn the whole set (`gearCommand`); Delete on the carrier's body is the plain body delete. Defaults: 24 sun, 18 planets, 3 planets, module 2, width 6, no carrier. `test/planetaryui.test.ts` (6 tests) drives it through the controller.

**Tried in a browser (`in_798`, headless Chromium, software drawing, the vite dev server):** the tool starts from the menu action, the box and the ratio line show, a ratio of 5 gave 18 / 27 / 72 teeth, the set was made (6 bodies with a carrier, about 8 s to the bodies in this software setup), the side view shows the plate under the gears, Edit gear from a planet's side opened the whole set, a helical set of 4 planets was made from it (7 bodies), undo and redo went back and forth whole, Delete and its undo took and brought back the whole set, 5 planets said why and took the number back (the box flashed red); no errors in the browser's console. **Found:** the blue ratio line stood above the box's title (moved under it). **The dashed line (`in_801`):** it was the tool's alignment guide (`guidesOf`: a dashed line from the cursor to every corner it lines up with, level or upright). While a set is edited the cursor is the set's own centre, which always lines up with its planets' corners, so a line was drawn; it was not planetary-specific (any gear being edited next to other corners would show it). Fixed: a gear being changed, or a planetary set, shows no guides (`previewOf`); tested, and gone in the browser.

**Built after (`in_799`):** **Plate gap** (a sixteenth field after Carrier, mm, 0 by default): with some, the carrier is made on a plane of its own (`d/<id>.pl`) that far below the gears' plane and the pins are that much longer, so the planets and the plate do not touch (`test/planetary.test.ts`: the plate from -(gap + thickness) to -gap, the pins up to the gears' top, no overlap with any gear); **a draft of the bodies while a set is being edited** (`frame.ts` `operationResult`, as for a helical gear: the kernel's draft mode; about 8 s in this software browser, drawn when it arrives); **selecting the carrier's body and Edit gear opens the whole set** (it already did through the menu; tested; the gear chip is not shown for the carrier).

**Built after (`in_800`, the human chose the forms in the question panel):** **Spacing** (field, 0 or 1): 1 puts the planets at the nearest meshing positions, each a whole number of tooth steps `2 pi / (sun + ring)` from the first (`planetSteps`), so any number of planets mesh (the ring is turned to the first planet and the algebra of the whole number rule holds for any whole step), spaced as evenly as the teeth allow; a warning says the load is not balanced; the neighbour clearance uses the closest pair; checked in the kernel (3 planets on a 16 / 12 set, 3 helical, 5 planets on 24 / 18: no planet runs into the sun, the ring or the next, under 0.1 mm3). **Housing** (fields Housing, Housing cap, Housing fit, mm): a tube round the ring (`housing` thick, `housingFit` (0.2) outside the ring's wall, the width of the gears plus the plate gap) and, with a cap thickness, a disc on a plane of its own above the gears with the hole for the sun's shaft, each a body of its own (`shellStep`, `capStep` on the record; kept by name through edits; the ring stays separate); tested for volumes, heights, no overlap with the ring, carrier, planets or sun; an end cap with no housing is refused. **Moving a set** (Move and Rotate of a gear's sketch) is tested at last (the record's centre and turn, every gear, the carrier; undo; a turn about a planet swings the set round it) and was tried in a browser (a typed move of 30 mm moved the set from 21.65 to 51.65 with all its bodies).

**Not built:** unequal spacing chosen angle by angle (Spacing 1 picks it); the housing has no bolt flange or mounting features; the planets and the cap have the plate gap between them, not a gap of their own.

**Rules found (web search, standard mode, pages not read in full):** `Zr = Zs + 2 Zp`; planets on a circle of radius `m (Zs + Zp) / 2`; `(Zs + Zr) / N` a whole number for N equally spaced planets in phase (an out of phase design, AGMA's "sequential", or uneven spacing can do without); tips of neighbouring planets clear; Willis `(ns - nc) / (nr - nc) = -Zr / Zs`. Sources: eng-tips thread 357212; Glinsky, drivetrainhub "Chapter 4 - Planetary Gears"; AGMA "Epicyclic gearboxes" (Aug 2021); tec-science planetary pages; woodgears.ca planetary ratio page; tspi.at planetary calculator. The checks in the tests (a set that passes the whole number rule fits; one that does not, does not) are the AI's own verification of the rule against the kernel.

## Rationale

The set is made of the gear records that already exist, so the whole of the gear tool's machinery (the speed rules, the kernel's caches, selecting, extruding, meshing) applies unchanged; the record only holds the numbers that tie them together and re-derives them on edit, the same way a gear's parts are re-derived from its record. Planets as separate bodies is what the human asked for, and one carrier body avoids a plate and N loose pins.
