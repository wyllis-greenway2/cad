# Hardware tools in the model view: a small helper bar, the selection as the clicks, a live preview

**Accepted as proposed by the AI from `in_879`, with the human's choices (`in_880`: Fastener Holes first; `in_881`: the hardware dialogs are replaced by the panel and the preview, Path repeat keeps its dialog for now).** The human asked that choosing hardware in the menu return to the normal CAD screen, where there are options, where clicking on surfaces shows what would be done, and where changing a setting updates the preview.

1. **Choosing the tool.** Shape, Models, **Fastener Holes** (and later **Fasteners**) arms a tool; no dialog opens and the view stays as it is. A **panel** stands still on the left of the page (as the gear box does, `in_685`) with the settings; the page's own selection is how surfaces are chosen: the face, with Shift the corners, circles and lines that place the holes (as before, `in_707`, `in_813`). Nothing new to learn: the selection is the click.
2. **The panel** shows the same fields as the dialog had (the receiver cards with their cross-sections, size and clearance chips, where the holes go, the path rules, the depth, the thread engagement), made from the same descriptions (`receiverDialog`), so they cannot differ; a button **Cut** (Enter does the same), **Close** (Esc does the same). While no flat face is selected it says so and what to select.
3. **The preview** is drawn in the model view, over the face, from the numbers: for each place the outline of what would be cut as the cross-section of the receiver turned to four sides and as rings (the hole's circle, the countersink's, the counterbore's, a nut pocket's hexagon), down to the depth (a hole right through shows to the far side of the body). It is redrawn as the selection or any setting changes, with no kernel. It reuses the view's toolpath overlay, so nothing new is drawn by the renderer.
4. **Committing.** Cut makes the holes as one command (one undo, as now) and the tool stays on with the selection kept, so another set can be made at once; Close leaves. A refusal (a number that is not one, too many holes, an offset too big) is said in the panel and shows no preview.
5. **Order:** (a) the panel from the field descriptions and the Fastener Holes tool with its preview; (b) the **Fasteners** (the parts) the same way, a ghost of the part placed on the surface or in a hole that is clicked; (c) Path repeat later if the human wants it.

## Changed by the human (`in_882`, `in_883`, `in_884`): a small bar, not a panel

The first build (below) was a large panel on the left. **The human said it should be smaller**: choose the hardware, the window goes away, and a **little helper bar** stands at the top like the one for a fillet, with a few options: select a **line** and the holes are traced along it with a separation and a division method; select a **face** and the centre line of the cut path (the face's outline moved in) is shown, and an **inset** changes it; different patterns across the surface. **Chosen (`in_883`, `in_884`): the numbers are small boxes in the bar that are typed in; the first pattern for a face is the outline moved in by the inset.** **Built:** `ui/holebar.ts` (the pill, in the style of the operation bar: the receiver and the size to choose, a spacing by a distance (*Every*) or a number (*Number*), the inset and the corner rule for a face, the end rule for a line, a depth, a check and a cross; a line under it with how many holes and how far apart, or why none; the numbers answer as they are typed) replaces the panel (`ui/optionspanel.ts` is gone; `ui/fields.ts` stays, shared by the dialogs); `main.ts` reads what the selection says (**a line selected: along it, with no corner rule; a face: along its outline moved in by the inset; corners or circles picked: at them; nothing yet: it says what to select**); a **line drawn on a flat face is enough on its own** (it stands for that face, `selectedFace`); the preview now also draws **the path the holes follow as a dashed pale line** (`guideLines`, the outline moved in, or the line) beside the holes in red, through the same overlay. **Tests:** `holepreview.test.ts` (10): the guide of an outline (the outline moved in by the inset, on the face), the guide following a different inset, the dashes, a line alone as the face, the spacing; and a browser pass (the bar over a block, the face clicked: *along the outline*, six holes and the dashed line drawn; the inset typed as 12: the holes moved in, 16 mm apart; Enter cut them; an inset that is too big said why, in the bar). **Not built:** a grid or two rows of holes across a face (the human asked for the outline first), dragging the inset in the view, the Fasteners (the parts) in a bar, a depth for each kind by default.

## Built first (`in_880`): the panel (replaced by the bar above)

**Shape, Models, Fastener Holes** now opens the **panel** on the left (always enabled: it says what to select) and leaves the view as it is; **the dialog is gone** for it. The panel is `ui/optionspanel.ts`, drawn from the same field descriptions as the dialog (`receiverDialog`) by the shared field drawing `ui/fields.ts` (taken out of `ui/dialog.ts`, which uses it too): receiver cards with their cross-sections (in as many columns as the panel holds), size and clearance chips, the picture, where the holes go, the path rules, depth, thread engagement; a number typed answers **as it is typed** (the preview follows each key); the cursor stays in the box. **The selection is the click:** the face, and with Shift corners, circles and lines; *where the holes go* follows the selection (picked places, else lines, else the middle) until a place is chosen in the panel. **The preview** (`interaction/holepreview.ts`, no kernel): for each hole the section of the receiver on four sides and a ring for each step (the hole, the cone of a countersink, a counterbore, a nut pocket's hexagon at the face and at its floor), a hole right through drawn to the far side of the body, a typed depth to that depth; as lines in the toolpath's form so **the view's existing overlay draws it** (`setToolpath`). **Cut** (the button, or Enter) makes the holes as one command (one undo) and the tool **stays on** with the selection; **Close** or Esc leaves (Esc is taken by the tool first, so it does not clear the selection). **Tests:** `holepreview.test.ts` (6): the lines of one hole (two rings and the section: 76 lines), the depth drawn to the body's far side and never past it, the extra rings of a countersink, a counterbore and a nut pocket's hexagons, a row along the outline (one set for each hole), the preview following the size and no preview with no place, the plan's refusals; `receiverPlan` now makes the places for the cut and the preview alike; and a browser pass (a block opened from a file, the tool opened from the ring with nothing selected, the face clicked: the hole drawn through the block; the countersunk card, M6, *along the face's outline*, a distance typed: six holes drawn as the number changed; Cut: the holes made). **Not built:** the **Fasteners** (the parts) in the panel ((b), their dialog stays for now), Path repeat in the panel ((c), only if asked), a hover preview of the hole under the pointer before a click, the preview cleared after a cut (it still shows over the holes just made, until the selection changes), touch layouts for the panel.

## Rationale

The dialog had to be closed to see the model, so every setting was a guess followed by a cut and an undo. The preview is cheap to draw from numbers, the descriptions of the settings already exist, and the selection already says which face and which places, so the tool needs no new way to click. Drawing the preview with the existing overlay keeps the renderer as it is.

## Far-side nut, shift (`in_909`)
**Built:** a **Nut** choice in the bar (*none* / *pocket on the other side*) cuts a hexagonal nut pocket on the far face at every place, at the same time as the near-side head profile (clearance, countersunk or counterbored): head on one side, nut on the other (`farNutSteps`, `ReceiverPlan.far`; refused for a tapped hole or a nut pocket, and for a typed depth, since it needs the hole right through; the preview draws the far hexagon too). A **Shift** box walks every place along the path: on a line the whole row moves (places past an end are dropped, with a refusal if all are), on a closed outline the places walk round it (`shift` in the path rules, number `pr9`; older records without it read as 0). **Hardware is changed in the bar:** Type and Size selects. **Not built:** curved surfaces (asked in `in_909`; what is meant (radial holes through a curved wall, a path wrapped on a curved face) is to be asked).

## One hardware tool: drill, tap, both, and insert (`in_910`, `in_911`)
**Asked (`in_910`) and answered (`in_911`):** a hole can be **drilled**, **tapped**, or **both** (a clearance hole to a depth, then the tapped hole past it: *chosen*), and the tool is the **one hardware tool**: it cuts the holes and can also put the hardware models into them, **Insert off by default**, a toggle to turn it on; Insert puts in the **screw plus the optional washer and nut** (*chosen*); **Fastener Holes becomes the one bar and the Fasteners menu entry goes away** (*chosen*).
**Built:** Type **Drill + tap** (`ReceiverKind` `drillTap`: the clearance hole to *Drill to* mm, then the tap drill on to *Depth*; *Drill to* empty means the tapped part is 1.5 diameters deep, a rule of mine, not read from a source: open for the human). **Insert** (*no* / *yes: put the hardware in*) makes, after every hole is cut (a cut works on the last body, which a screw must not be), a screw as its **own body** at each place: a countersunk screw in a countersunk hole, a socket head cap screw in the others (head in the counterbore, or standing on the face for a plain, tapped or drill + tap hole), with its hexagon socket, of the **longest standard length** (ISO 888 list) that fits the hole, or the **Length** typed (refused if longer than the hole); with **Nut** on the far side its nut is inserted in the pocket, and a **nut pocket** on this side gets its nut (its screw would come from the other side, so no screw). The sizes are the same nominal ones as the loose parts. The *Fasteners* entry and its dialog are gone from the ring (`models:fasteners`); the part builders it used stay as the code the tests use.
**Not built:** a washer under the head (no washer pocket or seat is cut for it yet; asked in `in_911` as "optional"), a screw from the far side into a nut pocket, thread drawn on the screw or the hole (the tapped hole is the tap drill and the screw's shank is the major diameter, so they overlap as a thread does), a smooth head option in the bar, a preview of the inserted screws.

## Menu place and look (`in_912`)
The tool is no longer a model: it sits **directly under Shape** with the **screw icon**, named **Fastener tool** (`tool:fasteners`); the Models group is gone from the ring. The preview is a **wireframe** (a `wire` layer of the toolpath overlay, charcoal like a body's edges, 1.3 px) instead of the red cut lines; the dashed guide stays pale grey. A try, as asked: say if it should change (a colour, or dashed hidden parts).

## Curved surfaces (`in_929`)
**Asked and answered:** the tool works on a curved surface (a hole through a curved wall, and holes along a line on a curved face, both) *like a flat surface*: a line is drawn, the fasteners are positioned along it, each going in along the surface's normal.
**Built (round faces: a post's wall, a bore, a piece of one):** select the round face and, with Shift, a line (or corners and circles); the spacing, number, ends, shift and inset-free rules are the flat ones, measured **along the drawn line**; each place is taken **straight out from the axis onto the surface**, and its hole goes in along the normal there (away from the axis for a post, toward it for a bore). So a line drawn along the axis gives a row of holes down the post; a line drawn across (or round) it gives holes round it. Each hole has its own way in (`ReceiverPlan.spots`); the preview, the nut pocket on this side (its hexagon on a plane touching the surface at the hole) and the inserted screw and nut follow it. **Limits:** the spacing is along the drawn line, not along the surface (a line drawn round a post at a different radius from the post's spaces the holes by its own length); a place past the end of the round face is refused; a nut on the far side and a screw without a typed depth are refused on a curved surface (how far the hole goes there is not known: a post may be hollow); the head's recess is made at the touching plane, so a wide head sits a little proud at its sides; **only round (cylindrical) faces**, not cones, spheres or freeform faces; no outline to follow on a round face (a line or places are needed); a line cannot be drawn on a curved face itself, it is drawn on a plane and projected.

## One panel layout for the tools (`in_931`, `in_932`)
**Asked:** the app has many different interfaces; reuse the gear box's layout. **Chosen (`in_932`):** the fastener tool takes the gear box's layout, as one shared component. **Built:** `ui/panelkit.ts` (the panel on the left, a titled section, a row with its name, box, unit and a line saying what it is, number boxes, dropdowns, the buttons at the bottom, the red / teal / blue message boxes) is used by the gear box (`ui/gearbox.ts`, same look as before) and by the new `ui/holepanel.ts`, which **replaces the helper bar** (`ui/holebar.ts` is gone; it was the answer to `in_882`, which asked for the panel to be smaller; the human has now chosen the panel layout again, with the rows kept short: a line under a row only where it says something). The panel has three groups: *The hole* (Type, Size, Drill to, Depth, Nut), *Where* (what is selected, Spacing, Every or Number, Inset, Shift, Corners, Ends) and *Hardware* (Insert, Length), with Close and Cut at the bottom (Enter and Esc work as before). **Not done:** Path repeat's dialog and the other dialogs are not moved to the panel yet (the shared pieces are there for it); the panel is taller than the bar was and stands over the left of the view.

**The other dialogs (`in_933`):** the question page (`ui/dialog.ts`: Export, Import, Path repeat, material, machine, laser, layers and the others) now stands in the same left panel (`ui/panelkit.ts`) instead of a centred card: the screen is not dimmed, the model stays in view, and a text, a number or a plain list of choices is a row with its name and a box or dropdown (`panelFieldRow` in `ui/fields.ts`); cards, chips, pictures, ticks and shown values are drawn as before, as wide as the panel. It keeps its old rules: a click outside it, or Esc, cancels, Enter answers, and the page waits for the answer. A plain list of choices is now a dropdown (it was radio buttons), so a long label is cut in the closed box and shown whole in the list.

**A round face alone (`in_946`):** the first build needed a line drawn across the round face, so the face alone only said so. A flat face alone gives a row along its outline, so a round face alone now gives a row **round it at the middle of its height**: the path is the arc (or the whole circle) of the face at its mid height, **spaced along the surface** itself by the same rules (distance or number, ends, shift), and the **Inset** box is how far in from the face's two ends the first and last hole stand (a whole post has none); the holes go in along the normal as before. A line across the face still gives a row along that line. The corner rules do not apply to a round face. **Limits:** the row is at the middle of the height (a line gives another height); the face's turn is measured from the face's mesh.

## Through everything, with the body of the face giving the defaults (`in_948`, `in_949`)
**Asked (`in_948`):** a lid fastened to an object below; the holes could not go past the body of the selected face (the preview stopped there). **Chosen direction (`in_949`):** the cuts go fully through everything under or behind the selection, and the selected body's thickness gives the defaults: the depth a clearance goes to, with a pilot for the tap past that point.
**Built:** the plan has two lengths: `reach` (the body of the face) and `stack` (everything in the way: a ray from each place into the model through the bodies' boxes, `stackReach`). The **preview** and the **far nut** (at the bottom of the stack) use the stack, so the preview shows what is cut. **Drill + tap**, when there is more body under the one of the face: *Drill to* empty is the body's thickness (clearance through the lid) and *Depth* empty is that plus a pilot of 1.5 diameters; with nothing under, as before. An empty depth of any other hole is right through everything. An **inserted screw** is fitted to the whole run (the longest standard length up to the depth, or the stack). The panel's Depth hint says how thick the body is and how far everything reaches, and *Drill to* shows the body's thickness as its placeholder. **Limits:** the stack is found from the bodies' boxes, so a body whose box is in the way but whose shape is not is counted (the preview is long and a far nut would be placed at its bottom); on a round face the stack is the face's own reach as before; a tapped pilot is the thread's tap drill, not a drawn thread.

## One set of rules for the lines a face gives, flat or round (`in_958`)
**Asked (`in_958`):** the tool should not treat a round face differently from a flat one: stepping the edges in is the same on both; the mid-height row of a round face is really a mode of its own for any face (one edge stepped in, here to the middle, and in general one edge stepped in and repeated); the tool has rules that make lines from the edges available, and these lines get the pattern repeat like any lines. No special cases.
**Built (`interaction/holerows.ts`, used by `receiverPlan` for every face):** a surface says its **outline** (in its own two directions, counter-clockwise) and how to turn a point of them into a place with a way in: a flat face is its plane (`flatSurface`), a round face is **unrolled** (the way round by arc length, the way along the axis: `roundSurface`; its outline the rectangle of its extents). The rules make the lines: **edges stepped in** (the whole outline moved in by the Inset, with the corner rules), **one edge stepped in** (an edge selected with the face, else the longest side, moved in by the Inset), **one edge, centred** (that edge moved to the middle of the face: half the face's width across it); a line **drawn** on a plane is followed as it is (taken onto the surface). Each line is placed by the path rules like any line (distance or number, ends, shift, corners), and may be repeated in **rows**: a number of rows and the spacing between them (the first at the Inset, the rest each a spacing further in; the centred line has them either side of it). The spacing along a round face is along the surface. A face that goes all the way round (a post's wall) has no side edges: its lines are the lowest and the highest line round it, stepped in along the axis. An edge of the body selected with the face used to be a path of its own; it is now the edge the rows are stepped in from (a line drawn on a plane is still the path). **Limits:** a round face's outline is the rectangle of its extents, so a wall trimmed any other way (a slanted cut, a hole in it) is followed as the rectangle; the stack and the checks are as before; the corner rules apply to the rectangle's corners.

## Defaults, the look, moving a hole, and the wireframe (`in_965`)
**Asked (`in_965`) and built:** (1) the tool starts as a **countersunk** hole. (2) An **inset** too big for a small face goes **smaller** instead of failing (half, and half again, down to none; the panel's line says what it was cut down to); a round wall that goes all the way round does the same with the wall's height. (3) The preview shows the holes **as a part looks in wireframe**: the surfaces (each hole's side view turned round its axis, a nut pocket's walls) nearly clear and dark (opacity .14, the colour of a wireframe part) with the thin charcoal edges (`ToolpathView.surfaces`, `holepreview.ts: holeSurfaces`). (4) **A hole of the preview can be picked up and moved** with the pointer: press on a hole (within 16 px of its place on the surface), drag, release (`interaction/holemove.ts`; `main.ts` takes the pointer events that are for a hole before the view does); the moved hole keeps its place (`ReceiverValues.moved`) and is cut there, until the rules that make the row change (spacing, number, ends, corners, inset, shift, line, rows) or another surface is selected, when the row is made again. A round face brings a moved hole back onto itself straight out from the axis. **Not built:** moving a hole by typing numbers, deleting a single hole, moving a hole across to another face. (5) The **wireframe stays on until it is taken out** (the menu's Wireframe): a right click used to put it away (`in_394`); now a right click opens the menu with it still on. The cross section is still put away by a right click.

## Automatic choice, and precise moves through points (`in_980`)
**Asked (`in_980`):** (1) a fastener's position can be moved with precision, by the existing xyz move, rotate or orb tools; (2) the tool picks a reasonable preset for the conditions: an inset neither so big that fasteners overlap nor so small that they break through the edge, the centre line when there is really no space, and more or fewer fasteners when they are cramped.
**Answer to (1):** the previewed holes are not parts, so the move tools cannot take hold of them; rather than teach every tool about them, **Make points** (a button in the panel, flat faces) makes a **point (a corner) at each hole** on the face and selects them with the face; they are parts like any other, so every tool that moves a point moves them (drag, the arrows, typed numbers), and the tool follows the points selected as places picked (an existing rule). The pointer drag of a preview hole (`in_965`) stays for a rough move. Not built for a round face.
**Built for (2): *Line: automatic* is now the default** (`interaction/holerows.ts: autoRows`; the other lines stay as choices). The rules, **the numbers of which are mine and not from a source: open for the human to change**: the head's radius is the widest of the head (a standing screw's), the countersink, the counterbore, the nut pocket and the hole; the **inset** is the one asked for (the box, 10 by default) but **at least the head's radius + 1.5 mm** (so the head does not break through the edge) and **at most 85% of the room the face has** to step in (so the row is not a loop with no room); the **spacing** asked is made **wide enough that two heads are at least 2 mm apart** (2 x radius + 2 mm between centres), by taking the next of a few wider spacings, or by one fewer hole at a time when a number is asked; a hole at every corner is kept when it fits and left out when it does not; **no room for a loop** (fewer than two holes, or the room under the least inset): **one line along the middle** (the centre line method); a post's wall: a loop at each end, or one round the middle when it is too short. The panel's line says what was chosen ("Automatic: edges stepped in 10 mm, 6 holes, about 20 mm apart"). **Not built:** adding holes when they are too far apart (the spacing asked is the most; a number asked is kept when it fits).

## The screw is too long rather than too short (`in_982`)
**Asked (`in_982`):** go with fasteners that are too long rather than too short when it is needed to fit the hole. **Built:** for a hole open at its far end (right through everything in the way) the inserted screw is the **shortest standard length that reaches** the far end (30 mm for a 29.6 mm hole); for a **blind** hole (a depth typed that stops short, or a drilled and tapped pilot) it is still the longest standard length that fits (a screw longer than a blind hole has nowhere to go), and a typed length longer than a blind hole is refused as before; in a through hole a typed length that is too long is allowed. The panel's line says which rule gave the length.

## The depth is chosen for the part (`in_984`)
**Asked (`in_984`):** on curves the holes (long bolts all leaning toward the centre) crowd each other; limit their depth; and do not default to through at all: a certain screw length, or through if through is not too far. **Built:** an **empty depth** is chosen (`hardwareOf` in `interaction/addpart.ts`): **right through** when everything in the way is no more than **1.5 times a screw length** (a screw length = the shortest standard length at least **6 diameters**: M3 20 mm, M4 25, M5 30, M6 40, M8 50: *my numbers, not from a source: for review*), else **that screw length, blind**; the **far nut** still needs the hole right through; a hole to tap keeps its rule (the body's thickness and a pilot); a typed depth is kept as typed. **On a round face** the depth is also kept below the depth at which two holes' axes come within **a hole and a 1.5 mm wall** of one another (`meetLimit`: the holes lean toward the middle, so deep ones meet), but not below the head's recess + one diameter; when the heads leave no room for that the panel says they are too close and to spread them out. Holes side by side (a flat face, a row along the axis) never meet and are not limited. The panel's line says how the depth was chosen ("Depth: 20 mm deep (a 20 mm screw; through would be 56.4 mm)").
