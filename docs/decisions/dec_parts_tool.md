# Hardware tools in the model view: a small helper bar, the selection as the clicks, a live preview

**Accepted as proposed by the AI from `in_879`, with the human's choices (`in_880`: Fastener Holes first; `in_881`: the hardware dialogs are replaced by the panel and the preview, Path repeat keeps its dialog for now).** The human asked that choosing hardware in the menu return to the normal CAD screen, where there are options, where clicking on surfaces shows what would be done, and where changing a setting updates the preview.

1. **Choosing the tool.** Shape, Models, **Fastener Holes** (and later **Fasteners**) arms a tool; no dialog opens and the view stays as it is. A **panel** stands still on the left of the page (as the gear box does, `in_685`) with the settings; the page's own selection is how surfaces are chosen: the face, with Shift the corners, circles and lines that place the holes (as before, `in_707`, `in_813`). Nothing new to learn: the selection is the click.
2. **The panel** shows the same fields as the dialog had (the receiver cards with their cross-sections, size and clearance chips, where the holes go, the path rules, the depth, the thread engagement), made from the same descriptions (`receiverDialog`), so they cannot differ; a button **Cut** (Enter does the same), **Close** (Esc does the same). While no flat face is selected it says so and what to select.
3. **The preview** is drawn in the model view, over the face, from the numbers: for each place the outline of what would be cut as the cross-section of the receiver turned to four sides and as rings (the hole's circle, the countersink's, the counterbore's, a nut pocket's hexagon), down to the depth (a hole right through shows to the far side of the body). It is redrawn as the selection or any setting changes, with no kernel. It reuses the view's toolpath overlay, so nothing new is drawn by the renderer.
4. **Committing.** Cut makes the holes as one command (one undo, as now) and the tool stays on with the selection kept, so another set can be made at once; Close leaves. A refusal (a number that is not one, too many holes, an offset too big) is said in the panel and shows no preview.
5. **Order:** (a) the panel from the field descriptions and the Fastener Holes tool with its preview; (b) the **Fasteners** (the parts) the same way, a ghost of the part placed on the surface or in a hole that is clicked; (c) Path repeat later if the human wants it.

## Changed by the human (`in_882`, `in_883`, `in_884`): a small bar, not a panel

The first build (below) was a large panel on the left. **The human said it should be smaller**: choose the hardware, the window goes away, and a **little helper bar** stands at the top like the one for a fillet, with a few options: select a **line** and the holes are traced along it with a separation and a division method; select a **face** and the centre line of the cut path (the face's outline moved in) is shown, and an **inset** changes it; different patterns across the surface. **Chosen (`in_883`, `in_884`): the numbers are small boxes in the bar that are typed in; the first pattern for a face is the outline moved in by the inset.** **Built:** `ui/holebar.ts` (the pill, in the style of the operation bar: the receiver and the size to choose, a spacing by a distance (*Every*) or a number (*Number*), the inset and the corner rule for a face, the end rule for a line, a depth, a check and a cross; a line under it with how many holes and how far apart, or why none; the numbers answer as they are typed) replaces the panel (`ui/optionspanel.ts` is gone; `ui/fields.ts` stays, shared by the dialogs); `main.ts` reads what the selection says (**a line selected: along it, with no corner rule; a face: along its outline moved in by the inset; corners or circles picked: at them; nothing yet: it says what to select**); a **line drawn on a flat face is enough on its own** (it stands for that face, `selectedFace`); the preview now also draws **the path the holes follow as a dashed pale line** (`guideLines`, the outline moved in, or the line) beside the holes in red, through the same overlay. **Tests:** `holepreview.test.ts` (10): the guide of an outline (the outline moved in by the inset, on the face), the guide following a different inset, the dashes, a line alone as the face, the spacing; and a browser pass (the bar over a block, the face clicked: *along the outline*, six holes and the dashed line drawn; the inset typed as 12: the holes moved in, 16 mm apart; Enter cut them; an inset that is too big said why, in the bar). **Not built:** a grid or two rows of holes across a face (the human asked for the outline first), dragging the inset in the view, the Fasteners (the parts) in a bar, a depth for each kind by default.

## Built first (`in_880`): the panel (replaced by the bar above)

**Shape, Models, Fastener Holes** now opens the **panel** on the left (always enabled: it says what to select) and leaves the view as it is; **the dialog is gone** for it. The panel is `ui/optionspanel.ts`, drawn from the same field descriptions as the dialog (`receiverDialog`) by the shared field drawing `ui/fields.ts` (taken out of `ui/dialog.ts`, which uses it too): receiver cards with their cross-sections (in as many columns as the panel holds), size and clearance chips, the picture, where the holes go, the path rules, depth, thread engagement; a number typed answers **as it is typed** (the preview follows each key); the cursor stays in the box. **The selection is the click:** the face, and with Shift corners, circles and lines; *where the holes go* follows the selection (picked places, else lines, else the middle) until a place is chosen in the panel. **The preview** (`interaction/holepreview.ts`, no kernel): for each hole the section of the receiver on four sides and a ring for each step (the hole, the cone of a countersink, a counterbore, a nut pocket's hexagon at the face and at its floor), a hole right through drawn to the far side of the body, a typed depth to that depth; as lines in the toolpath's form so **the view's existing overlay draws it** (`setToolpath`). **Cut** (the button, or Enter) makes the holes as one command (one undo) and the tool **stays on** with the selection; **Close** or Esc leaves (Esc is taken by the tool first, so it does not clear the selection). **Tests:** `holepreview.test.ts` (6): the lines of one hole (two rings and the section: 76 lines), the depth drawn to the body's far side and never past it, the extra rings of a countersink, a counterbore and a nut pocket's hexagons, a row along the outline (one set for each hole), the preview following the size and no preview with no place, the plan's refusals; `receiverPlan` now makes the places for the cut and the preview alike; and a browser pass (a block opened from a file, the tool opened from the ring with nothing selected, the face clicked: the hole drawn through the block; the countersunk card, M6, *along the face's outline*, a distance typed: six holes drawn as the number changed; Cut: the holes made). **Not built:** the **Fasteners** (the parts) in the panel ((b), their dialog stays for now), Path repeat in the panel ((c), only if asked), a hover preview of the hole under the pointer before a click, the preview cleared after a cut (it still shows over the holes just made, until the selection changes), touch layouts for the panel.

## Rationale

The dialog had to be closed to see the model, so every setting was a guess followed by a cut and an undo. The preview is cheap to draw from numbers, the descriptions of the settings already exist, and the selection already says which face and which places, so the tool needs no new way to click. Drawing the preview with the existing overlay keeps the renderer as it is.

## Far-side nut, shift (`in_909`)
**Built:** a **Nut** choice in the bar (*none* / *pocket on the other side*) cuts a hexagonal nut pocket on the far face at every place, at the same time as the near-side head profile (clearance, countersunk or counterbored): head on one side, nut on the other (`farNutSteps`, `ReceiverPlan.far`; refused for a tapped hole or a nut pocket, and for a typed depth, since it needs the hole right through; the preview draws the far hexagon too). A **Shift** box walks every place along the path: on a line the whole row moves (places past an end are dropped, with a refusal if all are), on a closed outline the places walk round it (`shift` in the path rules, number `pr9`; older records without it read as 0). **Hardware is changed in the bar:** Type and Size selects. **Not built:** curved surfaces (asked in `in_909`; what is meant (radial holes through a curved wall, a path wrapped on a curved face) is to be asked).

## One hardware tool: drill, tap, both, and insert (`in_910`, `in_911`)
**Asked (`in_910`) and answered (`in_911`):** a hole can be **drilled**, **tapped**, or **both** (a clearance hole to a depth, then the tapped hole past it: *chosen*), and the tool is the **one hardware tool**: it cuts the holes and can also put the hardware models into them, **Insert off by default**, a toggle to turn it on; Insert puts in the **screw plus the optional washer and nut** (*chosen*); **Fastener Holes becomes the one bar and the Fasteners menu entry goes away** (*chosen*).
**Built:** Type **Drill + tap** (`ReceiverKind` `drillTap`: the clearance hole to *Drill to* mm, then the tap drill on to *Depth*; *Drill to* empty means the tapped part is 1.5 diameters deep, a rule of mine, not read from a source: open for the human). **Insert** (*no* / *yes: put the hardware in*) makes, after every hole is cut (a cut works on the last body, which a screw must not be), a screw as its **own body** at each place: a countersunk screw in a countersunk hole, a socket head cap screw in the others (head in the counterbore, or standing on the face for a plain, tapped or drill + tap hole), with its hexagon socket, of the **longest standard length** (ISO 888 list) that fits the hole, or the **Length** typed (refused if longer than the hole); with **Nut** on the far side its nut is inserted in the pocket, and a **nut pocket** on this side gets its nut (its screw would come from the other side, so no screw). The sizes are the same nominal ones as the loose parts. The *Fasteners* entry and its dialog are gone from the ring (`models:fasteners`); the part builders it used stay as the code the tests use.
**Not built:** a washer under the head (no washer pocket or seat is cut for it yet; asked in `in_911` as "optional"), a screw from the far side into a nut pocket, thread drawn on the screw or the hole (the tapped hole is the tap drill and the screw's shank is the major diameter, so they overlap as a thread does), a smooth head option in the bar, a preview of the inserted screws.

## Menu place and look (`in_912`)
The tool is no longer a model: it sits **directly under Shape** with the **screw icon**, named **Fastener tool** (`tool:fasteners`); the Models group is gone from the ring. The preview is a **wireframe** (a `wire` layer of the toolpath overlay, charcoal like a body's edges, 1.3 px) instead of the red cut lines; the dashed guide stays pale grey. A try, as asked: say if it should change (a colour, or dashed hidden parts).
