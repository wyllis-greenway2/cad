# Gear assemblies as models: a gearbox, a bearing, a differential and a cycloidal drive

**Asked** (`in_1100`) which gear assemblies to offer after the planetary set, and **approved** (`in_1105`): "build all those things. Lets put them under shape fabrication models": the four recommended (a reduction gearbox, a ball bearing, a
differential, a cycloidal drive; not a multi-speed transmission), under Shape, Fabricate, Models (where the Geneva plate already is).

**How they are made.** Not records that keep their numbers (as the planetary set is), but **models**: a **placement tool** (`in_1129`: "work more like the gear menu", approved with "offer both overall size and the numbers") whose first click is the middle of the model on a surface, and a simple picture of it (the outlines in the plane, red when its numbers make none) follows while the box takes its numbers; the model is then made as ordinary bodies, a part at a time. The gears in a model are gear records (`gear` commands: editable and meshable as any gear); everything else (rods, plates, races, balls, discs, pins) is a plain body made by extruding or turning drawn figures. The pieces are separate bodies so each can be moved, printed or edited; rooms are numbers, with defaults that print (`prin_manufacture_agnostic`).
- **The tool** (`interaction/modeltool.ts`; the `model` tool kind in `tools.ts`, the ring items `model:*` in `control/pointer.ts`, begun in `modal.ts`): armed, the pointer picks a surface as the gear tool does; the first click sets the middle and the surface's frame (the plane's two directions and its normal; the model is made in its own frame and put there: `Place`, `modelparts.ts`, so it stands on any plane, turned about its normal); the pointer's distance from the middle sets the **overall size** until a click, the box then stays open; the next click, Enter or Create makes it, Esc puts it away.
- **The box** (the gear box's panel): **Size** (the way across the longest side, mm), **Turn** (degrees), then the model's own numbers. A number typed is kept; **every length not typed is grown or shrunk by the one factor that gives the size** (found by halving, `modelkinds.ts` `modelNumbers`), so a person can say how big or can say what the numbers are, and say both. Counts and the rooms left for the printer (fit, gap, play, room) do not scale. The box says the model's ratio, or in red why the numbers make none.
- **One list** (`operation/modelkinds.ts`): for each model its numbers (name, hint, whether it scales), usual values, problem, longest extent, middle, steps and the outline for the picture; the tool knows no model by name.
- **A part at a time** (`in_1129`): a model's steps are in parts, bottom up, each with a name (`Builder.part`, `ModelBuild`); Create makes the first part at once and each next one when the kernel has answered for the one before (the intent `buildNext`, `AppState.building`, driven from the frame loop in `main.ts`), so the page draws the parts going in, and the notice says "Reduction gearbox: Stage 1 gear (6 of 9)". Measured: the part by part build costs about a quarter more in the kernel (9.5 s against 7.5 s for the gearbox in Node; 9.9 against 9.1 s in the browser) because the kernel keeps the earlier steps' answers. Esc stops it and keeps what is made. **Undo takes one part at a time.**
- **Play for each** (`in_1129`; `operation/modelplay.ts`, `gearcouple.ts` `modelPlay`, `motion.ts`): when the last part is made, a record `Scene.models` (the command `modelPlay`, saved with the project) keeps how the model moves, in the world where it stands (the driver part with its axis; each part that follows, its axis, its ratio and, for a part that also goes round, the orbit: the same `Follower` the planetary carrier uses) and the step (or gear) that made each part's body. Play from any body of the model (the ring's Move, Play) plays the whole from its driver; the notice says what moves and what is held. **Gearbox:** the first pinion drives; each stage turns the other way by the teeth, the gear of a stage and the pinion of the next turn together, the rods with their gears. **Bearing:** the inner race drives, the outer race is held, each ball goes round at `(pitch - r) / (2 pitch)` of the race's turn (a sphere's own spin does not show). **Differential:** the cage drives with axle 2 held: the cage's caps, pin and drive gear turn with it, axle 1 and its side gear twice as fast (the axles' turns add up to twice the cage's), and each spider orbits with the cage and spins on the pin by `side / spider` of the cage's turn the signs the no-slip condition at the bevel pair's contact line gives (tested). **Cycloidal drive:** the shaft drives, the disc goes round on its eccentric (a spin about its own middle by `-N/(N-1)`, then the orbit, which turns it by one more: it ends turned `-1/(N-1)`), the output plate turns `-1/(N-1)`, the base is held. Kinematic only; a model's pose cannot be kept (it is one picture). Not tried: a model whose bodies were moved or deleted after it was made (parts that are gone are left out).
- A bevel gear's body is named by the loft that began it (a later `combine` cuts it), so `bodyOfGear` does not find it; the model's record finds it by the first step that began it.

**The four.**
- **Reduction gearbox** (`modelgearbox.ts`): 1 to 4 stages of a spur pinion and gear (10 or more teeth on the pinion, 1.5 to 6 times a stage), each stage in a layer of its own; the gear of one stage and the pinion of the next share a rod; rods through a base plate with four posts and a top plate (a gap of 0.2 mm under the lid so it stays a separate body). Numbers: stages, pinion and gear teeth, module, gear thickness, rod diameter, the room in a gear's bore and in a plate's hole, plate thickness, layer gap.
- **Ball bearing** (`modelbearing.ts`): an inner and an outer race, each turned with a round groove, and the balls, a body each, free in their grooves. Numbers: bore, outside diameter, width, balls, ball diameter, room round a ball. Default a 608 (8 by 22 by 7 mm, seven 4 mm balls).
- **Differential** (`modeldifferential.ts`): two side gears on the x axis and two spider gears on a cross pin along y (four straight bevel gears made with the gear tool and meshed by `meshWith`, so their teeth stand as the gear tool stands a pair; the second spider is the first turned half a turn about the axle, which needs an even side gear), a tube cage with a hole through its wall for the pin (cut before anything else is made, so it cuts the cage alone), two caps 0.2 mm off the tube, two axles that end before the pin, and a spur drive gear round the cage (0 teeth for none) that also keeps the pin in. The cage's size comes from the gears' blanks (`bevelLayout`: the back cone stands behind the big end's plane). Numbers: side and spider teeth, module, axle and pin diameters, rooms, wall, cap, the drive's teeth, module and thickness. Default 16 / 10 teeth at module 2.
- **Cycloidal drive** (`modelcycloidal.ts`): a base plate with N ring pins (one body), the disc with N - 1 lobes, the input shaft with its eccentric (joined), and an output plate with pins through holes in the disc (one body). The disc's outline is the curve the pins' centres trace on it, `R e^(it) - E e^(iNt)`, taken in by the pin's radius and the room (derived from the rolling, checked: the disc clears every pin at 90 turns of the input, the least room being the room set). Refused: the eccentricity at or over R / N (the outline loops), pins too big for the lobes' curvature, an eccentric too small for the shaft, output holes into the eccentric's hole or through the disc's edge. Default 10 pins, ratio 9 : 1.

**Checked** (`app/test/models.test.ts`, with the kernel): each model has the bodies it should; the gearbox's rods touch no gear or plate; the bearing's balls are 33.5 mm3 each and touch no race; the differential's side gears and spiders share under 8 mm3 each pair and nothing else touches; the cycloidal drive's bodies are all apart and the disc clears every pin at every turn. Made in the real app from the ring (Shape, Fabricate, Models). Times in Node: gearbox 7.5 s, bearing 1 s, differential 18 s, cycloidal 3 s.

**Not built:** a multi-speed manual transmission (shift forks and synchronisers do not print and work); the models do not keep their numbers (change one by making it again); Play (the motion tool) was not tried on them: the gears of a model are linked by `meshWith` as Mesh gear links them, but no mate or pivot is made for the rods or the cage.

## Rationale

The planetary set took a record, a set of editing paths and a long chain of behaviour; a model needs none of that to be useful, and a fastener-style model gets the four assemblies into the person's hands quickly, with every gear still an ordinary gear. A model that
wants to keep its numbers can be made into a record later (the numbers are already one object each). The numbers come from the person, not from a standard, except where a part has one (the 608 size).
