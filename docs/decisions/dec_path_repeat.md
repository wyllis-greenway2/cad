# Path repeat (the "sweep pattern repeat"): copies of a figure, a body or a fastener placed along a line, an edge or the outline of a face

**Accepted by the human as proposed by the AI from `in_840`, with the two choices they made (`in_841`, `in_842`): the name and the order below. Nothing built yet. The rules in 3 and the limits in 7 are the AI's reading and were not each answered: the human may change them.** The human asked for a repeat that follows a line or the edges of a face (by a distance, or equally divided, with an offset), to be built first and then used for fastener holes in a pattern (a distance from the edge, a spacing, work in from the corners, a fastener in the corner with the spacing adjusted, a number equally spaced). The AI's reading: it is all one machine, **a path, rules that turn it into places, and a consumer that does something at each place.**

1. **The path.** A chain of **lines and arcs** drawn on a plane (open, or closed: a rectangle, a circle), a **body's edge or a chain of its edges**, or the **outline of a flat face** (its edges joined as a closed path). Read from the parts each time (`dec_sweep`: the path follows an edit of what it is drawn in; an edge is found again by `dec_stable_references`). A path is walked from one end (an open one) or from a start corner (a closed one); which end or corner is the start can be chosen.
2. **The offset.** The path can be moved sideways by a **distance in its plane** (towards one side or the other; for a face's outline, into the face is positive): "the fasteners 10 mm in from the edge". An offset path of straight sides is the sides moved with their corners mitred; an arc keeps its centre and changes its radius; an offset that would turn a side inside out is refused with the reason.
3. **Where the copies go (the rules).** All of them are numbers of the record, shown as a short form:
   - **Spacing:** a **distance** between copies, or a **count equally divided** (an open path: the first and last copies at its ends; a closed one: the count equally round it).
   - **Ends** (for a distance on an open path): **leave the remainder at the end** (the copies at 0, d, 2d, ...), **fit**: the spacing is adjusted a little so the last copy lands on the end (the count is the nearest whole one, and the adjusted spacing is shown), or **centre**: the copies are centred, the leftover shared between the two ends.
   - **Start inset and end inset:** the first and last copy stand this far from the path's ends (so a path that ends at a block's corner can have its first copy 8 mm along from it).
   - **Corners** (a path with corners: a rectangle's outline, any chain): **ignore them**, or **put a copy at every corner and divide each side between them** (the spacing adjusted on each side so the corners are always taken: "a fastener in the corner even if it means adjusting the spacing a little"), or **work in from the corners** (distance steps counted from each corner towards the middle of the side, the leftover in the middle of the side).
4. **What the copies are.** At each place the machine gives a **frame**: the point, the way the path runs, and the way across it in its plane. A copy **keeps the orientation** of the original by default (as a linear repeat does); a choice, **turn with the path**, turns each copy by the angle the path has turned since the start (for a bolt circle or a row along a curve).
5. **What it repeats (three consumers of one machine).**
   - **A drawn figure** (corners, lines, a circle and their relations), as the linear and circular repeats do (`Scene.patterns`, a record of kind `path`: the figure's parts, the path's refs, the numbers).
   - **A body** (the existing `pattern` step, with a path kind: the copies are joined to it, as the linear repeat's are).
   - **A fastener hole** (`dec_parts_fastener_pilot`): the **Fastener Holes** dialog takes a path and the rules, and cuts a receiver at each place; the copies follow the path if it is edited (the hole is a step made from the record's places).
6. **How it is built, in order (each piece is tested alone and used by the next):** (a) the pure machine: a path in, rules in, frames out (`operation/pathrepeat.ts`, no scene, no kernel); (b) a repeat of a **drawn figure** along a drawn path, with the editing of its numbers; (c) a repeat of a **body** along a path; (d) the **fastener holes** along a path or a face's outline, with the rules. The tool is offered in the Repeat group of the right-click ring, beside Linear and Circular.
7. **Limits of the first slice:** paths of straight lines and circular arcs (a spline path later); a path in one plane (a path in space later); the offset for a path in a plane only; nothing is moved or turned in three dimensions along a twisting path.

## Built (`in_842`, step (a): the machine)

`operation/pathrepeat.ts` (pure, no scene or kernel; `test/pathrepeat.test.ts`, 18 tests): `pathOf` (a polyline with bulges, open or closed), `circlePath`, `lengthOf`, `pointAt`, `cornersOf`, `offsetPath` (the path moved to its left; sides cut or extended to meet, arcs keep their centre; refused when a side turns inside out or a curve is less than the offset), `placeAlong` (the rules below) and `lifted` (a frame put on a plane in space). **Rules as built:** spacing by **distance** or by **count**; for a distance on a path with no corner rule, **ends** `fit` (the default: the nearest whole number of steps, the spacing adjusted to land on the end), `remainder` or `centre`; **insets** from the two ends of an open path (on a closed path the start inset says where the first copy stands); **corners**: `ignore`, `each` (a copy at every corner and each side divided into equal steps no longer than the distance asked) or `inward` (the distance asked in from each corner, the leftover in the middle of the side: it can be shorter than the distance); a corner rule needs a distance, not a count (refused otherwise), and on a path with no corner (a circle) divides it evenly like `fit`; the **offset** is applied first. The left of a counter-clockwise outline is its inside, so a positive offset goes in. **Not built:** a path that is a spline or in space, the face-with-holes choice, everything of (b) to (d): the repeat of a figure, of a body, of a fastener hole, and the tool in the ring.

## Built (`in_843`, step (b): a figure repeated along a path)

**The tool.** In the right-click ring, Repeat group, **Path repeat** (beside Linear and Circular; dimmed with its reason until it can be used). Select the **figure** (a closed drawn shape or a circle: its face) and, with it, the **lines and arcs of the path** (selected lines that are not the figure's own), then press it: a dialog with a **picture of the path and the places** (the first, filled, is the figure itself; a corner is ringed in red) and a line saying how many places, how far apart and how long the path is, redrawn when a number is finished. Its fields: spacing (a distance, or a number equally divided), the end rule (distance only, no corner rule), corners, the two insets (an open path with no corner rule), the offset, whether the copies keep their direction or turn with the path, and which end to count from. **The record** is a `PatternRecord` of kind `path` (`operation/repeat.ts`: `path`, the lines; `params`, nine numbers, `operation/pathrepeat.ts` `numbersOf`), saved with the file; the copies are derived from it as the linear and circular repeats' are (`operation/pathmaps.ts`: the path read from the lines in the figure's plane, one chain with two ends or one closed loop, an arc's bulge from where its middle is; a map for each place). **The first place is where the figure is** (it is the first copy): every other copy is the figure moved as its place along the path has moved from the first, and, with *turn with the path*, turned about it as the way the path runs has turned (so the figure keeps its relation to the path). To change the rules, select any part of the repeat (the figure, a copy, a line of the path) and press Path repeat again: the dialog opens with the rules and **Change** sets them (`setPattern`; refused, as for the other repeats, if a copy that is used would go). The path must be drawn on the **same plane** as the figure. **Tests:** `pathfigure.test.ts` (the selection and its reasons, the copies' places by distance and by count, an offset rectangle, the turn at a corner, changing the rules, the file round trip, the refusals, the dialog and the ring) and the browser: a circle and a bent path opened from a file, the figure and two lines selected by clicks, Repeat then Path repeat, the picture, the rules changed, the repeat made (no page errors). **Found and fixed on the way:** the turn is in degrees in the repeats, not radians. **Not built:** the figure's own place to the path (an automatic offset), a face's outline as the path, moving the copies' preview live in the view, deleting a line of the path (the copies then go until it is drawn again), bodies and fasteners ((c), (d)).

## Built (`in_844`, step (c): a body repeated along a path)

**The same Path repeat item,** with **a body** selected (the ring's Select, Bodies, or a click that selects the whole body) and the **lines or arcs of the path** (all on one plane), instead of a figure: the dialog is titled "Path repeat of a body" and is the same (a picture of the path and the places, the rules, a note that the first place is the body itself). It makes a **step of the history**, a `pattern` step with `mode: "path"` (`path`: the lines; `pr0` to `pr8`: the nine numbers of `numbersOf`; `body`, when it is one of several bodies; `src`, the name the body had, to find it again), like the linear and circular repeats of a body. The places are worked out when the history is compiled (`operation/pathmaps.ts` `bodyPlaces`, called from `compiler/graph.ts`) and the kernel is given them (`mode: "placed"` in the interpreter: a turn about a point, in degrees, then a move, for each place after the first; copies that touch are joined, those that do not are separate bodies). **The body is the first place; each copy is the body moved as the path's place has moved from the first** (as for a figure). **To change the rules,** select the body (the original or any copy) with no path line selected and press Path repeat: the dialog opens with its rules and **Change** sets them in the same step (`setStepParams`); in the History list the row of such a step says to do that, since the linear and circular repeat's own tool cannot show these rules. **Tests:** `pathbody.test.ts` (10) with the kernel: the volumes of the copies (apart, joined at 5 mm, by count, a bent path with the turn), one undo, changing the rules, the places (a straight path, a quarter turn in degrees), the refusals, the dialog and the ring; and a browser pass (a block and a path from a file, the body selected from the ring, the path line added with Shift, the dialog, the repeat made along the line). **Not built:** a path that is a face's outline, the figure-to-path offset done for you, a live preview, fasteners ((d)). **Limits:** at most 200 copies; the path is read from the scene when the history is compiled, so moving the sketch of a path with a later move step is not followed (the path stays where it is drawn).

## Built (`in_845`, step (d): fastener holes along a path or a face's outline)

**Fastener Holes** (Shape, Models) has a **Where the holes go** choice: the **middle of the face**, the **places picked** (corners and circles selected with the face, as before), **along the face's outline** and **along the lines selected**. The last two use the path repeat's rules and picture (`pathRuleFields`, shared with the Path repeat dialog; the same numbers: a distance or a number equally divided, the end rule, corners ignored / a hole at every corner with each side divided / worked in from the corners, the insets, and the offset, here "move the path in from the edge"): a picture of the face's outline with a dot for each hole (a corner ringed in red) and a line saying how many holes and how far apart. **The outline** of a flat face is read from its boundary edges (`faceEdges`) as a closed path in the face's plane, made counter-clockwise as seen from outside so that **a positive offset goes in**, whatever order the edges come in (`operation/pathmaps.ts` `readFaceOutline`); a face with holes in it follows its outer edge only. **The lines** are any lines or arcs selected with the face that are not circles: drawn on the face (or another plane, taken onto the face along its normal) or edges of the body (they lie in the face). Every hole is cut by the same receiver steps as one hole (`receiverSteps`), the whole row in **one command**: one undo takes it all back; at most 200 holes. The dialog starts at *places picked*, else *lines*, else *middle* (`receiverStartFor`). **Tests:** `pathholes.test.ts` (10, with the kernel): the outline read in either order and with a hole in the face, ten holes with a hole at each corner 10 mm in from the edge of a 100 x 60 face, six equally divided, a row along a line drawn on the face, a row of holes to tap, an edge of the body as the path, the refusals, the dialog and where it starts; and a browser pass (a block opened from a file, the face clicked, Fastener Holes from the ring, *Along the face's outline*, the picture, the holes cut). **Found on the way:** the ring test that lists every item had not been updated for the two new Models items and Path repeat (the full suite caught it); and a default of "middle of the face" in `receiverStart` had switched off the picked places for the old callers: the default is now set only where the selection is known. **Not built:** the hexagon of a nut pocket turned with the path, a face's inner outlines as paths, a hole row on a curved face, the path in space.

## Built (`in_1301`: a body repeated along any line path, in space)

The human asked for a "loft repeat" that follows **any line path**: a lone line in space, the edge of a body, or touching selected lines such as a perimeter or a square of four lines (`in_1300`, `in_1301`; they also asked to check how loft takes its path). The AI's reading: it is the Path repeat of a body, no longer held to lines on one plane, so it is not a new repeat kind. **What was checked:** a loft (`dec_loft_sketches`) takes sketches, not a path, while the sweep (`dec_sweep`) takes a chain of lines, arcs and edges on any plane, anywhere; the path here is read the same way (`walkPath`'s rules: one chain with two ends, plus a closed loop). **Built:** with a body selected, **any lines, arcs or body edges** (not only drawn ones) can be the path. When they lie on one plane the old planar path is used (so the offset still works); otherwise the path is read in space (`operation/spacepath.ts`: `readSpacePath`, pieces from `curvePoints`, so a curved or elliptical edge is followed by its length), laid straight (`rolledOf`: `Path.rolled`, `Seg.corner` in `pathrepeat.ts`) so the same rules place the copies (distance, count, ends, insets, corners, shift), and each place is taken back into space (`spacePlaces`, called from `bodyPlaces`; the kernel needs no change since a place already carries a turn about any direction). *Turn with the path* is the shortest turn from the way the path ran at the first place. The offset is refused for a path not on one plane. **Tests:** `spacepath.test.ts` (6: a closed square of four edges, a copy at every corner, the turn, a curved edge, the refusals, `bodyPlaces`). **Not built:** a figure along a path off its plane (a drawn figure stays on its plane), a spline drawn line as the path, the offset in space, a picture of the path in its true shape (the dialog draws it laid straight), a browser look.

## Settled and still open
- **The name (`in_841`):** **Path repeat**, in the Repeat group of the ring (Sweep is already a solid operation).
- **The order (`in_842`):** the pure machine, then a drawn figure, then a body, then fasteners.
- **Still the AI's default, not asked: a path that is the outline of a face with holes** (a plate with a hole in it): the outer outline only by default; the others by choice.

## Rationale

A fastener pattern, a repeat along a curve and a bolt circle are the same thing: places along a path. Building the machine once, as pure arithmetic on a path, lets the figure, the body and the fastener each use it and keeps the rules (spacing, corners, insets, offset) in one place that can be tested without a kernel. Following the repeats the program already has (a record with numbers, copies derived and named stably) means a path repeat edits, saves and undoes like them. The name avoids the word the program already uses for a different thing.

**A count with a hole at every corner (`in_945`):** the *every corner* rule also works with a count: the count is **all** the copies, the corners (every corner of a closed path; the two ends and the corners of an open one) are some of them, and the **rest are shared out between the sides by the length of each side** (the largest remainders get the odd ones), each side's own copies spaced evenly; fewer copies than corners is refused ("the number must be at least that"). *Work in from each corner* still needs a distance.
