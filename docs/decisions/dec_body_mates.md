# Mates: two faces of two bodies brought together

The human approved (`in_314`, after `in_306` and `in_307`: an assembly is just a model whose bodies are in relationships) starting on constraints between bodies. This is the first slice, a **placement mate**: select a face of one body, then a face of another; the first one's body is **moved and turned** to meet the second (the one selected first moves, as with sketch constraints, `in_229`).

- **Mate: flush faces** (two flat faces): the first face is turned to face the other (its normal opposite) by the shortest turn, and moved along the other's normal until the planes meet; it slides freely in the plane (nothing about where along the face is decided).
- **Mate: coaxial** (two round faces, a post or a hole): the first axis is turned parallel to the other's (the nearer way) and moved across onto it; its place along the axis is kept.
- Offered in the menu for the two faces (`actions.ts`), refused with the reason for faces of one body or the wrong kinds (`mate.ts` `whyNotMate`).
- The motion is worked out in `mate.ts` from where the faces are now and written as **ordinary steps**: a turn about X, one about Y, one about Z (the angles that make the turn, about a point on the face or the axis), then a move by a vector, each naming the body, with the body's own sketches carried by the same motion (`carrySketches`, as for any move of a body). So a mate undoes in one undo, saves, opens and replays like any other, with no new kernel operation.

**Checked, and put back on request (`in_315`, option 2 of the former `q_mate_persistence`).** Each mate is also **recorded** as a relation of kind `mate` (`operation/mates.ts`): it names the two bodies and keeps, in numbers, how each face stands when the mate is true (its direction, a point on it, a round face's radius) and the gap. The faces are found again by those, as anchors are: the face of that body with the same way round, nearest to the remembered one (within eight degrees and 10 mm; else the mate is *lost*). After every change `mateStatus` says whether each mate is **ok** (the faces touch or share an axis within a thousandth of a millimetre), **off** (found, but apart or turned) or **lost** (a body or a face is gone). A change that newly breaks a mate says so in the message (the change stays), and the **⋯ menu** has "Put the mates back" while any is off: it makes each one again from where the faces are now, as new turns and moves, one at a time (a body moved by one mate may be what the next rests on), and records it again with the same id. A mate of a body that is deleted goes with it. It is **not automatic**: nothing moves unless the user asks.

**A gap that can be typed, and parallel mates (`in_317`).** *Flush* has a number, the gap the faces are left at (mm, 0 or more; positive is apart, along the other face's outward normal): with two faces selected a number stands over them showing how far apart the first face is from the other's plane now, and typing one makes the mate with that gap (the menu's "Mate: flush faces" is a gap of 0); it is recorded and checked at that gap, and made again between the same two bodies it replaces the mate there (one record, `existingMate`). Two new mates **only turn** the first body and leave it where it is (a point of the face or the axis stays put): **Mate: parallel faces** (flat faces: the normals made parallel, the nearer way round, either facing) and **Mate: parallel axes** (round faces: the axes made parallel). They are checked by the angle left (degrees) and, being about a turn, find their faces again within forty-five degrees. A mate that needs no motion (the faces meet already) is only recorded.

**Perpendicular and angle mates (`in_318`).** Four more mates that only turn the first body (it stays where it is): **perpendicular** (faces, and axes of round faces) turns it until it is at a right angle to the other, and **angle between faces / between axes** turns it until it is at the **typed angle** (degrees, 0 to 90, kept in the record's `gap`; 0 is parallel, 90 perpendicular). The angle is the one between the two **as lines** (the planes of the faces, or the axes): the turn is the smaller one, about the line across both, toward the other when the angle is to be less and away when more (`turnToAngle`); a face has no "right way round" for these. Checked by the angle off (degrees) and found again within forty-five degrees. Two selected faces show **both numbers** over them, the gap and the angle (`mateMeasures` in `annotations.ts`; `Annotation.measureId` says which action a typed value is for), each typed number makes that mate (the intent `relation measure` carries the action's id).

**A number for coaxial, and a distance for parallel faces (`in_319`).** *Coaxial* has a number, how far apart the two axes are left (mm; 0, the default, is one axis): the axes are made parallel and moved across until they are that far apart, the way the first one stands off from the other's axis now (any way when it is on it), keeping its place along the axis; the number over two round faces shows the distance now. *Parallel faces* has an **optional** distance: the menu's row, with no number, only turns (as before); a distance typed turns the body and leaves the two planes that far apart on the side it is on now. The record keeps whether the number counts (`fix`: always, except a parallel mate that was given none); checked in mm (the planes' distance, or the axes') once the turn is right, else in degrees. *Parallel axes* stays turn-only: with a distance it would be the same as a coaxial one with a number.

**The numbers over two faces, told apart (`in_320`).** Seen in a browser, the gap, the distance and the angle stood in a row, each only "0" with a padlock, so nothing said which was which. Each now has a **word before the number** (gap, distance, offset, angle), the **unit after** it, and the mate's link mark in place of the padlock (a mate's number is not locked: the mark makes the mate at the number shown, and a typed number changes it); and they stand **one under the other** in a column, each a step lower than the one before (38 px, so they are not laid side by side), each with the thin line back to the spot they belong to (`Annotation.tag`, `Annotation.stack`; `ui/bubbles.ts`). The stack is on the screen only: they all belong to the same place in the model.

**Marked on the model (`in_316`).** Each mate has a small round mark on the model where the two bodies meet (the middle of the first face, `Frame.mates`; `ui/matemarks.ts`): **teal with a link** when it is true, **amber with a bang** when it is not (its title says how far off, and a click puts that mate back: `mate:reapply:<id>`), **grey with a query** when a body or a face is gone. A click on any other mark selects the mate's two bodies (`mate:select:<id>`). The title is also what a held press shows on a phone.

**Found and fixed on the way (browser check):** a saved file with a relation to a part of the body could not be opened in the browser: the replay runs before the kernel's thread has answered, so the body is not there to be named, and `addRelation` refused it. A relation may now name a part of the body (`b/...`) that is not there yet, and the description of such a relation (the anchors, the mate's numbers) is put into the command when it is made (`describedCommand`, `session.run`), so the file carries it and the replay needs no geometry. This also holds for the relations to edges, corners and faces of `dec_anchor_geometry`, which would not have opened in the browser either.

Not a standing constraint (bodies do not follow their mates by themselves): that is the open `q_mate_persistence` (option 3). Not built: a flush that also lines up an edge, an angle of more than 90 degrees (a face that must face the other way round), the angle between a flat face and an axis, pivots and slides (the mates that leave freedom), a body that is fixed in place, snapping suggestions while a body is dragged.

## Rationale

A placement mate needs no new subsystem: the kernel already moves and turns one body of several, the sketches follow, and the history already replays. It gives the assembly user the most used action (put this face on that face, this pin in that hole) at once, and its steps are the ones a standing mate would later keep correcting, so nothing built here is thrown away.

**Axis perpendicular to a face (`in_325`, AI's reading, for the human to correct).** A flat face and a round face's axis can be mated so the axis stands at a right angle to the face (along its normal): a pin upright on a plate. The mode is `axisToFace`; the first face's body is only turned (nothing is moved), either face may be first, and the pair must be one flat and one round face. It is the turn-only family (`in_317`): checked, standing (`in_321`), put back. Not built: a mixed mate with a typed angle, or a parallel one (an axis lying in a face's plane), and the pin is not brought onto the face (use coaxial with a hole, or move it).

**Angles beyond 90 (`in_326`, AI's reading, for the human to correct).** The typed angle of an angle mate (faces or axes) now runs 0 to 180. Up to 90 it is as before, between the planes or axes as lines. Past 90 it is between the directions themselves (a flat face's outward normal, a round face's axis direction), so 120 leans the faces away from each other and 100 is not 80. Cost to know: a round face's axis has no natural sign, so for axes past 90 the direction is the one the kernel gives the face, which may flip if a body is turned half way round (the mate then reads as off and putting it back turns it again). The number shown on the face pair (the measure) is still the angle as lines.

**The column of numbers goes up too (`in_327`, AI's reading).** The numbers over a pair of faces (`in_320`) stand one below the other; when the column would run off the bottom of the page (its last bubble past the bottom) and there is room above, it goes up instead (`stackOffset` in `bubbleplace.ts`). Chosen by where the column begins, not by a setting. Still avoids lines and other bubbles through the usual placing.

**Mates are shown like other constraints (`in_338`, human direction).** Two selected faces show no numbers any more (no gap, distance, angle, offset): the menu offers the mates as plain rows that make the mate at what the faces are (angle rows at the angle they have now; flush at a gap of 0; coaxial at no offset). A mate is a constraint bubble like any other: it has its own icon (one per kind, `ui/icons.ts` `mate:<kind>`, not the measure stick), shows its number when it has one (flush gap, coaxial offset, parallel distance, angle), is typed over on the bubble (`setMateValue` makes the mate again at the number), turns red when it is not true (a click on it puts it back), is taken away by the cross, and is shown **only while a body or a face it joins is selected**. The always-on marks on the model are gone. A refused change flashes the mates it would have broken red (`annotationsOf` includes them). **Tape measure (`in_338`).** A toggle (`Tape measure` in the ⋯ menu; `tape:measure`; Escape puts it away first): while on, the selected face (or two) shows read-only bubbles with the measuring stick icon: two flat faces their angle and, when parallel, their distance; two round faces the angle and offset of the axes; a flat and a round face the angle of the axis to the face; one flat face its area (mm²); one round face its radius and diameter (`interaction/tape.ts`). **Mates hold during motion (`in_338`).** A body that is dragged (free drag, a move arrow, a turn ring) is drawn where its standing mates leave it free: the pose nearest to the one the pointer asks for at which every mate of the body holds, the other bodies where they are (`solveDriven` in `operation/matesolve.ts`, `constrainedMotion` in `interaction/mate.ts`, used by the picture of the drag in `frame.ts`). The mates that held it back are red while it is held (their bubbles are shown red even though they are not selected), as a refused sketch change shows what it would have broken. On release the raw move is made and the standing mates put it where the preview was, in the same step. Only standing mates hold (a mate made placed-once is only checked). Followers are not moved in the picture: a body mated to the one dragged catches up on release.

**Why a body could be pulled off its mate (`in_339`, found in the human's file).** A mate finds its faces again by how they look (the way they face and where they were). A face was accepted only if it was within about 10 mm of where the mate remembered it, so a body pulled 16 mm off its flush mate had a mate that read as *lost*, and a lost mate holds nothing: the standing correction and the live drag skipped it. A face is now also accepted when it is the only face of the body that faces that way, however far the body has been pulled (`findFace`); where several face that way the nearest is taken, within 10 mm as before. The human's file is kept as a test fixture (`body-pulled-off-in_338.json`): its mate reads off by 16.685 mm, and the standing correction puts the body back. Not done: opening a file does not correct mates that are already off (they are corrected with the next change).

**A release makes only the held pose (`in_340`).** Releasing a drag (free drag, a move arrow, a turn ring) of a body whose standing mates hold it back makes the pose they leave as its own steps (`heldCommand` in `interaction/mate.ts`, used by `runOp`), one history entry, not the move asked for followed by a correction. When the mates leave it nowhere to go that way nothing is made and the message says so. A move no mate holds back is made as asked. Movement of under a millionth of a radian or ten nanometres is treated as none (the solver's own rounding). Checked in Chromium: dragging B along its face's normal and sideways ended with one step (`s_041b0m`) and B against A, slid along it. The correction after a change that is not a drag (a face widened, a number typed) is still made with the change, as before.

**Followers move in the preview (`in_341`).** While a body is dragged, the bodies that its standing mates take along are drawn moving too, each by its own matrix (`Frame.followers`; the view moves each body of the mesh on its own, `ui/view/content.ts` `split` takes a list). The solver (`solveDriven`) treats every body that is the first of a mate as free, the others (a body only mated to, one of a set that only move each other) as fixed, the dragged body as free but pulled toward where the pointer is, and takes the smallest move for the followers, so what a mate leaves free (a face sliding on a face) is not copied: a body flush against a dragged one follows along the normal and stays where it is across it. Taking the followers into the dragged body's pose is how a body is held when it is the one others hang on; a body squeezed between two that stay cannot be taken along, and then the dragged body is held by its own mates alone and nothing follows. Checked in Chromium: dragging A, with B mated to it: B went with it along x in the preview and ended where it was drawn after the release. Not done: after the release the picture of the followers is not kept until the kernel's answer comes (they may jump for a moment); followers do not flash.
