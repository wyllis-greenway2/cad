# Cross section: a view of the model cut by a plane

`in_392` asked for a cross-section slice tool to see inside parts: a view, not a cut; x, y and z planes offered at the middle of the part; a click slices to that plane; the plane moved with the xyz or the rotate handles. **The AI's reading, for the human to correct:** View › Cross section offers three faint planes through the middle of the model's box; a click on one makes it the section (the part of the body and of the drawn lines on its positive side is not drawn, both faces of what is cut are shown); its arrows along x, y, z move it and its rings about x, y, z turn it; the same menu row (or Esc while choosing) puts it away. It is state of the view (`AppState.section`), not of the model: no step, no history, not saved in the file, and the kernel's solid, the export and the picking are untouched. **Reworked after `in_394` (again the AI's reading):** it begins at once as one semi-transparent plane through the middle of the part, across the axis the camera looks along, cutting away the half nearer the camera; chips on the plane swap the axis (X, Y, Z), swap the half cut away, and switch the handles between moving (arrows, the default) and turning (rings); where the plane cuts a solid the cut face is drawn solid and hatched with slanting parallel lines (a stencil count along each line of sight, no mesh is cut); a right click, Esc or the menu row puts it away. **After `in_397`:** the half that is cut away stays as a faint ghost; the cut's own edges are anchors for a sketch on the plane (the flat faces it cuts are lines, the round faces circles: the existing anchors of `in_313`, now also offered by the drawing hints, so a corner snaps on a cut edge and is made with an on-line constraint to that face); Look straight at it turns to the plane. **After `in_416` (the AI's reading):** the cut belongs to the plane (`Plane.cut`), not to its being selected: the menu row inserts it cutting, a click on the plane switches its cut off and another on again whether or not it is selected, a right click or Esc only deselects it (the cut stays), and the plane is faint, fainter still while cutting, and hidden by the part where the part is in front of it. **After `in_398`:** the corners where two of the cut's edges meet are anchors too (`anchorCutCorners`): a point near one is taken to it and is held by an on-line constraint to each of the two faces' cuts (a cut corner is no part of its own, so it is named by the faces that meet there). Not built: cut edges of curved faces other than a cylinder along an axis. **After `in_396` (the AI's reading):** the cross section plane is a **part of the model** (a drawn plane with `section: true`, inserted by View › Cross section through the middle of the part and selected alone, the object deselected); the view is cut **while that plane is selected**; deselected it stays as a faint plane in the part (right click or Esc lets it go; it is picked again by clicking it; the menu row with it selected deletes it); it is saved in the file and undone like any step (`addPart`, `placePlane`), and a drag of its handles is one step. The cut face is drawn like a face of the part (hidden by anything in front of it, not drawn through the part), finer gray hatching, a more transparent plane. Because the plane is a real plane, a sketch can be drawn on it with the sketching tools; (the cut's own edges as anchors: see `in_397` above). **After `in_395`:** numbers can be typed (a bubble at each arrow gives where the plane is along that axis, at each ring the angle its normal makes about that axis); the plane is kept in a saved file (a `view.section` key beside the history, `interaction/viewstate.ts`; a file without it opens as before); the part that is cut away is not there to point at (corners, lines, and the surface where the cut part would be hit, `PickOptions.clip`); the chips find a place no bubble covers. (The old text follows.) Not built: a cap (a flat filled face where the plane cuts, so the cut looks solid), typing a position or an angle, keeping the plane across a save, flipping which side is kept, slicing the kernel mesh for picking.

**Treat the cut view as the real object (`in_680`, the human's wish):** *Done:* the teal of a selected face and the ring of the pointer are **cut where the part is** (the orb materials take the sheets as clip planes, `orb.ts` `sectionClip`; the lines and basic materials of the highlight take them too); the **cut face is a little darker** than a face of the part (0.80 against 0.92 grey) and its **hatching is closer** (a line every 1/50 of the body's size, was 1/34); the **plane's picture follows its drag live** (`frame.ts` `sectionPlanes` uses `sectionLive`; the clip and the handles already did, the faint plane was left at where it was taken until the drag ended). *Not done (proposed, below):* the cut face as a real surface.

**The cut face is a real face (`in_681`, the human took the recommendation):** where a sheet that is cutting passes through the body, `operation/cutface.ts` slices the kernel's triangles by the sheet's plane, chains the cut into outlines (one inside another is a hole), keeps what the other sheets leave, fills them (`operation/polygon.ts`: the ear clipping and the hole bridging moved there from `interaction/surfaces.ts`, so the compiler can use them) and `compiler/body.ts` adds, named by where they are like every part of the body, a **flat face** (`b/body/f@nx,ny,nz,d~middle`, a plane part with `cap: true`), its **edges** (`e@a~b~m`) and **corners** (`v@x,y,z`); `compile.ts` gives it the cutting sheets of the scene. So the pointer's ring lights on it, a click selects it, **a sketch tool chosen with it selected draws on a plane made from it** (as for any face of the body), and **constraints to its edges** work: a line drawn from a point on a cut edge got `onLine` with the cut edge and `parallel` and `perpendicular` with the others. *The three choices, as proposed:* (1) the cut face is derived from the scene, so it **updates when the sheet is let go**, not during the drag (the picture of the cut follows the drag live as before); (2) **picking skips the cut-away half** (it already did: `PickOptions.clips`); (3) **a sketch on the cut face lives on the new plane made from it**: moving the sheet afterwards does not move it. *Also:* over the part, a sheet and its own cut face lie in one plane: **the cut face wins** (`picking.ts`; the sheet is reached past the part's edge); **a push / pull, move, fillet or any operation of a cut face is refused with a reason** (`operations.ts` `startOp`): it is a view of the cut, the body is changed through its own faces. *Curved cuts (`in_682`, the human took the recommendation):* runs of points of a cut that lie on one circle (a cylinder, a cone or a sphere cut across its axis, or a round hole) become **arcs**; an outline that is all one circle is **two half arcs that know each other** (`twin`), as the kernel's own rims are, with the circle on them (centre, normal, radius), so a radius or a diameter can be set on it and a point can be put on it. The test is a run of at least five points within 2.5% of the radius (and no less than 0.01 mm: the tessellation leaves the cut a little inside the curve) with no side longer than 0.7 of the radius (a regular pentagon is not a circle); an ellipse (a cylinder cut at a slant) stays a polygon of short sides; the outline is walked from its sharpest corner so no arc starts in the middle. *The limits closed (`in_683`, the human took the recommendation):* **a sheet that passes exactly through a corner, an edge or a face of the body** (as the planes at the origin do, through a symmetric part) cuts cleanly: the plane is taken a hair (0.00002 mm) to the cut-away side for the slicing, which the ten-thousandth of the names does not see; and **a plane that has no material on one side** (it only touches the body or lies in a face of it) makes no cut face. **With several bodies each is cut on its own** (only its own triangles) and its cut face, edges and corners carry `of`, its body. *Not done:* a cut face for a body that is cut by a sheet lying a hair from a face (it may show a sliver of a face beside the real one).

**Sketching on a face (`in_1351`).** While a sketch tool is on and a flat face of the body (or the plane a sketch on one stands on) is the surface being sketched on, that surface cuts the view along itself, the half it looks out to being cut away (`sketchCut` in `control/section.ts`, listed with the sheets in `cutsNow`). It is no part of the model: no sheet is made, and it goes with the tool. Where a cutting plane lies exactly on a face of the part, the triangles of the part in the plane are drawn again over the hatch with the part's own material (`view.ts`), so only what is really cut looks like the cut.

**The temporary cut stays while sketching on a plane, and nothing shows through (`in_1374`).** The cut made for the surface being sketched on (`sketchCut`) stays after the tool is put away while what is selected is drawn on that plane (the figure just made), and while an offset of it is open; it goes when the selection leaves the plane. While any cut is on, the edges that are out of sight (the faint ones behind a face, and those behind the sketch's plane) are not drawn (`syncHidden` in `view.ts`).

**Sketch mode (`in_1375`).** The cut along the plane being sketched on is a state (`AppState.sketchOn`: the plane, its middle, the way it looks out), not a reading of the tool or the selection (which replaces what `in_1374` did). It is taken when the view goes normal to a plane, a face or a sketch (the Look action: a face looks out, any other plane, a world plane too, towards the camera) and when a sketch tool begins on a face or a plane of a body (a world plane does not: the tool draws where it is clicked), and it stays, the tool or no tool, selection or none, until **Exit sketch** (a button in the bar at the top, shown while the state is on; the intent `exitSketch`, which also puts the tool away). It goes too when its plane is gone (undone). It is left, besides by Exit sketch, by the X, Y, Z view buttons at once and by Escape when nothing else is open (Escape lets go in steps: a half-drawn figure, then the tool, then sketch mode, then the selection, `in_1376`); going normal to what is selected with N takes it, as the Look action does. The edges out of sight are not drawn while it is on (`in_1374`).

## Rationale

Recorded because production code needs an entry (`CLAUDE.md` rule 9); the form is the AI's reading and stays provisional.

**Reworked by `dec_sheet_planes` (`in_460`):** the chips, the single cut and the click that switched the cut are gone: a sheet has a mode icon and a move icon, any number of sheets can cut, and the plane is a face like any other.

## Built: what moves is cut like what stands (`in_1130`)
A body that moves on its own (a motion that plays, a body being dragged) is drawn from meshes of its own (`ui/view/content.ts`, `split`), made after the clip was set up, so they were not clipped (their edges went on in the cut-away space) and the passes that count the faces behind the plane, which make the grey hatched cut face, did not include them or follow their matrices (the cut showed through). Now (`ui/view.ts`): when bodies begin or stop moving on their own the clip is set up again (`applyClip`, which also takes the moving meshes), and every frame the counting passes copy the matrices of the meshes they stand for. Seen in the browser on a playing gear and lever; no automated test (it is the drawing).


## Changed: finer, lighter hatch on the cut face (`in_1210`)
The cut face is a mid grey a little lighter than before (0.56, 0.58, 0.63), still darker than a face of the part; its hatch is about two and a half times as many lines (the pitch is a hundred and twentieth of the model, not a fiftieth, never under 0.12 mm), each lighter than before (0.40, 0.42, 0.47) but darker than the surface. Looked at in the browser on a gear and a lever; the numbers are mine, for review (`ui/view.ts`).

## `in_1377`: the cut follows the sketch tool's plane; the cut-view icon
The cut of sketch mode follows the plane of the sketch tool that is on (a tool begun on another surface moves the cut there). An icon (the cross-section one) in the tool chip switches the cut view on and off without leaving sketch mode.

## `in_1378`: one rule for going normal to a surface
Whatever turns the view straight to a surface (a double or triple click on a plane or a face, the Look action of the ring, N, a sketch tool begun on a surface) goes through one function, `turnToPlane` (`control/section.ts`): the camera turns and the view is cut along that surface, on by default; the bar's cut icon switches it off and the choice is kept from one surface to the next. X, Y and Z are views of the world: they leave sketch mode (`in_1376`). The face of the body that lies on the cut is drawn from the body's own solid material, not from whatever the material was when it was made (it had been taken while a mirror made the body wireframe, and stayed dark and see-through).

**What a sketch's cut is of, and how the view is kept (`in_1385`).** (1) The cut of sketch mode is of the model as it was when the view went normal: `sketchOn.known` holds the faces of the bodies then; a face that is there now and was not (an extrusion into the cut-away space) is not cut: `view.ts` draws those faces whole by a mesh of their own (`setUnclipped`, from `frame.unclipped`) and `PickOptions.unclipped` lets them be pointed at. A body that grew keeps its old faces cut; only the faces made since are whole (their edges stay cut; a face that keeps its name and only grew is not seen as new). (2) The edges past the cut showed because letting the sketch plane's own clipping of the edge materials go (`behind(null)`) took the cut's planes with it: the edges are now clipped by the cut's planes in every case (`behind(plane, cuts)`). (3) A button in the bar (the eye) while sketching keeps the view square to the plane (`AppState.keepNormal`): it turns to it at once, turning by hand does nothing while it is on (pan and zoom go), and a plane that takes the sketch is turned to when it does. (4) **X, Y, Z only turn the view** while sketch mode, a tool or a cross section is on: sketch mode, the cut, the tool and the selection stay (this takes back `in_1376`, where they left sketch mode). (5) **A double click on a flat face turns to it** (the one way, `turnToPlane`: normal, cut along it, sketch mode, `in_1378`), and **three take the whole object**; this swaps `in_070`'s rule for flat faces only (the round faces, edges and points keep two for the object and three for turning). With a sketch tool on, a double click on a surface (the first click has put the first point) puts the sketch on that surface and turns to it. (6) The ring's pencil (the Sketch category) begins sketch mode as it opens, on the plane under the right click (selected, square, cut), and the ring stays open on the tools; with none under the click nothing happens (`doRunAction` "sketch:open", `ring.ts` `onOpen`).

**One eye, one pencil, a click grammar (`in_1387`, which replaces what `in_1385` said of the double click).** A click selects the part under it; a **double click selects the body whenever there is one under the click** (from a face, an edge, a point, a figure drawn on its face); a triple click is the part again and, for a surface, goes normal to it (`in_070`'s older rule restored; a double click with a sketch tool on is only a click again). In the ring **the eye is one action** (`view:eye`): with sketch mode on it turns the cut along the plane on and off, otherwise it puts a cross section plane on what is selected (`doPlaneNew`). The other ways of looking that were under it (Look at, Game mode, Hide body, Wireframe, All constraints, Show cuts, Tape measure) are a **View** list in the ⋯ menu (`viewRows`; the actions are the same ids). **The pencil** (the Sketch category) always begins sketch mode as it opens, on the plane under the right click, else the plane or face selected, square to it and with the cut on; with neither it says what to select. The sentence of `in_1387` that was cut off ("get rid of the eye and the") is read as the list under the eye going: to be confirmed.
