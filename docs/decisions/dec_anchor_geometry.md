# Anchors: the body's geometry as read-only references for a sketch

The human approved (`in_308`, after `in_305`) the first step of `q_body_constraints`: a drawn part can be held to the geometry of a body as to any other part, and the body's geometry is read, never moved.

Built: (1) `roundOf` / `relationKind` (`operation/part.ts`): a **circular edge of a body** (a full circle, or an arc: the circle through its ends and its middle) counts as a **circle** wherever relations are chosen, and is marked an *anchor*. (2) The relations table and the annotations take it as a circle, so a drawn circle selected with such an edge is offered Concentric, Equal radius and Tangent (`actions.ts`, `rows.ts`, `annotations.ts`). (3) The solver reads it as a **fixed virtual centre and a fixed radius** at its projection on the plane being solved (`solve.ts` `roundCentre`, `radiusId`): the drawn part moves, the edge does not. (4) The **drawing hints and suggestions** see the same edges, those in the plane of the sketch (`operation/anchors.ts` `anchorRounds`): a circle's centre drawn near an edge's centre goes there and is made concentric with it; a line's end on it, or tangent to it; a selected circle is offered Tangent when it nearly touches an edge and Concentric when their centres are close (`hints.ts`, `suggest.ts`).

**Stable naming (`in_309`, as `dec_stable_references` says: names first, geometry as the fallback).** An edge of the body is named by where it is, so a change of the body (a depth, a size, a chamfer) used to leave a relation to it pointing at nothing. Now a relation to a circular edge of the body also keeps **how the edge looked** when the relation was made (`Relation.anchors`: its centre, radius, which way it faces, and its height in the body's box; made when the relation is added, `commands.ts`); when the compile finds the edge gone (`compile.ts`, `anchors.ts` `rebindAnchors`) it names the edge that looks most like that one: facing the same way, with the centre and radius changed least and at the same relative height; the drawn parts held to it are then solved again. Only a clear winner is taken (nothing within reach, or two alike, takes none); then the relation is listed (`CompileOutcome.lost`), the change stays, the constraint flashes and a message says so (`session.ts`), and it holds again when the body does. The description stays the one made with the relation, so each later change is judged against it. The document is not rewritten: the renaming is in the compiled scene, found again from the description on every compile.

**Straight edges and corners (`in_310`).** The straight edges of the body that lie in the sketch's plane (`anchorLines`) and its corners on the plane (`anchorCorners`) are anchors for the drawing hints and the suggestions, as the circular edges are: a line being drawn takes level, upright, parallel, square, on-the-edge and as-long-as with them (`hints.ts`); a figure whose corner is drawn on a corner of the body, and a line that ends on one, is **held to it** by a Coincident relation (`joinedAtCorners`, now also for lines); a selected drawn corner near a body edge is offered On line / Coincident with circle, and a selected circle, arc or line near a corner of the body is offered it (`suggest.ts`). The recorded look and the finding again (`in_309`) cover them too: a straight edge by its direction, its middle across the sketch's plane, its length and its height; a corner by its place across the plane and its height (`EdgeAnchor.kind`, `axis`).

**Edges that are not in the sketch's plane (`in_311`).** The edges and corners of the body count by the way they **fall on the plane** (straight down its facing): a rim lower than the sketch (the bottom of a chamfered post) is a circle on the plane with its own centre and radius, a slanting or lower straight edge a line, a lower corner a point (`anchorRounds`, `anchorLines`, `anchorCorners` give each with its `offset` from the plane; an edge that runs along the facing falls on a point and is left out; of several that fall on one place the nearer to the plane is the one). They are used by the hints, the suggestions, the first point of a circle, the snap of the pointer (a lower corner of the body is snapped to where it falls) and the corners a figure is held to. The solver already put every body corner and line on the plane it solves, so it held them already; the relation names the real edge or corner.

**Hidden and visible (`in_312`).** A part of the body that is **not in the sketch's plane** is offered by proximity (the hints, the snaps, the first point of a circle, the suggestions) only if it **can be seen** from the camera: no face of the body between the eye and some point of it (its ends and middle, eight points round a rim; `visibleFrom` in `picking.ts`, the rule `candidatesAt` already used for picking: a face between the eye and the plane being sketched on hides nothing; the answer is kept for the same scene and eye). Parts in the plane are always offered (they are the face being sketched on). The relations table and the solver are not filtered: a part that is chosen by selecting it, or already named by a relation, is used whether it is seen or not. Orbiting so that an edge comes into view makes it offered; looking so that it is behind the body takes it away.

**Faces (`in_313`).** A face of the body is an anchor by what it makes **in the sketch's plane**: a **flat face** is the line where it cuts the plane, clipped to the face (`traceOfFace` in `part.ts`; a face that runs alongside the plane or that the plane does not reach has none), a **round face** (a post, a hole, a piece of one) is the circle it cuts across its axis (`roundOf`). Where the plane is within the face's extent the anchor is in the plane (offset 0); a round face the plane does not reach is only where it falls on the plane and is offered when it can be seen (`in_312`). Where an edge is already that line or circle the edge stays (it comes first), so the faces add what the edges do not: the walls of a body cut by a plane through it. A face and an edge of the body are a *line* or a *circle* wherever relations are chosen (`relationKind` with the plane being worked in): a drawn corner and a flat wall are offered On line, Distance and the rest; a drawn circle and a round wall Concentric, Equal radius, Tangent. The solver takes the trace as two fixed virtual corners and a round face as a fixed centre and radius (`solve.ts`). The recorded look covers a flat face (its middle, which way it faces, its height) and is found again by how far it moved along its facing, with up to eight degrees of turn counted against it (a tangent wall turns a little when a circle's size changes); the same loosening now holds for straight edges. Only a face with a clear winner is taken; else the relation is listed as lost, as before.

Not built yet (each its own step): a curved face that is not a cylinder (a cone, a sphere, a fillet) has no line or circle in a plane and is not an anchor; a face that is parallel to the sketch's plane (it is not cut: it has no trace, only a distance); body-to-body constraints (`q_body_constraints`).

## Rationale

Constraints between a sketch and the body it sits on are the first thing a user reaches for (a circle on a face, concentric with the edge); the solver already holds body corners and lines fixed, so the missing part was that a circular edge had no centre to hold. Treating the edge as a circle with a fixed centre and radius keeps one relation vocabulary for drawn and body geometry, which is the start of the one system that `q_body_constraints` proposes, and needs no new solver.

**What curve an edge is (`in_341`, `in_344`).** A body edge is a straight line, a circle (or an arc of one), or **another curve** (an ellipse, a curve where two round faces cross, where a tilted round cut crosses a cylinder), and which it is comes from the **kernel's own type** (`circle` is set only for `CIRCLE`; `Line.path` carries the kernel's points along any other curve). A circle is **never made from an edge's ends and middle** unless the kernel says it is one: before, `roundOf` made one for any curved edge, so an ellipse was lit and picked as a circle far outside the body and offered as a circle anchor with a wrong centre and radius. The geometry of a curved line is in one place, `curvePoints` (`operation/part.ts`), which drawing, picking and lighting read. An edge that is another curve is no round (not a relation, hint or anchor circle), its length is its length along the curve (the Similar chip), its fillet arrow stands on it, and it is not offset (`offsetbody`). A history made before `dec_no_stray_corners` replays with `Scene.planeAlias` (`in_341`).
