# Why many gears, many teeth and worm wheels are slow, and what would fix it (measured; most of it built, see Landed)

**Asked (`in_701`):** too many gears, gears with many teeth, worm gears and above all worm wheels are so slow they nearly crash the app, even one of them. Measured in Node with the kernel in the same process, one run each, a sandbox with few cores (good for comparing, not for absolute times).

| case | time | what the time is |
|---|---|---|
| spur sketch, 20 / 60 / 120 / 200 teeth | 25 / 32 / 93 / 186 ms | the sketch only: 24 parts a tooth |
| the same extruded 10 mm | 750 / 1300 / 2600 / 5200 ms | the kernel; the scene then has 1931 / 4827 / 9619 / 16023 parts (about 80 a tooth: a face and its edges for every arc of every flank) |
| 1st to 6th spur gear of 30 teeth, extruded, one after another | 0.8, 1.9, 2.0, 2.5, 3.3, 3.2 s each (13.8 s in all) | the new body's extrude is about 400 ms each time; the rest (2 to 3 s and growing) is work that covers the whole model on every edit |
| worm (24 mm, 30 long) | 0.6 s | |
| **worm wheel, 24 / 40 / 80 teeth**: the page works out the sections | 2.4 / 3.8 / 6.8 s | `wheelsections.ts` `material`/`inside`, marching squares over **every** gap of the wheel, in the page (main thread), on the way into `addGear` |
| worm wheel, 24 / 40 / 80 teeth: the kernel (sections already cached) | 1.4 / 10.5 / 9.0 s | the loft through the 5 sections 7.9 s, naming its faces (`nameFaces`/`surfacesOf`, freeform faces) 4.5 s, 12000 to 15000 parts in the scene |

For the 40 tooth wheel the whole is 14 s; the page is blocked for the first 3 s of it, and the kernel's job limit is 60 s (`kernel-async.ts`).

## Landed (`in_706`: "yes land those three changes with tests")

Three changes, from `prototypes/wheel-fast` (the numbers are in its README): **the quick box for naming the freeform faces of a shape that has more than 64 of them** (`facenames.mjs` `quickBounds`, `QUICK_FROM`: 4.2 s to 8 ms of the 10.3 s kernel time of a 40 tooth wheel, same volume; a loft's own sides are named by comparing the exact box of each face with the box of the sketch's corners, so a body with few freeform faces keeps the exact box, found by the suite); **the faster wheel sections** (`wheelsections.ts`: 10 bisections, a coarse grid first, exact crossings only for chains that can pass, a scan step of 0.2 mm along the thread; 5 to 7 times quicker, within 0.003 mm); **`BRepLib.SameParameter` after a smooth loft** (`interpreter.mjs`: the wheel is no longer refused for 24, 28, 30, 32, 44, 48, 60 teeth). Tests: `test/wheelspeed.test.ts` (the sections against the old scan's numbers; the time; the counts that used to be refused, with their volumes; a bored wheel; the quick box). Of the five proposals below, 1 and 2 are done, 5 is built as `dec_gear_standin` (a plain disc while the body is made, `in_707`), and 3 as `dec_gear_feature` (a gear's body has a few parts, `in_716`); 4 (per-body checks) as `dec_per_body_checks` (`in_726`: six gears 13.8 s to 9.2 s, the rise per added gear about a quarter of what it was).

## The gear's sketch parts (`in_725`): measured first, and what was found instead

Asked: cut down the sides and corners of a gear's outline that stay in the scene after its body is made (about 20 a tooth: 2000 of the 2019 parts of a 100 tooth gear). Measured before touching them, in the real app (a 100 tooth gear with a bore, extruded, headless Chromium) and in Node: **the parts themselves are cheap**: a scan of all of them is some tens of microseconds, picking skips what an extrude used up, the highlight no longer builds them. What was dear was what is **derived from every arc of the outline for each frame**: `avoidOf` (the places a bubble keeps off) turned each of the 400 arcs into 64 points on every frame, 5.6 of the frame's 8.2 ms in Node. And a second cost not tied to parts at all: each axis label asked the whole scene (every segment of every line, every triangle of the body) whether the body hides it, every frame, and threw the lines' answers away. Fixed: `avoidOf` and `centreDots` are worked out once for a scene (`WeakMap`); the label rays go to the meshes only and are not repeated while the camera, the size and the drawn model are the same (`world.ts`, `view.ts`). The 100 tooth gear: frame 12.2 to 9.7 ms orbiting, 11.7 to 6.0 ms hovering (after the gear as one feature had taken 17.5 to 10.9); `frameOf` 8.2 to 2.6 ms in Node; the screen is the same pixel for pixel in three views. **The parts are not removed**: the steps and constraints name them (`d/g_001.s17`), the gear's record would have to make them on demand for the compiler and the solver, and what they cost is now small; that is left unless a number shows they matter (an edit's compile is 15 to 30 ms at 100 teeth: `compileTimings`).

## The page's work per edit (`in_727`) and a bigger cost found with it

The page's own work at an edit that leaves the kernel's graph as it was: **103.6 to 30.8 ms** for eight extruded gears (5799 parts) in the real app (`dec_scene_derivation_cache`). **Fixed (`in_729`, `dec_per_body_checks` 6: 8 gears 4.0 s to 1.5 s, 16 gears 10.5 s to 3.3 s):** a change to the first of eight gears made the kernel build all eight again (about 4 s, the page's own work is under 0.2 s of that), because each step's kept answer is keyed by the steps before it (`interpreter.mjs` `compile`: the key of an op includes the keys of its target and its inputs), so a body that is only a compound beside the others is rebuilt whenever one before it changes. The work that is not the join (the prism of its own sketch, its checks, its mesh) does not depend on the others and could be kept under its own key.

## Worm wheels again (`in_796`: the cost beyond the loft, a 200 tooth wheel, other worms)

Measured first through the kernel (Node, module 2, a 24 mm worm, width 10; the app's job limit is 60 s): 24 teeth 6.7 s, 60 teeth 9.9 s, 100 teeth 21 s, **200 teeth (module 1) 71 s, over the limit, so it would be refused in the app**; the time grows faster than the teeth. Every other worm tried builds (two and three starts, modules 1 to 3, 30 to 100 teeth, with a bore). 38 thousand parts for 200 teeth (the five sections' arcs). Where the 21.8 s of 100 teeth went: the loft 12.7 s, describing the body 5.5 s (3.9 s the kernel's mesh of its 1900 freeform faces: 344 thousand triangles), reading the edges of the five sections for the names of the faces 3.1 s, the validity check 0.9 s, the sections 1.2 s. **Done:** (1) the edges of a section are read by hash (`facenames.mjs` `edgeEnds` used the library's own edge list, quadratic in the edges, which `topo.mjs` says of it): 3.1 s to a fraction; (2) a body with more than 300 freeform faces has a coarser **screen** mesh (`evaluate.mjs` `describeSolid`: 0.1 mm and 0.5 radian, not 0.05 and 0.2: 344 thousand triangles to 77 thousand, 5.0 s to 2.0 s; exports are meshed at their own quality and are not changed; the picture is the same to the eye, 0.5 per cent of the pixels differ faintly on the curved flanks); (3) **12 arcs to the gap, not 18** (`wheelsections.ts` `NGAP`): the loft's time goes as about the 1.5th power of the arcs (100 teeth: 18 arcs 16.9 s, 14 arcs 10.8, 12 arcs 8.7, 10 arcs 7.4) and the volume moves by 0.01 per cent; each arc is 1.5 times longer, so its distance from the flank is about three times the 0.002 mm of the 18 (about 0.007 mm: the loft is 0.05 mm off the exact wheel, a printer 0.2). A saved wheel is made again from its numbers, so it has the 12 arcs and a volume 0.01 per cent different. **Result:** 60 teeth 9.9 s to 7 s, 100 teeth 21 s to 8.7 s, **200 teeth 71 s to 26.8 s** (inside the limit), with 26 thousand parts (13 thousand for 100 teeth, from 19 thousand). `test/wheelspeed.test.ts` (the coarse mesh and the names kept and worked out the same on a wheel; the section test now compares where each tooth starts, as the number of arcs is the code's). **Left:** the loft is still about 12 s of the 17 at 100 teeth (OCCT's through-sections, superlinear in the edges); lofting a tooth and turning it, or a few teeth at a time, is untried; the page's parts for the sections (about 130 a tooth) are still the main weight of a wheel's scene.

## The kernel thread's memory (`in_818`)

Measured (Node, the app's kernel): the WebAssembly memory only grows (its largest is 4096 MB, read from `replicad_single.wasm`); six gears edited 48 times plateau at 600 to 714 MB, so edits do not leak; a **200 tooth worm wheel peaks at 1210 MB**, mostly the mesher's working memory in `shape.mesh()` (800 MB), a loft at 413 MB. The memory is not given back, so after one heavy model the thread stays big and the next heavy one can pass the limit. **Done:** the worker reports its memory with each answer (`heapMB`); `kernel-async.ts` starts a new thread (the page's results stay) when it is over 1800 MB and nothing waits, and before posting a job when it is over 3000 MB (`restartMb`, `hardMb`); the triangulation of a described body is freed (`BRepTools.Clean`) once its mesh is read. Tests: `kernelmemory.test.ts` (a stand-in worker: restart when idle, not with a job waiting, always past the hard limit, the job goes to the new thread). **Not done:** meshing a wheel in parts to lower the 800 MB (cracks between faces are the risk); the restart costs the new thread's start (about a second) and the kept per-body answers in the worker, which are made again.

## The worm wheel's loft (`in_823`)

Tried in the kernel (`BRepOffsetAPI_ThruSections`, a 60 and a 100 tooth wheel): a lower maximum degree, the other parametrisations (centripetal and iso-parametric: the loft is refused), continuity C1 or C0, smoothing off: none helps. **What helps is not checking the sections for compatibility** (`CheckCompatibility(false)`): that check compares every section's edges with the others' and was about half the loft's time. A wheel's sections are one gap turned, matched edge for edge already (`matchSections` in the graph), so the check has nothing to find. **Done:** a loft op of a worm wheel's gear carries `matched: true` (`compiler/graph.ts`), and `interpreter.mjs` `matchedLoft` makes it without the check; if the solid is not valid the loft is made again with the check. Only a worm wheel's lofts (bevel gears' and the person's own keep the check). **Result:** the loft 60 teeth 3.3 s to 1.9 s, 100 teeth 7.3 s to 3.7 s; the whole wheel 60 teeth 5.4 to 4.1 s, 100 teeth 11.0 to 7.3 s, 200 teeth 26.8 to 17.5 s; the volume is the same to every digit (a bored 30 tooth wheel too, where there is no gain: its time is in the bore's cut). Test: `wheelspeed.test.ts`. **Not tried:** lofting one tooth and turning it (the fuse of a hundred teeth to a core would cost what is saved: unmeasured); what is left of a 100 tooth wheel is the loft 3.7 s of 7.3, the rest the sections, the mesh and the names.

## The wheel's other costs (`in_825`)

Profiled a 100 tooth wheel (kernel in Node, 6.0 s, sections cached; the sections are another 1.3 s in the page): the loft 3.7 s (through-sections 1.5, the sketches 0.3, the roles of its faces 0.35 and, after, naming every face against them 0.5, the validity check 0.55, a volume measured for nothing 0.35), the description 1.9 s (the mesh 0.6, the volume 0.35, the rest). **Done:** (1) a loft without holes no longer measures its volume (it is measured when described); the validity check is made once; (2) a worm wheel's loft names only its two end faces by role (`loftRoles` `capsOnly`): the roles of a thousand sides (a box each, every face looked against every box) were about a second, and the flank faces get the names of new faces, which the gear as one feature does not use. **Result:** 60 teeth 4.1 s to 3.5 s, 100 teeth 7.3 s to 6.1 s, 200 teeth 17.5 s to 14.7 s (sections included). **Tried, not worth it:** the validity check without its geometric controls (0.55 s to 0.38 s, a weaker check); the caps (only relevant to the one-tooth strips, `q_wheel_tooth_loft`). **Left:** the sections (1.3 s at 100 teeth, 2.8 s at 200: the worm's scan), the screen mesh and the volume (about 1.5 s: the exact volume of a thousand freeform faces), the sketches 0.3 s.

## The kernel thread's restart looked at in the real app (`in_827`)

Run in headless Chromium with the real worker (a count of the workers started, the memory each answer reports): a first 200 tooth wheel shows the stand-in disc and the label ("Building… 11 s") and then the wheel, with no error on the page; its memory is 1210 MB after the first answer and 1342 MB after more, below the 1800 MB limit, so no restart at the real limit. With the limit lowered to 1100 MB for the run the restart happened after every answer and each answer then took twice as long (the new thread makes the model again): **a model that needs more than the limit from the start would have been restarted after each answer.** Now a thread is restarted only when it has grown by more than two fifths beyond its own first answer (`kernel-async.ts` `baseMb`, `grown()`); with the same low limit the run then keeps one thread and the times are those of no restart. Test: `kernelmemory.test.ts` (one more). **Also found:** a 200 tooth wheel of module 1.02 or 1.03 (not 1.0, 1.01, 1.04) fails in the kernel's loft with a wasm exception and the message "it gave no reason"; it did before this session's loft changes (checked), is not the memory and is not the sections (no tiny edge): `q_wheel_loft_failure`.

## Causes, and the fix that goes with each (the first two are landed; the rest are for the human to choose from)

1. **The wheel's sections and loft** (`in_704`, measured in `prototypes/wheel-fast`): the sections were already one gap turned z times, so that was not the cost; the cost is the scan over the worm's turns at every grid node and every bisection. A faster scan makes them 5 to 7 times quicker (40 teeth 3.4 s to 0.5 s) within 0.003 mm. **Naming the loft's faces took 4.3 s of the kernel's 10.3 s** only for the exact box of each freeform face; the quick box takes 8 ms and gives the same volume. **The smooth loft was refused by the kernel's validity check for about half the tooth counts** (30, 60, 24, 28, 32, 44, 48 failed at module 2 with a 24 mm worm). **Cause (`in_705`):** the loft's edges did not have the same parameter on the 3D curve and on the faces; the solid was whole (same volume to every digit). **Cure:** `BRepLib.SameParameter` on a smooth loft, three lines in the loft op; every count from 12 to 60 then builds, a bore cuts exactly. Together with the quick box and the faster sections a 40 tooth wheel takes 7.0 s in all against 13.7 s, and 24 and 60 teeth now build. One tooth lofted and joined is not tried: the loft is now reliable, and what is left is the loft's own time and the mesh.
2. **The sections are worked out in the page.** After the faster scan they take about half a second at 40 teeth, which is short enough to leave there; moving them to the worker is dropped unless 200 teeth is still long.
3. **Faces are many and every later stage is by face.** About 80 parts a tooth: a face for each of 8 or more arcs a flank, named, described, meshed, picked, listed in the scene, highlighted. A gear as a **feature** with few selectable parts (the tooth flanks of one gear are one part, as a cylinder's wall is one face) removes most of the page's and the naming's work for every gear, and the **Detail** setting already there (`dec_speed_rules` 8) can be lowered for large tooth counts by default.
4. **An edit pays for the whole model, not for what changed.** With six gears each new one costs more (0.8 s to 3.3 s) though its own extrude is 400 ms: the fingerprints of every step, the validity and self-crossing checks of the whole compound, the edge names of the final solid and the description fingerprints all cover every body. Bodies that do not touch are independent: keep the checks, names and fingerprints per body (the description cache already is, `describedMs 0`) so a new gear costs what it is.
5. **Warn and degrade before the limit.** A gear or wheel whose estimated cost is large (teeth times arcs, wheel teeth) draws its wireframe preview (`dec_speed_rules` 7) and builds the solid after a pause, with the job's time shown, rather than appearing to hang for 10 s.

## Rationale

The measuring shows two separate things: a few places where the work is simply too big (the wheel's sections and loft: proportional to teeth, where one tooth would do), and a general one (the cost of every edit grows with the size of the model, so a second and a third gear are what make a single gear's time unbearable). The first is a local rewrite of one file with a test against the present sections (same shapes, to a tolerance); the second is the holistic one the human asked for and is checked with the same script before and after (six gears one after another).
