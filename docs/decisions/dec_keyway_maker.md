# The keyway maker: keys, flats and splines, apart from the gear and the wheel

**Asked (`in_1267`):** offer splined shafts and bores, with real spline tooth profiles (gear-shaft splines and flutes, not drafting splines); take the bore maker out of the gear maker and the wheel maker (which would then make smooth round holes only) into a tool of its own, probably called the keyway maker, that keys shafts to holes with **keys, flats and splines**; keys and flats user defined but with catalogue choices of standard sizes that can be bought; the spline profile chosen from the standards as a gear's teeth are.

**Approved (`in_1268`: "Approve the design, and I'll upload the standards tables as files"); the design (nothing built yet):**
1. **One tool, on a round hole or a round shaft** that is selected (as Cut spokes reads a round body: `roundbody.ts`): it cuts the keyed shape into a hole, or builds the matching shaft end (the male side), with the same numbers for both so a shaft and a hole made from one entry fit. Kinds: key (a keyway, and the key as its own body), D flat, double flat, spline.
2. **The gear, the wheel and Cut spokes keep Bore diameter only** (a round hole); Bore flat, Key width and Key depth leave their boxes. A gear or wheel with a keyed bore is a gear or wheel and then the keyway maker on its bore. Saved projects with a gear that has a flat or key (`GearSpec.flat`, `key`, `keyDepth`) must keep working: they would be read as the gear plus a keyway step (a migration to design before building).
3. **Catalogues** by standard, chosen as the gears' teeth are: keys by shaft diameter (DIN 6885 / ISO 773 widths and depths, ANSI B17.1 square and rectangular, Woodruff), splines by designation (straight-sided 6/10/16-spline of the SAE series, ISO 14 light and medium series, an involute family of ISO 4156 / ANSI B92.1), plus "own numbers".
4. **The first slice** would be what can be sourced and verified: the keys already in `bore.ts` (DIN 6885 by bore, 6 to 38 mm: `standardKey`) and flats, then one straight-sided spline series, then an involute spline (formula driven, so it needs no table).

**What is known about the data (a search, 2026-11-20, `docs/sources/README.md`):** nothing is verified yet. The session cannot read the sites that carry the tables (engineersedge.com, fasten.it, bd-warehouse docs: host not allowed in the session's network), so the search gave only snippets: DIN 6885 rows for 2 to 8 mm and 12 to 30 mm from one hobby calculator page (not authoritative), the ISO 14 designation form N x d x D (6x23x26 and 6x26x30 light, 6x23x28 medium) from sample pages, and the fits (shaft keyway N9, hub keyway JS9, key h9). **No SAE 6/10/16 fractions, no ANSI B17.1, Woodruff, ISO 4156, DIN 5480, B92.1 formula or table was obtained, and none is to be guessed.** Standards are sold (DIN, ISO, SAE, ANSI); facts such as dimensions and formulas are not copyrightable but wholesale copies of a standard's tables may be a concern: derive from formulas where there are any and cite the standard by number (the human's call).

**Status:** (a) the design is approved (`in_1268`); (b) the data is not here yet: the human will upload the tables as files (the list of files asked for is in the handoff); nothing is encoded until they are read; (c) was asked and is not yet answered, so the gear's and the wheel's flat and keyway stay as they are until the new tool exists. Earlier wording of what was needed: either allow the hosts of the sources (the session's network settings) or send the tables as files (as was done for the gear standards): DIN 6885-1, ISO 14, an SAE 6/10/16 spline table or formulas, ANSI B17.1, ISO 4156 or ANSI B92.1; (c) whether the gear's and the wheel's existing flat and keyway should stay until the new tool exists (recommended: yes, so nothing built breaks) and be taken out at the end.

## Rationale

It is a change of where a feature lives and of what data the program holds, and the data is the hard part (real standards, not numbers made up), so it is asked before anything is built, as `dec_piston_maker` was.

## Built so far (`in_1270`: the human sent ISO 14 and two copies of its table)
**Data in hand:** the whole nominal table of ISO 14 (light 15 rows, medium 20 rows, d 11 to 112 mm: N, D, B), read from ISO 14:1978 and ISO 14:1982 table 1 (the two agree in every row), checked against the norelem catalogue (medium 11 to 46) and a web copy (whose medium d=56 designation, 8 x 56 x 66, is a slip: the standard has 8 x 56 x 65, D 65). The fits of the web copy (hole B H9, D H10, d H7; shaft sliding d10/a11/f7, close f9/a11/g7, fixed h10/a11/h7; symmetry t 0.010 to 0.018 by B) are one source (after roymech) and are **not used yet**. The standard centres on the minor diameter d (internal centering) and gives no corner radii in what was read.
**Code (`operation/splines.ts`, `test/splines.test.ts`):** the table (`ISO14`, `iso14("8 x 56 x 65")`, `splineName`), `splineProblem`, and `splineOutline`: the minor circle with N lugs of width B out to the outside circle, as corners and bulges (a shaft is it extruded, a hub is it cut). Tested: the area of the outline against the exact area for three sizes, and in the kernel a splined shaft and a hub cut from a cylinder have the volumes the profile says.
**Still missing data:** the SAE 4/6/10/16 and the involute splines (ISO 4156 / ANSI B92.1 / DIN 5480), the parallel keys of DIN 6885-1 as a full table (only the bores 6 to 38 already in `bore.ts`, from earlier sources), ANSI B17.1 and Woodruff keys. **Not built:** the tool itself (select a round hole or shaft, choose key, flat or spline), the catalogue boxes, and taking the bore numbers out of the gear and the wheel.

## Slice one built (`in_1278`: "build the keyway tool next")
**Two tools in Shape, Fabricate, Models** (`operation/modelkeyway.ts`, kinds `keyhole` and `keyshaft` of `modelkinds.ts`, so the box, the stay-open Create / Update / Done, the 3D preview, take-back of numbers and Play are the models' own): **Keyed hole** (a round body is selected, or clicked as Cut spokes does: `cutFillOf`; the hole is keyed in it) and **Keyed shaft** (a round shaft on the surface: its diameter and length). The box: **Shape** (Key, Flat, Spline), the bore (the body's own, locked, or the one that is made, 10 mm to start), then by shape: **Key width** (0: the DIN 6885 width for the diameter, a quarter of it outside the table) and **Key depth** (0: the standard's hub depth, 0.45 of the width outside the table); **Flat depth** (0: 0.15 of the diameter; there is no standard for D flats); **Series** (Light, Medium) and **Spline size** (the minor diameter; the nearest row of ISO 14 is taken; 0: the smallest that goes round the bore), the box saying the designation (6 x 23 x 28) and its numbers. A key or a flat in a body that has no bore makes the round bore first. A spline hole is made as wide as the minor circle if the bore is smaller; a bore bigger than the spline's minor diameter, a spline that would reach the body's wall (or a gear's roots) and a key too deep are refused with the reason (and the number is taken back as every model's is). The shaft's keyway depth is 0.55 of the key's width, **not from a standard** (the DIN 6885 shaft depths t1 are not in hand): to be replaced when the table is. The hole's cuts are made so that none shares a surface with the bore that is there (the slot starts inside the bore, the flat's metal reaches past its wall, the spline's slots start inside the minor circle). Tests: `test/keyway.test.ts` (volumes in the kernel for a key, a flat and a spline in a solid body and in a bored one, the shaft's volumes against the profile's area, the refusals, the box). Checked in a browser (Playwright): the box and the 3D preview on a cylinder.
**Not built:** the key as a body of its own; taking Bore flat, Key width and Key depth out of the gear, the Wheel and Cut spokes (they stay until the human says the new tool is enough, as agreed); own spline numbers; SAE and involute splines; Woodruff keys and ANSI keys; the fits' tolerances.

## The bore numbers out of the gear and wheel, and Mesh (`in_1279`)
**Taken out:** the gear's box no longer has Flat, Key and Key depth (nor the standard-key line), nor does the Wheel's or Cut spokes's (`WheelSpec` lost `flat`, `key`, `keyDepth`): a bore is a smooth hole there, and its key, flat or splines are made with the keyway maker on the hole. **Kept for what is made already:** `GearSpec.flat`, `key` and `keyDepth`, `bore.ts` and the gear's outline still read them, so a saved gear with a flat or a keyway opens and edits as before (editing keeps the shape it has; it cannot be changed in the box any more).
**Four tools, one set of numbers** (`modelkeyway.ts`; kinds `keyhole`, `keyshaft`, `keyhub`, `keyfit`): the keyed **hole** cut into a round body, the keyed **shaft** (now with a Start along its axis), the keyed **hub** (a disc with a keyed bore, from scratch: one extrusion with the hole in it, so it takes no material from a shaft standing in it) and the keyed **fit** (the same keyway cut into a round shaft that is there: a key's slot, a flat, or, for a spline, the metal outside the profile). **Play** (mm) is the room of a fit: a hole is made that much wider than the nominal, a shaft that much narrower (default 0 when made on their own, 0.1 when made by Mesh).
**Mesh keyway** (`interaction/keywaymesh.ts`, the item next to the keyway tools, enabled with a keyed hole or shaft selected): what the keyway maker makes is remembered (`Scene.keyways`, `KeywayRecord`: the body it is on, hole or shaft, where it stands and its axis and x direction, how far it runs, and the shape with its automatic numbers filled in: the key's width, the flat's depth, the spline's series and size). A **keyed hole** selected: Mesh opens **Keyed shaft** placed on its axis, the same shape, play 0.1, reaching 10 mm out of each side; a **shaft** selected: Mesh opens **Keyed hub** round it (a ring 1.8 times the shaft or 12 mm more across, up to 15 mm thick), in the middle of the shaft. Every number can still be changed, and the box stays open as every model's does. Then, as a gear's Mesh is placed by a click, **a click on round geometry that is there cuts the matching keyway into it**: after the hole's Mesh, a click on a round shaft opens the keyed fit with the hole's shape (the shaft's diameter is the body's); after the shaft's Mesh, a click on a round body opens the keyed hole with the shaft's shape (a body's own bore is locked as always). The spline's teeth stand alike on both (the record keeps the x direction). Tests: `test/keywaymesh.test.ts`, `test/keyway.test.ts`.
**Known limits:** a cut into geometry (the keyed hole or fit) takes material from every body it overlaps, as every cut does: make the hole before the shaft is put in it. The shaft's keyway depth is still 0.55 of the key's width (no DIN 6885 shaft depths); nothing yet makes the key itself as a body.

**Keyed shaft on a rod that is there (`in_1283`):** with a round shaft selected (its surface or the whole body, no bore) the Keyed shaft item keys that shaft where it stands, as the keyed fit does, instead of waiting for a surface to make a new one (the human could not key a rod in their project: the tool kept saying to select a surface). With nothing selected, or a body that has a bore, it still makes a new shaft on a surface. Test: `test/keyway.test.ts`; checked on the human's file (the rod's volume 109427 to 104056 with a key).
