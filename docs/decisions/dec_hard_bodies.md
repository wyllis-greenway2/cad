# Hard bodies: bodies that collide, kept apart by one clearance for the model

`in_472` asked whether bodies that cannot pass through each other ("hard bodies") are possible and feasible, as a natural real-world constraint for interlocking parts, or whether it takes a whole physics engine. The AI's answer and proposal were approved as written, with one clearance for the whole model (`in_473`).

**Answer: possible, and it does not take a physics engine.** What is wanted is a *kinematic* rule, "a hard body may not overlap another body", checked while a body is moved or turned by hand, not dynamics (no gravity, forces, bounce, momentum, friction). The app already has the other half: a driven move is solved against the mates (`solveDriven` in `operation/matesolve.ts` takes the pose the pointer asks for and gives back the nearest pose the mates allow, with `blockedBy` naming what held it back, and the picture flashes red, `in_338`). Non-penetration is one more constraint of that kind, an inequality instead of an equality.

**How it would work**
1. *A flag* on a body: **hard** (saved in the file; shown with a small mark; a body that is not hard behaves as now).
2. *The test:* two hard bodies overlap when the volume of what they share is more than a small tolerance (the kernel's boolean `intersect` and `measureVolume`, which it already runs); touching faces share no volume, so flush and fitted parts are fine; a cheap check of the boxes first, then the exact one only for boxes that meet.
3. *In a drag:* the pose asked for is tested; if it overlaps, the body stops at the last free pose on the way (a search along the path between the last free pose and the one asked for, to the tolerance), the contact is flashed (the touching faces, as a mate's are), and a message says what it is against. A turn is the same along the angle.
4. *Then sliding:* with the contact's normal (the kernel's minimum distance between the two shapes gives the nearest points and so the normal) the part of the motion along the surface is kept, so a peg is pushed along a hole's wall instead of sticking; this is a second step.
5. *What it is not:* nothing falls or is pushed by another body; a body only stops. Pushing a chain of bodies is the next level and is where it starts to be a physics engine.

**Edge cases, and what I would do**
- *Typed and stepped changes* (a move by a typed number, a repeat, a mirror, a push / pull that grows a hard body into another): the same test, refused with the reason, as a move against a mate is.
- *Already overlapping when the flag is set:* allowed, with a message; the moves that reduce the overlap are allowed, the ones that increase it are not.
- *The history:* the rule is for the moment of editing; the steps record the poses reached. Replaying the history does not test them again (or an earlier edit that changed a size would break every later move), but the model is looked at after every replay (`hardOverlaps`, `interaction/hard.ts`): hard bodies that overlap are found, the last step that moved or made either of them (`overlapBlame`) is marked in the History list ("the hard bodies … overlap here: move one of them clear"), both bodies' badges turn red and name what they overlap, and the message line says so. Nothing is refused or undone: it is a warning.
- *Clearance for printing:* a fit needs a gap (a hole 0.2 mm bigger than the peg); a per-body or global **clearance** (the bodies count as overlapping when closer than it) would let the model be the real size and the test keep the gap; this is the part that makes it useful for printed parts.
- *Cost:* exact booleans on bodies with hundreds of faces take tens to hundreds of milliseconds, too slow for every pointer move; so the test is run on the kernel's mesh (a bounding-volume tree of triangles, in the worker) for the drag and the exact boolean is kept for the final answer.
- *Mates:* a mate may put hard bodies in contact (flush faces); that is touching, not overlap. A mate that holds two hard bodies overlapping is reported as a conflict.
- *Sketches and groups:* sketches carried with a body follow it; a group of bodies moved together tests the group against the others, not against itself.

**Size:** the flag, the exact test, blocking a drag at contact with the flash and the message: about a session; sliding along contacts and the clearance: another; mesh-based speed for large bodies: another. No new dependency is needed for the first two (OpenCascade is there); a triangle-mesh tree is a few hundred lines of our own.

**Built (`in_473`, the first two of the three steps, the clearance one number for the model):**
- *The flag and the clearance:* `Scene.hard` (the names of the hard bodies) and `Scene.clearance` (mm, 0 = only going into each other counts), saved, set by the commands `setHard` and `setClearance` (undoable); the ring's *Hard body* (Shape; every body selected, off again when all are hard) and the ⋯ list's *Hard bodies: clearance …* (goes round 0, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5, 1 mm).
- *The test (`operation/collide.ts`, pure):* triangle lists, a tree of boxes over the one that stays, proper crossings of a triangle by an edge (a face on a face, an edge or a corner on a surface are touches), the least triangle distance when there is a clearance (so a face resting on a face is a collision when the clearance is more than nothing), a body wholly inside another by a counted ray; the way a rigid motion goes (`partOf`: a turn about an axis with a slide, taken any part of the way) and `freePart` (24 steps along it so a thin wall is not stepped over, the last free one narrowed in 10 halvings).
- *The rule (`interaction/hard.ts`):* `hardLimit` for a group moved by hand against every hard body that stays (a pair already overlapping is left out so bodies can be pulled out of each other); the triangles come from the face meshes of each body; `limitedOp` for what releasing makes: a drag's move or turn taken only as far as it can go, a number typed that would run into another is refused with the reason, a body already against another says so. While dragging, the picture stops where the bodies meet (`frame.ts` `gpuMotion`) and the body it is against flashes.
- *Sliding along the contact (`in_474`):* a drag by hand (a move taken free, not along an arrow or typed) that meets a hard body goes on with the part of what is left of it that runs along the surface it is against (`hardMotion` in `interaction/hard.ts`): the surface's way is the sum of the normals of the triangles of the other body that the mover goes into (`contactNormal`: only those that face against the motion, not those it brushes), the part of the motion into it is taken out, the rest is made (a free-part search again, with the clearance less a hair so a body that is on the surface may run along it), and so on round a corner up to four times, stopping against the next hard body; the picture and the release (`limitedOp`) give the same place. A turn, a move along an arrow and a typed number still only stop (a slide off the axis of an arrow would not be the arrow's).
- *Copies and growth (`in_475`):* the copies of a repeat (a line, a circle of them, a quick copy) or of a mirror, of the source bodies that are hard, are tested at the places they would stand (`hardCopies`: the same placements the repeat makes, the mirror's plane; each copy against every hard body and against the earlier copies); one that would run into a hard body is refused when the repeat is applied or the mirror made, with which copy and what it is against, and while it is set up the body it is against flashes and the message says so (`hardNote`). A hard body's flat face pulled out (push / pull, more than nothing) is taken as far as it goes before it meets another hard body and drawn so in the preview, or refused when a number is typed (`growth`: the face's own triangles moved along the normal, as an open piece of surface). A push / pull of a round face, a push in, and an extrude of a sketch are not tested. The test was made steadier with this: the moving triangles are nudged a hundredth of a micron before they are tested (so a crossing exactly on a face's diagonal is not lost and a touch stays a touch), and a body wholly inside another is found by a point just inside its surface and not by a corner, which may lie on the other's surface when they line up. Two copies that fall exactly on another body (every face on its face) are found by that inside point as well.
- *The mark (`in_476`):* each hard body has a small round badge with the hard icon over the middle of its top (`Frame.hardMarks`, `ui/hardmarks.ts`: page elements over the picture, not parts of the model); pointing at it says what it means and pressing it makes the body soft (`body:hard:<ref>`, one step). It is there whenever the body's mark is in view; a very crowded model may want fewer.
- *Mates (`in_477`):* a mate that is made (`mateCommand`) is refused when the pose it puts the body in would be inside a hard body, or nearer to one than the clearance, with the body's name and, when there is a clearance, a reminder to give the mate a gap of at least that (two hard bodies a flush mate puts face to face are fine at clearance 0 and refused at any more). A drag of a body that standing mates hold: the order is now the same in the picture and at release: what is asked is first limited by the hard bodies (stop or slide), the mates then settle that, and if the pose they leave is in a hard body the move is refused (at release, with the reason) and the picture shows the body where it was with the body it would be in flashing (`hardCollision`). Not tested: mates that move other bodies along (a chain), a joint's drive.
- *Heavy models (`in_479`):* the moving body has a tree of its own, and `collidesMoving` (`operation/collide.ts`) walks it together with the tree of the body that stays, each box taken to where the motion puts it, moving only the triangles of leaves that meet (a drag, a stop search, a pull of a face and the mate and history checks use it). It gives the same answers as moving every triangle (tested on random moves and turns, with and without a clearance). Where the boxes of the two bodies overlap but nothing touches it was about 3.5 times quicker on 19,200 moving triangles; a body far from the others is stopped at the first box as before.
- *Joints and chains (`in_480`):* a joint (a pin in a hole) driven by its handle or by a typed number goes only as far as its body can before it meets a hard body (`hardDrive`, `interaction/mate.ts`: tested along the way, then narrowed; the body it is against flashes during the drag; a typed number that is too far goes as far as it can, with no message). Standing mates put back together after a change (`solveStanding`, a chain of bodies each resting on the one before) are tested at the poses they settle on: if any body of the chain would be in a hard body, nothing is made, the change is refused and the message names the hard body (`atOddsMessage`).
- *Round faces and extrudes (`in_481`):* a round wall pulled out (a post's, a hole's, also on a turned body) is a piece that grows about its axis, its radius changing by the distance (a post grows, a hole closes in), tested along the way against the other hard bodies like a flat face; a number typed on it is the diameter it is to have, as everywhere. A sketch extruded (either way) off a flat face of a hard body, the sketch lying on that face and going away from the body, is a piece moved along the extrusion (`extrudeCap` gives its triangles); it stops at another hard body, a typed depth that goes in is refused. A sketch that is on no hard body's face, a body made `new` away from them, and extrudes that cut, are not tested (they make a soft body, or take material away).
- *Revolve, sweep, loft (`in_482`):* a figure turned about an axis (`op.kind` revolve) off a flat face of a hard body is the figure moved by the screw the turn makes (the angle, with the advance in proportion): it is turned only as far as it can before it meets another hard body, the advance cut short in proportion, a typed angle that goes in is refused. A sweep of a sketch that stands on a hard body's face is the sketch at stations along the path (`sweepCarry`, `interaction/sweep.ts`: the path's near end carried onto the plane, the shape turned with the path's direction, as the kernel does); a sweep has no distance, so it is refused with the reason when any station is in another hard body, and made when none is. Finding: a loft or a sweep whose solid meets a body is made by the kernel as a cut when it overlaps one (`cutOrAdd`) and joined when it only touches, so it never leaves two bodies overlapping; the hard-body rule can only look ahead, as above, not check the result.
- *Fillet and chamfer faces (`in_483`):* pulling or pushing the face of a fillet or a chamfer changes its size (the step's number), not where the face is. The face is taken as the same shape seen from the sharp edge it rounds (`sharpEdge`: the edge of the step nearest the face, brought to where the body stands now): a fillet or a chamfer at another size is the face scaled about that edge (`homothety`, exact for a chamfer and for a fillet). It is tested along the way like a flat face pulled out, either way: smaller (the corner filled in) can come into a hard body standing in the notch, bigger (an inside corner filled) into one in the corner; a typed size that would go in is refused. A size that changes along the edge (`end`) and a chamfer with two distances (`d2`) are not looked ahead.
- *Colliding and sliding, never refusing or flashing a drag (`in_484`):* a body dragged by hand (a free move) stops at a hard body and slides along it, and that is all that is shown: no flash, no message, and a drag that has nowhere to go leaves the body where it was (it is not refused and cancelled, which read as a sticky mode). Typed numbers, repeats, mirrors, pulls, sweeps and mates are still refused with the reason. Found with a project of a pin in a hole of nearly its own size (`test/fixtures/hard-body-pin-in-hole-in_484.json`): (1) the kernel makes the faces of a body again after each move, and two bodies stopped exactly at the edge of the test could come out a hair inside each other; the pair then counted as already overlapping and the rule was off for it, so a hard pull went through the wall. The stop test is now deliberately more conservative than the overlap test (`MOVE`: triangles a hundredth of a millimetre bigger in their plane; `DEEP`: two bodies are in each other only when they really go in by 0.015 mm), the move is looked at as it is kept (to a thousandth, `settled`), and the crossing test counts a crossing on the edge of a triangle for both triangles (it gave other answers for which body was the one that moved). (2) A long pull took steps of a twelfth of its length and could jump over a thin wall: the steps are now no longer than half of the thinnest hard body (0.2 to 2 mm, up to 600). (3) The plane test in front of the triangle test (`apart`) makes the whole check about four times quicker. Not built: a body that turns and tumbles as it is knocked about (it slides, it does not rotate by contact: that would take a physics engine, which `q_hard_bodies` ruled out).
- ***Changed by `in_1084`:** a drag that a hard body stopped is still cut short and slides as before, but **it is no longer silent**: when it is let go it says what it ran into, the body it ran into flashes red, and the button ("Delete constraint to proceed") deletes the hardness that stopped it (`setHard` and `setFixed` off) and makes the rest of the move; a typed number that would run in is refused with the same button. `inv_constraint_uniformity`: a hard stop is a violated constraint like any other. The picture during the drag is as before.*
- *Sliding that follows a wall (`in_485`):* pulling the pin out of its hole with the pointer a little to one side stopped after a few millimetres, and a pin set down beside the hole slid backwards down a ramp. Two causes. The contact found at the rim of the hole faces along the pull, so there was no surface to slide along: when the contact does not say which way to go, the motion is now tried with each of its parts left out and the one that gets furthest is taken (`trial`), and a drag is followed in up to 40 short legs, given up when three in a row get nowhere (a wall that bends away is followed in many legs). And the normals of everything it touched were added up into one, so a floor and a wall at once made a tilted ramp: `contactNormals` now gives one normal for each way a face faces, and what is left of the motion has what goes into each taken out in turn, so it keeps only what runs along all of them.
- *Speed of the drag (`in_486`):* after the slide was made to follow walls, dragging the block of the pin-in-hole project took 0.4 s a frame (up to 1.1 s): a freeze. Now: the slide makes one leg at a time and tries the fallback only when the contact does not say where to go; the moving body's triangles are placed with numbers and not arrays, the plane of each fixed triangle is made once, and most pairs are told apart by a plane test before any crossing test; steps of a search are no more than needed (no fewer than 6); a slide has at most 350 tests, 10 legs and 90 ms to spend (a frame that takes a second is worse than a slide that stops short); and the same motion asked again for the same scene is kept (`motionMemo`). The whole frame is now about 60 ms (up to 170) for the block, 33 ms for the pin.
- *A drag follows the pointer (`in_487`):* dragging the block of the pin-in-hole project (the pin standing in its hole, so the block can move only a third of a millimetre either way) bounced it to and fro along a straight drag: each picture was worked out again from where the drag began, and the choice of which way to go round a facet of the wall flipped from one picture to the next. Now a drag by the pointer (a free move, `op.grab` set) goes on from where the last picture put the bodies, towards where the pointer is (`hardMotion`'s `follow`, kept per scene in `dragMemo`; it starts again from the start when the pointer is back at the start or jumps more than 60 mm), and a picture is never farther from the pointer than the one before: the bodies get nearer or stay, they do not wander off along a wall and back. Where the slide has no surface to go along it tries the ways of getting nearer (not the way that goes furthest), and a last try from where it ended with each part of what is left. The release makes what the last picture showed (the same scene and pointer place give the same answer). A pointer drag is now path-dependent on purpose; typed numbers and anything without `grab` are not.
- *Marks like constraints, no numbers in a free drag (`in_489`):* the mark of a hard body is hidden, as a constraint that holds is; it shows (red) only while the body is violated: while it is bumping into another hard body (the body that is dragged, where it has got to, and the body it is against, each saying which), or when an earlier change has put it into one (`in_478`). Pressing a shown mark makes the body soft; the ring menu and the ⋯ row are the ways to make one hard. (`Frame.hardMarks[].bumps`, `bumpNote`, from `gpuMotion`'s `bumping`.) The X, Y, Z position bubbles are not shown while a body is dragged free (`numberShown` in `frame.ts`): only while an arrow or ring is dragged, typed, or armed. Asked to leave for now: rotation of a hard body when it cannot go the way it is pulled (a prototype first, `in_488`).
- *One hard body is enough, and it is a constraint (`in_490`):* a hard body treats every body it meets as hard, so only one of two has to be declared hard for them to keep out of each other, whichever of the two is moved by hand (a hard body moved at a soft one stops, a soft body moved at a hard one stops); two soft bodies still go through each other. Done as one rule for a pair, `meets(a, b)` = either is hard (`hard.ts`), used where the pairs were taken among hard bodies only: the drag, mates and joints (`hardCollision`), copies, pulls, extrudes, revolves and sweeps (their owner and the bodies it can run into), and the overlaps the history can leave. A mark shows on the hard body of a pair (a soft body has none). In the menu the Hard body item is in the Constraints section (offered for a selected body), not under Shape.
- *Not built:* making a fillet or a chamfer (the edge operation) against hard bodies (it takes material away from a convex edge, and adds it in an inside corner, where a hard body could stand); a loft against hard bodies (what it makes between its sections is only known by the kernel); the tree is not used for the contact normal of a slide; a typed joint number that runs into a hard body is cut short without saying so; a sweep is tested at stations (24 to a side), so a wall thinner than a station's step may be missed.

## Rationale
It adds a property to bodies and a rule to every motion, so it is asked before it is built.
