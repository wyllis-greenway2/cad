# Bevel gears: built as an approximation (`dec_bevel_gears`); what is still to verify

**Asked (`in_719`):** 90 degree gears and other angles, as real profiles to the standards, manufacturing agnostic. **The prototype (`in_720`, `prototypes/bevel-pair`)** makes a straight bevel pair for any shaft angle as ruled lofts of a Tredgold back cone tooth and it fits (no overlap at the computed place, 2 to 4 mm3 at 0.3 mm closer, 120 to 220 mm3 tooth on tooth), see its README. **Sources (`in_722`: the human sent three PDFs):** the Autodesk Inventor help page "Basic Geometric Calculation for Bevel Gears" gives the formulas (pitch angle, cone distance, equivalent teeth, addendum `m (ha* + x - k)`, dedendum `m (ha* + c* - x)`, dedendum reduction `k = 0.02 (17 - zn)`, face and root angles, tooth thickness with the unit correction) and the prototype now uses them (the fit is unchanged); the ISO 23509 and AGMA 937 PDFs are previews with no formulas, and show which sections hold the rest. **KHK-USA's article (`in_723`) closed the long and short addendum and the face width:** wheel addendum `0.54 m + 0.46 m / uv`, pinion `2 m` less that, dedendum `2.188 m` less the addendum, face width at most `R / 3` (and at least `10 m`, which cannot hold for a small gear); the prototype uses them by default (the fit holds, a 20 / 30 pair with a long pinion addendum overlaps nothing at its place). **Backlash (`in_734`: the AGMA backlash article from Gear Technology, Sept / Oct 2017, "Backlash and Axial Movement", Bob Wasilewski, chairman of the AGMA bevel gearing committee):** ISO and AGMA define bevel backlash as the **outer normal backlash at the tightest point of mesh** (the tabulated values are a suggested range for that point only, measured at the outer diameter, perpendicular to the tooth); the transverse backlash is `jet = jn / (cos an cos be)` (a straight bevel: `be = 0`); the axial movement for a change of backlash `dj`: `dj1 = dj tan d1 / (tan d1 + tan d2)`, `dj2 = dj tan d2 / (tan d1 + tan d2)`, `da = dj / (2 tan an sin d)`; the values to use are in AGMA 2008-D11 (assembly) and the AGMA / ISO backlash tables, **which the article does not give**. The three Gleason PDFs sent with it (Gear Technology Solutions, pages 293 to 298) are the blade definitions chapter for cutter heads (blade angles, grinding summaries): about making the teeth, not their proportions: not used (the parts are manufacturing agnostic). **What is still open:** (1) from the standard: the split of the tooth thickness between pinion and gear (the thickness modification: 0 here, both `pi m / 2`), the backlash, the undercut limit and the suggested pinion tooth counts (AGMA 937 Figure 8), the pressure angles other than 20; before the KHK article these were open too: the defaults the Autodesk page does not give: `ha*` and `c*` (1 and 0.2 are used, unverified), how the unit correction `x` (the long and short addendum) is shared between pinion and gear, and the face width: these are the page's own sub-pages, "Distribution of Unit Corrections for Single Gears" and "Design of Face Width" (and the maximum dedendum filleting), and AGMA 937 Table 10 and Figure 8 or ISO 23509 section 7; before this the proportions were Boston Gear's stock numbers (20 degrees, `a = m`, `hT = 2.188 m + 0.0508 mm`, tooth thickness `pi m / 2`), not the standard's (AGMA 2005 / ISO 23509 / DIN 3971 could not be read here: paywalled, and the sites of KHK, Autodesk and Engineers Edge are not on the session's allowed hosts); the standards' long-and-short addendum (the pinion's addendum longer, by the ratio) is not applied; (2) the face width (a third of the cone distance, from lecture notes, and a minimum of 10 m from the same); (3) backlash, undercut and pointed teeth checks for small tooth counts; (4) the tool: a Bevel gear in the Gear submenu and a Mesh bevel gear that places the partner at the shaft angle with the other's apex and phase; spiral bevel (a lengthwise curve at a spiral angle, 35 degrees in the maker's stock gears) and hypoid (shafts that do not meet: an offset) as approximations marked as such, since their flanks come from machine settings, not a formula. **The human decides** whether to build it and from which source (a source the human can send, as the pulley data was).


## The octoid tooth: what the research of `in_764` found (web searches; the pages themselves were not read)

- **What an octoid is:** the straight and spiral bevel tooth form of generated gears, "the bevel gear analog of the involute" (Stadtfeld, Gear Technology). Its flank is the envelope of the **flat flank of an octoidal crown rack** whose pitch surface rolls without slipping on the gear's pitch cone (Figliolini and Angeles, J. Mech. Design 127(4), 2004, DOI 10.1115/1.1900147; and a 2015 paper on spatial octoidal gears, DOI 10.1115/1.4031679). The line of pressure (the path of contact) on the sphere is a **figure of eight**, the octoid, where the spherical involute's is a great circle arc. The octoid is **close to, but not the same as, the spherical involute** (an Iranian university paper: cutting with straight blades gives the octoid, forging can give the true spherical involute; another source: the octoid is thicker near the root and thinner toward the toe, not confirmed from a page).
- **The tooth form is not in the standards' main text:** ANSI/AGMA 2005 and ISO 23509 give the macro geometry (cones, depths, thickness); the form of the flank is a result of the generating method. AGMA 929-A06 (an information sheet) is the virtual spur gear way of top land and thickness, and says itself that the virtual spur gear is "only a close approximation to the true form".
- **Derived here (`prototypes/bevel-spherical`, no source needed): the spherical involute**, the exact conjugate tooth of a straight bevel gear: a cone through the apex over a spherical involute, whose azimuth is `f(t) = integral from gb of sqrt(sin^2 u - sin^2 gb) / (sin gb sin u) du` (`t` the angle from the axis, `sin gb = sin(delta) cos(alpha)`); the test checks the property it is derived from (the tangent of the curve has the constant axial component `sin gb`). The pitch plane's section is `r = R cos(delta) tan t`, so it drops into the body made now (`bevelRaw` in `operation/bevel.ts`) as a change of the points of one function. **Measured (`prototypes/bevel-spherical/README.md`):** for a 20 / 30 pair the spherical involute and the Tredgold tooth give the same overlap to 0.1 mm3 at every place of the mesh, so the exact form does not fit better at this size; not tried at larger ratios or few teeth. Two earlier runs measured nothing (no backlash; the originals kept).
- **Where the rest is (links for the human, none read by me):** Stadtfeld, Gear Technology, January 2001: https://www.geartechnology.com/ext/resources/issues/0101x/stadtfeld.pdf (free, the Octoid defined); Dooner and Vivet, "Deproximating Tredgold's approximation", open postprint: https://lirias.kuleuven.be/bitstream/123456789/539234/1//MMTPaper-Tredgold-postprint.pdf ; Gear Technology, "Bevel Gear" (2015): https://ik.imagekit.io/agmamedia/gt/issues/0315x/Bevel-Gear.pdf ; Gear Technology "A Practical Approach for Modeling a Bevel Gear" (AGMA 14FTM13): https://geartechnology.com/a-practical-approach-for-modeling-a-bevel-gear ; Thermal Processing, "Computerized design of straight bevel gears with optimized profiles for forging molding or 3D printing": https://thermalprocessing.com/computerized-design-of-straight-bevel-gears-with-optimized-profiles-for-forging-molding-or-3d-printing/ ; SAE 1999-01-3745 "Analytical Definition and Application of Straight Bevel Gear Tooth Form" (paywalled): https://saemobilus.sae.org/papers/analytical-definition-application-straight-bevel-gear-tooth-form-1999-01-3745 ; Figliolini and Angeles (paywalled, ASME; the abstract page: https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/algorithms-for-involute-and-octoidal-bevel-gear-generation/812273507588112385-4058 ); AGMA 929-A06 preview: https://webstore.ansi.org/preview-pages/AGMA/preview_AGMA+929-A06.pdf ; ISO 23509-1:2025 (paid, about 200 EUR): https://www.sis.se/en/produkter/mechanical-systems-and-components-for-general-use/gears/iso-23509-12025 ; Gleason Gear Technology Solutions page 55 (the straight bevel basics): https://www.gleason.com/gear-technology-solutions/files/basic-html/page55.html .

## Rationale

A bevel gear pair is the usual way to turn a shaft through an angle; the loft the worm wheel uses makes its tooth exactly, and the measured fit says the geometry is sound. The proportions are what a standard fixes, and this record does not guess them: the numbers used are named with where they came from and marked unverified.
