# A spline tool, curves made into arcs only when needed, and a cam helper that draws onto it

**Drafted at `in_737`; approved at `in_738`** (the human took the recommendation on how a spline is stored: control points, made into arcs only when the kernel needs them). What is built first is the spline with its ends constrained; dimensions along a curve are open (`q_spline_dimensions`).

Sketch sides are only straight lines and circular arcs (`Line.bulge`, `operation/part.ts`), so a freeform shape such as a cam cannot be drawn by hand; the gear code only gets by because arcs fitted through points of a known curve are exact enough. Proposed, in this order:

1. **A spline side.** A smooth curve through points the person clicks, between corners like any side. It stores its **control points** (so it stays editable) and is drawn as its own curve. Constraints at first: its two end corners, and the tangent direction there (to meet a line or arc smoothly). Dimensions on its inside are left for later (`q_spline_dimensions`).
2. **Arcs only when the kernel needs them.** A spline is turned into a chain of arcs to a tolerance (a thousandth of the largest size, as the gear fit is) when an extrude, hole, offset or export needs a closed outline, and not before; the edges of the body then are arcs that can be picked and constrained as now. Trim and offset of a spline work on the same fitted chain.
3. **Ellipse**, after, as a smaller addition (angled cuts through cylinders, threads).
4. **A cam helper** (a generator in the Gear submenu's manner, `dec_gear_tool`): base radius, lift, rise, dwell and return angles, follower (knife edge, roller with its radius, flat), and a motion law (uniform, parabolic, simple harmonic, cycloidal, 3-4-5 polynomial). It writes a spline (or the fitted arcs) onto the sketch; the person then edits the points or keeps the numbers. Eccentric and snail cams need no spline and can be done first.

Ratchet wheels and pawls, Geneva drives and splines (the earlier list) are further generators of the gear tool's kind and need no new curve, so are separate entries.

**Built (`in_739`): the spline tool, first slice.** `operation/spline.ts`: a centripetal Catmull-Rom curve through the points (`splineAt`, `splinePoints`) and its arcs (`splinePieces`, each through three points of the curve, split until within `splineTolerance`, 2 parts in 10,000 of its length, 0.02 mm for a hundred). `Line.spline` keeps the points between the ends as (along the chord, out from it) in chord lengths, so a curve goes with its ends when they are moved, turned or stretched. The **Spline** tool (Sketch ring, after Curve; `tools.ts`, `pointer.ts`): each click places a point, a click on the last point again, a double click or Enter makes it (two points make nothing; a curve that returns to its start is not made yet). One place makes the arcs: `loopSteps` (`part.ts`), so the faces, the fill, the extrude sent to the kernel (`graph.ts`; the pieces are named `<line>~k`) and the flat export (`exportsketch.ts`) all see the same chain. Repeat and paste carry a spline (a mirror turns the side it bends to). **Built (`in_740`): moving the points.** A selected spline shows the points it passes through as dots (`highlight.ts`); pressing on one and dragging moves it on the spline's plane (`handles.ts` `knotAt`, `moveKnot`; the new command `setSpline`), the curve follows, the ends stay, and the whole drag is one step as a corner's is. Pressing on the curve between its points still drags the whole line. **Built (`in_741`): adding and taking away points** (`interaction/splineedit.ts`, called from `doClickSelect`): a double click on the curve of a spline puts a point there (on the curve, so its shape hardly changes, between the two points either side), and a double click on one of its points takes it away; the last point between the ends cannot be taken away (the message says to draw a line instead). **Built (`in_742`): the eccentric and the snail cam.** Two items in a **Cam** submenu of the Sketch ring (`tool:eccentric`, `tool:snail`; the **cam** tool of `tools.ts`, with `armCam`, `camSpecOf`, `CAM_FIELDS`). The first click is the shaft; the pointer's distance from it sets the diameter; the number box has **Diameter**, **Offset** (an eccentric: how far the shaft is from the disc's middle, the follower moves twice this) or **Lift** (a snail: how far it rises over one turn), and **Hole** (the shaft, a fifth of the diameter by default, 0 for none); Tab goes between them. `operation/cams.ts` (the shapes and the reasons a cam cannot be made, e.g. a hole that would break through the edge) and `operation/camdraw.ts` (draws them as ordinary parts: an eccentric is two circles, a snail is a spline and a straight line closing it, and the hole; all on the plane, with the shaft where it was clicked). (Made at first as plain drawing; since `in_768` kept as a record, see below.) Tests: `cams.test.ts` (volumes against the formulas through the kernel). **Built (`in_757`): the cam helper (the disc cam).** Cam > **Disc cam** > Cycloidal, Harmonic or Parabolic (the law of the follower's motion while it rises and returns: all three start and end at rest, so the outline has no corner; cycloidal is the gentlest, harmonic the simplest, parabolic an even push then an even brake; a steady-speed law is not offered, its outline has corners the spline would round off). The box (a panel with every number named and explained, as the gear box, `CAM_HINTS`; its lengths follow the number system, its degrees do not): **Diameter** (the base circle), **Lift**, **Rise**, **Dwell** (at the top) and **Return** (degrees from +x, counter-clockwise; the rest of the turn is the dwell at the bottom), **Hole**, **Roller** (radius; the outline is the pitch curve taken in by it; a roller too big for a tight turn is refused with the reason). `operation/cams.ts` `discShapes`: the outline as knots of one closed spline (`splinering.ts`), the ends of the rise, dwell and return, some between and more wherever the spline strays from the true outline by more than 0.02 mm (about 50 knots by default, few enough to edit by hand); `camdraw.ts` draws it and the hole. Tests: `cams.test.ts` (the heights at the angles, the three laws against each other, the knots within 0.05 mm of the true outline, the roller, angles over a turn, the extruded volume, the box). **Built (`in_767`): the ratchet wheel and pawl** is a further shape of this tool, with its own entry `dec_ratchet`. **Built (`in_768`): cams and ratchets keep their numbers.** A cam or a ratchet is a **gear record** with `spec.cam` (`GearSpec.cam`, `teeth` and `module` 1 and unused), so everything a gear's record gives comes with it: the parts are made from the record each time the scene is derived (`operation/camscene.ts`: locked, named `d/g_003.s0`, `.p0`, `.k`, the shaft's hole `.b`, a ratchet's pivot `.q`, the pawl's sides `.t#`), the record is added, changed and deleted by `gear`, `setGear` and `deleteGear`, Move and Turn change the record's centre and turn, a body made from the outline follows a change of numbers by the same names (a step is kept; the outline's sides are swapped if their count changes), and Delete takes the record with its parts. **Edit cam** (Cam submenu, and Edit gear on a cam) opens the cam's numbers as they are now in the cam box and Enter changes the record (`editCamTool`, `cam:edit`); a cam keeps a turn it was given by Turn when its numbers change (only the ratchet has an Angle field). A cam does not mesh with a gear (a clear message). A cam's spline is locked: its points are not dragged or added by hand any more (change it with its numbers); the dots of a spline are shown only for a spline that can be changed. `drawCam`/`camdraw.ts` is gone: the tool makes the record. Tests: `cams.test.ts` (a body made from a disc cam follows a change of lift by the same step, a hole as wide as the cam is refused, a move and a delete follow the record, Edit cam reads the numbers and Enter sets the record, a turn is kept). **Built (`in_795`): the straight ratchet** (`dec_ratchet`). **Built (`in_782`): the Geneva drive**, with its own entry `dec_geneva`. **Not yet:** a flat-faced or swinging follower, a turn (angle) field for the cams other than the ratchet, the dwell and rise at other places than the start (start the cam by turning it), **Not yet (spline):** the tangent constraint at the ends, typing a point's place, snapping a point to a corner or line, touch, a spline cutting or being cut by the lines it crosses (`regions.ts` leaves it out), trim, offset and revolve (they refuse a spline as they do an arc where they cannot work one), a spline closed on itself, ellipse and the cam helper. Test: `spline.test.ts`.

**Built (`in_979`): the cam tools are sized and made as the gear tool is.** The first click puts the shaft; the next click only sets the scale (the pointer's distance: the diameter, the crank, the bar's length) and the number box stays open to change the numbers, the pointer then no longer changing the scale; the click after it, Enter or the box's **Create** makes the cam and the tool is over (it used to stay on for the next cam; `in_193` is for the figures). **Edit cam** has the gear's buttons: **Done** when no number was changed (a number the box shows rounded is not a change) and **Update** when one was. Tests in `geneva.test.ts`.

## Rationale

A cam is defined by a motion law, not by a tooth standard, so it must be drawn as a freeform curve; a person who wants their own shape needs the same curve, and the helper becomes one more way to write it. Keeping control points keeps the curve editable; fitting arcs late keeps the kernel, picking, constraints and export unchanged (they know only lines and arcs, `inv_kernel_boundary`). The cost is that a spline's own dimensions are hard (`q_spline_dimensions`), which is why the first slice constrains only its ends and tangents.
