# Edits that are valid geometry are not refused: face kinds, the feature behind a face, and fillets as removed regions

`in_402` found edits that are valid geometry and were refused (a fillet past the size where it reaches the corners of the faces next to it; a hole that cannot be moved through the skin in front of it; a chamfered top that cannot be pulled up into a pyramid), and asked for a simpler model with fewer edge cases. `in_405` approved options **A** and **B** of the question that followed, and asked for **C** to be a later project.

**The model.** An edit is a **set operation on regions**, and its numbers **saturate**: past a limit it adds or takes nothing more, it does not fail. A refusal is left for real constraints (a locked length) and for an edit that would leave nothing of the body. Built on OpenCASCADE's booleans, with simple solids (slabs, wedges, prisms, rings) made for the purpose.

**A (built, `in_405`).** (1) A push or pull of one **flat face** has three kinds, chosen on a chip beside the number (`ui/facekindchip.ts`): *Move* (what it did: the step that made the face changes), *Extrude* (a prism of the face's outline is added or taken), *Extend* (the face moves and the faces next to it are carried on as surfaces until they meet: the kernel operation `faceExtend`, a step `pushPull` with `extend: 1`; the slab between the old and the new plane is kept to the side of each neighbouring flat face that the body is on, and for a neighbour that rises above the face (a pocket wall) to the other side; past the point where the neighbours meet nothing is added, and a push inward that would leave nothing is refused). Extend needs flat or round neighbours (`in_410`: a cylinder is the inside of its cylinder, kept for the outline of the face and taken out for a hole in it or a post standing on it; a cone, sphere or torus is refused with "move it instead"). Several selected flat faces have the kinds Extrude and Extend (each along its own normal, by the same amount). (2) The **wall of a hole or a post** (a face that a circle made) is **moved or turned by moving or turning that circle** (`figureBehindFace` in `faceedit.ts`, used by `startOp` for Move and Rotate): along the axis it sweeps the hole through the part, across it slides it, turned it tilts it, and the steps after it are worked out again.

**B (built, `in_405`).** A fillet or chamfer of **straight edges between flat faces** is, when the kernel's own fillet cannot be made at that size, made as a **region** (`regionRound` in `interpreter.mjs`): the area between the two face lines and the rounding (the rolling circle, or the bevel) is a prism along the edge, **cut** from the body where the edge is convex and **added** where it is concave. There is no size at which it fails: past the size where the rounding stops touching the faces tangentially it crosses them (a convex corner is cut across the faces beside it; a concave one is filled as far as the faces go and no further, so nothing stands out as a wing). All the regions of one step are made from the body as it was. The step says so (a note: "could not be made as a smooth round at this size ... so the corner was cut as a region") and a size that would take all of the body is refused ("would take all of the body"). The kernel's own fillet is still tried first, so every size that worked before gives the same body, and it stays for edges between curved faces (rims, the edge of a dome and a hole), with its refusals and the neighbour retry of `in_379`. **B for rims (built, `in_406`).** The circle where a flat face meets a round wall about an axis at right angles to it (the end of a post or a hole, the foot of a post) is rounded or bevelled by a region made by **revolving** the corner area about the axis (`rimTool`), cut where it is the corner of the body and added where it is the foot of a post. A rim **saturates** at the largest size the faces beside it allow (the wall's height; the flat face's width, which for the end of a post is its radius, so past that the round is a dome), because a region crossing the axis or going past the foot of a post would cut into what is not the corner. Only a whole rim (the two halves the kernel makes of it are both chosen, as the tool chooses them). Straight edges cross the faces past their size (a convex corner), rims do not: the first is a cut across what is there, the second would be a drilled dent or a cusp on the axis.

**Variable fillet and chamfer (built, `in_406`, `in_410`).** A fillet or chamfer of **straight or curved edges that are not rims** can have a size at the start of each edge and another at its other end, and optionally a third at its middle (the library's `[r1, r2]` for a fillet; the kernel's radius law `BRepFilletAPI_MakeFillet.Add(UandR, edge)` for the three sizes, called directly: the places along the edge run from 0 to 1; a chamfer has no variable form in the kernel, so it is always made as a region). A chip beside the number (Constant, Variable), three numbers (Start, End, Middle; Tab goes round them, or click any), the start end of each edge in amber; the step has `value`, `end` and `mid`. The start is the end `a` of each edge (the first point of its name). Such a step is never rounded together with others. Where the kernel cannot make it, straight edges between flat faces are made as a region between profiles (a ruled loft for two sizes, the cone the rolling ball makes; a smooth loft through five profiles with a middle size), so a size at either end or in the middle does not fail either. A variable chamfer on a straight edge between flat faces is exact (its face is a plane). Not built: a size at more than three places along an edge; a loft for curved edges.

**Mixed selections and corners (built, `in_409`).** A selection that mixes edges a region can make (straight between flat faces, rims) with edges it cannot (`mixedRound`): when the kernel cannot round the whole selection, the regions are made from the body as it is and the kernel rounds the other edges on the result, or the other way about, whichever works; the step says so. Where the others cannot take the size either it is refused, with the kernel's reason. Corners where several rounded edges meet: where the kernel can round them together it makes its spherical corners; where it cannot, the regions of all the edges are made from the body as it was, so a corner is the body inside every rounded edge (valid, with creases where the rounds cross; tested on all twelve edges of a cube at more than half its side, to 0.4 per cent of the volume of that body).

**Corners and partial rims (built, `in_410`).** Where three rounded straight edges meet at a corner of three faces at right angles, the same size for all and no larger than half of any, the region method adds the corner's piece of a sphere (the cube of that size at the corner, without the ball), so it equals the kernel's own corner (tested to a millionth of the volume on three edges and on all twelve edges of a block). A piece of a rim that really ends (an arc that stops on a wall that cuts the round, such as a hole broken out through a side face) is rounded by a region that turns through the span of that piece and ends in a flat cut. **Changed (`in_479`):** one half of a circle the kernel splits a rim into is **not** such a piece: where the pieces between the same flat face and the same round wall (which the kernel may keep as two half faces) on the same circle close it, naming one is naming the whole rim, as the kernel's own round follows an edge all the way round, and the region turns all the way round in one turn. Before, the region of one named half took off half of what the kernel's round of the same rim takes off, so a bigger round took off less where the region took over (found by the continuity test of `in_477`: `cyl-fillet.cad.json`, 907 to 987 mm3 at 4 to 4.5 mm; now 898.7 to 890.1).

**Built, in part (`in_411`).** (1) **A size at up to five places along an edge** (the params `value`, `end`, `mid`, `q1`, `q3`; Tab goes round the fields; the kernel's radius law takes the points, the region fallback a monotone interpolation through them). (2) **A rim with a size that changes along it** (a closed rim: its start at the circle's first point, `rimTool` takes the sizes by angle). (3) **A spherical corner for chamfers** (exact flat corner) **and for corners not at right angles** (the patch is the slab intersection; agrees with the kernel to about 2e-4 of the volume). (4) **The region for curved edges** (edges between a flat face and a cone, sphere or torus, or two curved faces): `curvedRegion` draws sections across the edge, taking the two faces as the planes tangent to them there, and lofts them; it is **approximate** and is the last thing tried (only in the retry made with `corner`, after the neighbours), with a note saying so. Measured on a post with a cross hole: 6 per cent below the kernel's removed volume at 0.5 mm, 12 per cent at 1 mm, about half at 3 mm, so it is an honest stand-in, not the rolling ball.

**Still not built:** the exact rolling ball between curved faces (with C, the own kernel); a spherical corner for chamfers at corners not at right angles; a size at more than five places along an edge; a varying size on a rim made of one half or on several rims at once.

**C (later, with our own kernel).** Rebuild bodies from their surfaces (half-spaces, cylinders) so that every number saturates by construction, and kinds of face edit that need it (extend across curved neighbours). It is a second geometry engine, which `dec_direct_modification` rejected for now; it belongs to the project of writing our own kernel (`dec_kernel_hybrid`, "our own later"). Listed in `docs/roadmap.md`.

**Not decided:** a variable fillet (the library has a start and an end radius per edge, not a radius at several points; untested where a constant radius fails).

## Rationale

The refusals all came from one thing: the old operations are patches on the faces and edges that exist and fail where the patch would change which faces exist. Saying what an edit means as a region makes the limit part of the answer. Built on the booleans we already use, it needs no second engine, and `in_405` chose it over rebuilding from surfaces.
