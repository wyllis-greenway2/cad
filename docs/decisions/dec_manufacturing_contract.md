# Manufacturing routes are modules behind a plain-data contract

**Accepted by the human (`in_561`) as proposed by the AI in the brainstorm `in_543` to `in_551`. Nothing here is built.** The human decided directly: a plan is per part and machine, and findings advise and never block (`in_546`); each manufacturing process is its own route, offered as a machine type to choose from (`in_550`). The human asked to lay groundwork, not to build slicers now (`in_543`).

1. **Modelling stays manufacturing-agnostic.** The model holds geometry and process-neutral intent only (material, tolerance, fit); it never knows a machine. Manufacturing never edits it silently. A part (or a drawing) goes through a flow that checks and prepares it.
2. **Two kinds of route.** A *toolpath route* turns a part into machine instructions. A *derivation route* turns a part into new parts (mould cavity, sand mould, cast pattern with draft and shrink, sheet-metal flat pattern, print splitting). A derivation is a **step in the operation graph** (it regenerates when its source changes); the derived part is the *manufacturing version* (`dec_manufacturing_version`, `in_553`). A **plan** (`in_546`: one per part, in practice a manufacturing version, and machine) lives in a separate `manufacturing` section of the project, refers to bodies or drawings by stable ids, and names a route, a machine, a material and settings; it is never stored inside the model graph. Routes chain: source, derivation, toolpath route, machine.
3. **One route per process** (`in_550`): laser, plasma, waterjet, milling (router and mill), vinyl, FDM, and so on, each offered as a machine type. A route owns what really differs: its **kerf or compensation model** (laser: about constant beam width; waterjet: taper and corner lag with speed; plasma: amperage and thickness, pierce height; milling: tool radius), its **material limits** (a laser and PVC, a waterjet and brittle stock: reported as findings), and its operation list. Milling grows into 3D operations (stock model, gouge and collision checks, ball and bull tools, 3D roughing and finishing, extra axes) inside its own route without touching the others. A route's operations are its own sub-registry.
4. **Stages of every route:** *Fit* (does this part suit the route; the app can list the routes a part could take), *Check* (findings), *Prepare* (orientation, setups, nesting, tabs, kerf; changes the plan, not the model), *Plan* (toolpath), *Emit* (the machine's dialect writes the file: **shared code that every route gets, not a stage a route implements**, `in_566`, `in_567`; built: `manufacturing/core/gcode.ts`, `writeGcode`). A route implements only Fit, Check and Plan, and may stop at any of them (print readiness stops at Check and hands off a 3MF); the `Route` interface has no `emit`.
5. **Findings advise and never block** (`in_546`). A finding has a severity, a message, geometric references (so the viewport highlights it) and an optional fix that becomes an ordinary model edit the user approves. One findings list in the UI, fed by every route.
6. **The contract** is a small versioned set of plain types in one place (no kernel or app objects; survives `structuredClone`). *In:* part snapshot (mesh in mm and Z up as modelled, optional STEP bytes, flat outlines in plane coordinates, body ids, material tags). *Out:* a toolpath (ordered moves: travel, cut, extrude, power, feed, tool), preview geometry, findings, files (bytes and a name), and a **parameter schema** from which the settings UI is generated.
7. **A route declares itself to a registry** (built, `in_568`: `manufacturing/registry.ts`, `createRegistry`, `runRoute`): what it accepts, its parameter schema, the definition kinds it uses (`dec_manufacturing_definitions`), its menu rows, its preview. Removing a route removes its rows and nothing else. One shared preview overlay draws the toolpath in the existing camera and world (polylines coloured by layer, a scrubber; built, `in_570`: the toolpath's `frame` says where it is in the world; the stock box is not drawn yet); the renderer needs an overlay hook and no route logic goes into the UI (`inv_no_logic_in_ui`).
8. **Shared infrastructure, built once:** the contract, **analysis services** (wall thickness, overhang, minimum radius, draft, undercut, flatness, bounding box; our graph already knows holes, faces and fillets), the findings system, profiles and libraries (`dec_manufacturing_definitions`), the toolpath move list and preview overlay, a **job runner** (worker, progress, cancel), plan storage with stable references (`dec_stable_references`), 2D offset and ordering code, the file service. **Per route:** the strategies and the compensation model, and its own parameters. The Emit stage (toolpath to file) is shared and driven by the machine's *dialect* (a `DialectDefinition`: data, so a machine we never heard of needs a new dialect, not new code); `core/gcode.ts` is the G-code writer, and other file writers (SVG and DXF from a toolpath, 3MF) will sit beside it.
9. **Libraries:** permissive ones for geometry primitives (Clipper2, Boost; Manifold, Apache-2.0), our own code for strategies and G-code. Slicer engines (AGPL) and potrace (GPL) are excluded by rule 10, and the human confirmed the licence rules stay: the aim is not to be tied down by licences (`in_547`). Each licence is recorded in `docs/licenses.md` before use. Kiri:Moto (possibly MIT) is only a candidate to check.
10. **Groundwork that is cheap and safe now (done so far: the contract types and the boundary check, `in_562`; layers in SVG and DXF export, `in_563`, `in_564`; the material tag, `in_565`; the shared Emit stage as a G-code writer driven by a dialect, `in_567`; the registry, `in_568`; the part snapshot builder for a flat drawing and the first route, the laser outline route (`dec_laser_outline_route`), `in_569`; not yet: the definition schemas as data, the project's `manufacturing` section, the snapshot of a solid body):** this contract and `inv_manufacturing_boundary`; the definition schemas as types; a material tag on bodies; the reserved `manufacturing` project section; layers (cut, score, engrave) in SVG and DXF export (`dec_export_flat` lists them as not built).
11. **Suggested order** (the human has no single target user, only a wish for a very good CAD program, `in_548`): groundwork; print preparation (`dec_manufacturing_prep`) and print readiness; the beam-cutting route (laser first) with reference-image tracing; the milling route in 2.5D (holes and pockets are already in our graph); more beam processes; derivation routes (moulds); our own FDM G-code last, if ever (slicers are widely available).

12. **How the registry works (`in_568`, built):** `register(route)` adds a route and refuses, with the reason, another contract version, an id already there, no kind of part, or a settings schema that cannot be used (a repeated id, a default outside its own limits or not among its choices); `unregister(id)` takes it away and nothing else of the app knows it; `list` and `get`; `forPart(part, ctx?)` offers the routes that take the part's kind, each with whether and why it fits (a route that does not fit is still listed). `runRoute(route, part, ctx, dialect?)` does the standard stages in the standard order, Fit, Check, Plan, then the shared Emit stage if a dialect is given, and returns the findings, the toolpath, the files and the writer's warnings. **Nothing blocks (`in_546`):** a part that does not fit, a kind of part or a machine of another process, and settings outside their limits are findings, and the route still runs; **a stage that throws is a finding and the run goes on**, so a route cannot crash the app. The registry has no global state: the app makes one and holds it. `Route.fit` takes the context as optional (listing happens before a machine is chosen).

## Rationale

The human wants modules that can be changed or replaced "without too much surgery and risk" and source models that can go down any number of routes (`in_543`). The kernel boundary already shows the method: one data format, one folder, a mechanical check. A plan per part and machine (`in_546`) lets one part have a print and a CNC version. One route per process (`in_550`) keeps differing physics (kerf, material limits, tools) from being squeezed into a shared model that fits none of them; sharing only the plumbing keeps the cost of a new route low. Putting derivations in the graph keeps a mould linked to the bust it came from; keeping plans outside the graph keeps machine choices out of the model's history.

**What "modular" means here, in the human's words (`in_566`):** not entangled with the CAD system, so the kernel can be replaced and a part can be mesh based instead of face based; a clean cut point to add or remove a route; and routes that are **standardized and use generic reusable code wherever possible**, not bespoke, apart from each route's own special parameters. The contract's part (a mesh, STEP optional, flat outlines) assumes no faces; the registry is the cut point; the shared infrastructure of point 8 is where reuse lives.
