# Motion in general: joints, couplings and a driver, solved kinematically, drawn on screen and written to nothing

**Drafted at `in_823`, from the proposal after `in_821`; approved at `in_824`** with its five choices (last section). The human asked whether things in the app can move in general (a gear driving other gears or attached parts, a camshaft driving a piston).

- **A mechanism is joints, couplings and a driver.**
  - **Joints** are what the project already has: the pivots and sliders of coaxial mates, each with a value (a turn in degrees, a slide in mm) and stops (`dec_standing_mates`).
  - **Couplings** tie the value of one joint to another's (or to a body's pose). Each is a small function: **gear pair** (ratio of the teeth, opposite hand; the ring: same hand), **rack and pinion** (slide = pitch radius x turn), **belt or chain** (ratio, same hand), **screw** (slide = lead x turn / 2 pi), the **Geneva** and the **ratchet** (the rules already worked out and tested: the wheel's turn while the pin is in the slot; one way only), **cam and follower** (below). The generators make them: Mesh gear knows both tooth counts; a cam record offers "drive a follower"; a Geneva model comes with its wheel-to-crank rule.
  - **A driver** is a joint marked as driven: a steady speed, a swing between stops, or a value set by a scrubber. Everything coupled to it follows. A chain of couplings is worked out forward from the driver in one pass each tick. A **loop** (a four-bar, a slider-crank) has no forward order and goes to the pose solver the project already has for mates (`solveDriven`, `operation/matesolve.ts`), small and numerical.
- **Cam and follower is a contact coupling**, in the cam's plane: the follower slides along its axis (or swings about a pivot) to the place where it just touches the cam's outline at the cam's angle. For the disc cam the outline is the record's own curve and the lift is the law it was made with; a table of follower position against angle is made once per record (and when its numbers change) and looked up each tick. A roller follower is the follower's circle.
- **Kinematic only.** No forces, gravity, springs, friction, backlash or flex: the same line as `dec_hard_bodies`. A part moves because its joint's value says so.
- **The pose is temporary, and nothing is written.** Play and the scrubber move the **pictures** of the bodies on screen by one matrix each, as dragging a body does now; they make no step, no kernel run, no history entry. Stop puts every joint back to its rest value (what the history says). A **keep this pose** action writes the pose as the ordinary drive steps joints already use (`driveCommand`), one undo.
- **Drawn on screen by a matrix for each moving body** (measured, `prototypes/mechanism-play`, `in_822`): matrices cost under 0.1 ms a frame for 150 bodies; what the frame rate follows is the triangle count, the same as the still model; making new geometry each frame is the thing to avoid (about five times the render call). So the viewer, which now moves **one group with one matrix** (`bodyMatrix`, `bodySplit`), is to keep **one mesh per body** (split once when the kernel answers, from its face ranges) and take **a list of matrices** from the frame.
- **Collision is not part of a tick.** The exact check is a kernel boolean, far too slow per frame. During play at most boxes are compared (and a body that is hard and meets another stops at its last free pose, as in a drag). A **sweep of the whole cycle** is a separate action, run once, that steps the driver through its range and reports where hard bodies meet, with the angle: a clearance check for printing, run off the main thread.
- **Saved.** Drivers and couplings are saved in the file (as relations are), so a mechanism opens as it was left; the pose is not.
- **Built in stages**, each worth having alone:
  1. **Play and scrub for a joint that exists** (a pivot or a slider), no couplings; the per-body matrices in the viewer.
  2. **Gear ratio couplings**: from Mesh gear, rack and pinion, belts, a screw; so one driven gear turns the gears and the parts attached to them (parts mated to a gear's shaft follow it as a body).
  3. **Cam and follower contact, the Geneva and the ratchet** as couplings.
  4. **Loops** through the pose solver, and **the cycle sweep**.
- **Not in this entry:** forces and dynamics of any kind; a motor's torque or a load; flexible parts (the printed spring pawl); measuring speed or acceleration of a point (a later report).

## Built: stage 1 (`in_824`)

**Play and scrub for a joint that exists.** Select the body that is the first of a coaxial mate (a pin in a hole, a slide on a rod) and choose **Play** (Move ring). The joint starts turning (60 degrees a second) and a bar stands at the bottom: play and pause, a slider over the joint's range (its stops, or plus or minus 180 degrees for a turn and 30 mm for a slide when it has none), what it is doing (turned or slid, and how much), a switch between turning and sliding, **Keep pose**, and a cross. A hand on the slider stops the play; Esc and the cross put the joint back; **Keep pose** writes the amount as the ordinary drive step (`driveCommand`, one undo, the mate's record moves to it) and ends the motion; at rest there is nothing to keep (it says so). A joint with no stops that turns goes on turning; any other swings from stop to stop and back. **Nothing is written while it plays:** the history is the same object, no step, no kernel run (tested); the body is drawn by a matrix on the graphics card through the path a joint's handle drag already used (`gpuMotion` in `frame.ts`, `bodyMatrix` and `bodySplit`), the matrix made by `jointMatrix` (`interaction/motion.ts`; tested equal to the app's own picture of the same drive, turning and sliding, both senses). Files: `interaction/motion.ts` (pure: `startMotion`, `amountAt`, `tickMotion`, `setPlaying`, `jointMatrix`), `interaction/control/motion.ts` (play, switch, keep), intents `motionAt`, `motionPlay`, `motionKind`, `motionKeep`, `motionClose`, `AppState.motion`, `Frame.motionBar`, `ui/motionbar.ts`, the clock in `main.ts`, the Play item. Tests: `motion.test.ts` (6). **A correction to the spike's reading:** the viewer already draws **several bodies, each by its own matrix** (`bodyMatrix` for the first and `followers` for the others, `in_341`), made for dragging bodies together; stage 2 uses `followers` for the parts a gear drives, so the viewer needs no new mesh-per-body work. **Not yet in stage 1:** couplings (stage 2), the parts that are attached to the moving body (they stay), a play speed control, playing a joint that no mate makes (a free body).

## Built: stage 2, gears that turn each other (`in_825`)

**Play on a gear's body** now turns every gear meshed with it, each by its own matrix, and slides a rack. **What is stored:** a gear made with the **Mesh gear** tool (or by clicking where it would mesh) records the gear it was meshed with, `GearRecord.meshWith` (the `gear` command takes `meshWith`); gears are coupled along these links. **The couplings** (`operation/gearcouple.ts`, pure; `followersOf`): two outside gears turn opposite ways by the ratio of the teeth, a gear in a ring the same way, a gear and a rack: the rack slides the pinion's pitch radius for each radian it turns (worked from the pitch radius the outline reports, so a helical gear's is its transverse one), and the other way round when the rack is the one played; a chain is worked out by walking from the driver along the links, the ratios multiplied, so each gear goes the other way from the one before; each follower turns about its own plane's normal through its centre (one whose plane faces the other way is accounted for). **The picture:** `frame.ts` `gpuMotion` gives the played gear's matrix and, in `followers`, one matrix for each coupled body (the path that already drew bodies dragged together, `in_341`): no kernel, no history. **The bodies** are found from the steps that made them (`bodyOfGear`: the last step that uses the gear's sides or circle). **Keep pose** (`keepCommand`) sets the record of every gear in the set (`setGear`: the turn, or a rack's centre) in one batch, one undo; the bodies are made again from the records. Tests `gearmotion.test.ts` (5: the ratio and the follower's matrix, a chain and a ring and playing the middle one, a pinion and its rack both ways, through the app with nothing written and keeping the pose, the Mesh gear tool records what it meshed with); looked at in a browser (a 20 and a 30 tooth pair: at half a tooth the small gear has turned half a pitch and the big one half a pitch the other way, still meshed). **Limits:** gears made before this have no `meshWith` and are not coupled (a pair can be meshed again; inferring a pair from the distance between two gears is a later step); a body that a later step changed (a hole cut in it, a fillet) is not found from the gear's outline; **gears whose teeth touch exactly are made one body by the kernel** (a little backlash keeps them apart, as a real printed pair needs); worms, pulleys, sprockets and cams are not coupled yet (`edge` says why); **belts, chains and a screw (the rest of stage 2) are not built.**

## Built: stage 3a, a cam and its follower (`in_826`)

**Select the cam's body and the rod that rides it (the first body of a coaxial mate, its axis in the cam's plane), then Sketch ring → Cam → Follower.** The cam record stores `follower: {mate, side}`. Playing the cam then slides the rod by a table, one number per degree, worked out from the cam's real edge (the line of the rod meets the turned outline; the side is the side the rod's body is on). It is read between whole degrees and round the turn (`followAmount`); Keep pose writes the cam's turn and the rod's slider value in one batch. Select the cam alone and Follower again to let the rod go. **Not built yet:** the Geneva as a coupling (wheel driven by its crank with the nonlinear law) and the ratchet (pawl on the tooth ramp); a follower that is not on a straight line (swing arm, roller offset from the line).

## Built: stage 3b, the Geneva drive as a coupling (`in_827`)

**Play on the crank or the locking disc of a Geneva drive made as a model** (three bodies) turns the crank and the disc together about the second shaft, and the wheel about its own by the real law of the drive (`genevaTurn`): the pin is in a slot while the crank is within 90 - 180/n degrees either side of the line of centres, and there the wheel follows the slot; between visits it stands still, so it makes a step of 360/n degrees for each turn of the crank. The wheel itself cannot be played (it is driven). **Keep pose is refused** for a Geneva (it is one drawing: turn it with Edit cam). **Not built:** the ratchet as a coupling.

## Built: reverse, and planetary sets wired as meshed (`in_829`)

The motion bar has a **⇄ switch**: the speed changes sign and a motion that is playing goes on from where it stands the other way round. **A planetary set** (`Scene.planetaries`) is now known to be meshed: `followersOf` links the sun to each planet and the ring to the first planet, so playing the sun, a planet or the ring turns the others by the tooth ratios with **the carrier held** (planets spin on fixed axes). This works for sets made before too, since the links are derived from the set's record. **The carrier turning** (`in_830`): Play on the carrier body (the plate with the pins) turns it about the sun's axis with **the sun held**; each planet turns `S/P` about its own axis and goes round the centre with the carrier (so `1 + S/P` in the world), the ring turns `1 + S/R` (S, P, R the teeth of sun, planet, ring; `carrierPlay`, `Follower.after`, `followMatrix`). Keep pose is refused for it (turn the set with its own Turn instead). **Not built:** other holds (ring held with the sun driving the carrier, or carrier driven with the ring held).

## Built: the hand-made link (`in_838`, choice 5)

**Select two bodies that each turn on a pin or slide on a rod (the first body of a coaxial mate), then Move ring → Link joints.** The scene keeps `couplings` (`operation/coupling.ts`: `{id, a, b, ka, kb, ratio}`, commands `couple`, `setCoupling`, `deleteCoupling`, saved in the file; the pose is not). The first joint is played at once and the bar shows each link of the joint played: a **ratio box** (the linked joint moves this much for each degree or mm of this one; a link stored the other way round shows the inverse), a **turns / slides** switch for what the other end does (a turn that moves a slide is mm for a degree), and a way to take it away. Chains multiply; a joint played as a turn and as a slide has its own links. **Keep pose** drives every linked joint too, each worked out on the scene the one before leaves. **Not built:** links to gears or cams (those have their own couplings), a link made by hand between a joint and a gear; a ratio that is not constant (a crank-slider or a linkage needs a solver).

## Built: any body can be made to turn (`in_940`, `in_941`)

**Move ring → Pivot** gives a body an axis of its own, with no partner body and no mate: `Scene.pivots` (`operation/pivot.ts`: `{body (its id), point, dir}`, one to a body; commands `pivot` and `deletePivot`; saved in the file). The axis comes from the first of: **a rotate that is open on the body** (its pivot, where the orb has been put, and the axis it is turning about, `axisVec`), or **a round face of a body that is selected** (the face's axis). The rotate is closed (nothing is turned) and the body plays at once; **Stop turning** takes the pivot away. A pivoted body has the play button on hover (`animatedBodies`), plays by the bar (speed, reverse, scrub), and when a hard body is in the mechanism it can **yield** to what pushes it (`dec_contact_push`). Keep pose is not available for it yet (it stays where it is drawn). **Limits:** a model of a single body has no name for its body (the stand-in `b/body/o`), so it cannot be given a pivot until there are two bodies; sliding (a pivot that slides) is not built. **The AI's answer to the question of whether special animation linkages are still needed:** yes as an invisible fast path (long trains, belts and screws that do not touch, exact ratios), not as something the user sets up; the user meets two ideas, *this turns* and *hard*.

## The five choices (approved at `in_824`)

1. **The pose is view only** (nothing written until **keep this pose**), not a committed motion.
2. **Kinematic only**: no forces, springs or gravity.
3. **Drivers and couplings are saved in the file**; the pose is not.
4. **The first slice is stages 1 and 2**: play and scrub for a joint, and gear ratio couplings (so a gear drives gears and attached parts).
5. **Couplings are made by the generators** (Mesh gear and the others offer them), with a way to make one by hand between two joints and a ratio.

## Rationale

Every part of this exists in some form already: joints with values and stops, a pose solver for loops, collision that stops a body, records that know their numbers, and a way to move a body's picture without the kernel. What is missing is the **tie between joints**, **time**, and **one matrix for each body**; keeping the pose off the history keeps motion cheap (measured) and keeps the model what it was. Kinematic only keeps the scope the size of the existing collision decision, and nearly everything a person prints and wants to see move (gear trains, cams, Geneva and ratchet drives, linkages) is kinematic.
