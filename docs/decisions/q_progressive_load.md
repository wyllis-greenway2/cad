# Open a big model by showing the whole of it at once as ghosts and filling in detail from the camera outwards

**The human's idea (`in_990`):** do not load everything at once; put the person in the scene at once; fill in models and data from the camera backwards; keep the interface smooth after the load; draw what is near in full and the rest as ghosts (as a gear is a ghost disc while it is made); give detail and fidelity by camera position and selection.

**What it costs now (measured, `q_limits_and_failures`):** opening replays every command and derives the whole scene after each (the page frozen 4.4 s at 300 parts, 33 s at 1000, 441 MB); the kernel then builds all bodies in one answer (12.7 s at 300 parts, over 60 s past about 600, 3.8 GB at 961 bodies); the page then holds about 10 scene parts for every face of every body (104 thousand parts for four wheels), and the kernel describes every body in full. Nothing of the model is drawn until the one answer comes.

**Proposal (the AI's, in phases; each is checked on its own by the numbers above)**

*Phase 0, small, first: stop the replay being the freeze.* Open applies all the commands and derives **once at the end** (snapshots become lean at once, `history.ts`), and in slices between frames with a progress sign if it is still long; keep the last good scene when a step cannot be made; keep a copy of the project in the browser as it is worked; refuse a step before the kernel's memory reaches the crash. Expected: the 33 s freeze to a few seconds; no blank model; a crash that does not lose work.

*Phase 1: the ghost first.* Every body that a step makes from a sketch and a depth has a **ghost drawn from the commands alone** (the sketch's outline, the depth, its place: a box or prism, as the gear's disc is drawn from its record): the whole model is in view at once, in the camera's place, in tens of milliseconds, drawn as one batch. The kernel then **builds the bodies in an order the camera gives** (selected, then in view and near, then in view far, then the rest), **one body or a few at a time**, and each answer replaces its ghost. This needs the kernel to answer in pieces: a body that no later step touches (a push or a revolve in "new" mode, a pattern copy, a gear) is independent and can be made, kept and described alone (the per-body keys of `dec_per_body_checks` already say which); a chain of joins and cuts is built together, in order. The 60 s limit then applies to a piece, not to the model.

*Phase 2: fidelity by distance and selection.* The mesh of a body is made as fine as its size on the screen needs (the kernel already makes a rough "draft" mesh and a coarser one for heavy bodies): far bodies coarse or as their ghost, merged into few draw calls; the body being looked at or selected full, with its faces, edges and corners (the parts for picking) made **when it is hovered or selected** and not for every body (the gear feature already does this for a gear); far bodies get no edge lines. Moving the camera re-orders the work; work no longer needed is dropped.

*Phase 3: a kernel that does not hold everything.* The kernel keeps the solids it needs (the near ones, the selected) and lets the rest go, making them again when the camera comes back, so the 4 GB is no longer the size of the model; the answers kept by the page (the meshes) are bounded by a budget, as the descriptions are.

**What is not simple:** a change to an early step still invalidates every later one (the quadratic edit cost: it is only as cheap as the independent bodies are independent); bodies that touch (joins, cuts, fillets between them) must be made together; picking, measuring and clearance checks need the true shape and wait for the body (as they wait for the kernel now); the draw-call and triangle limits of a phone are not measured here. **Recommendation (the AI's):** Phase 0 now (small and certain), then Phase 1 as a prototype in `prototypes/` on the 300 and 1000 part projects to see how much of the time to the first picture and to the last body it takes.

## Safe interaction while it is loading, caching, and layers (`in_991`)

**The rule that makes it safe: the history is the truth, and what is drawn is a cache of it.** Every command is kept as it is whatever has been built; saving, undo, redo and every number the person changes act on the commands and on the page's own solver (sketches, constraints, dimensions, patterns, gear numbers: all worked out in the page, not in the kernel). Only what needs the **true shape** waits for it. So interacting early cannot change what the model *is*, only what is drawn of it now.

**1. Load the relationships first.** They are cheap and in the page: which step uses which parts (`step.parts`, the target chain), the constraints, the patterns and gears, the moves and turns, and from the ghosts' boxes **which bodies may touch** (a conservative test: the boxes of two bodies that do not meet cannot be joined, cut or collide; boxes that meet are treated as possibly joined and are built together). From this, before the kernel has made anything: the **groups** of bodies that can be built alone and in any order, the **chains** that must be built in order, and for every step what it **needs**.

**2. Readiness of a body, and what each thing needs.** A body is at a level: *ghost* (from the commands alone) < *coarse* (a rough mesh) < *full* (the screen mesh) < *named* (faces, edges, corners made: pickable) < *exact* (for export and measuring). A table of every operation and the level it needs of the bodies it touches (to be written from the code, one row for each): move, turn, hide, colour, delete, change a size, undo, save: **ghost** is enough (they act on commands and boxes); push or pull a face, fillet or chamfer an edge, pick a face or an edge, join or cut: **named** (and the bodies they meet at **full**); measure, clearance, export: **exact**. A thing that needs more than is there is **not refused and not guessed**: it is greyed with "building this part…", and the body is raised to the front of the order (selecting a body is the wish for its detail); done when it is ready, if the person still wants it. Nothing is ever applied to geometry that is only a guess.

**3. Answers are checked, not trusted.** Every piece the kernel returns carries the key of what it was made from (the commands of its own dependency chain and the kernel's stamp, as the answers kept now do); a piece whose key is not the current one is dropped. A step that fails when its body is finally made is marked as a failed step, as now (`outcome.errors`, the take-back of the last step), and the steps that stood on it are marked blocked: the person is told, and nothing is lost, because the commands are all there.

**4. How we would be sure (tests before any screen):** (a) **differential**: the same project made in one go, made body by body in random orders, and made with random edits (a size changed, a body moved, an undo) in the middle must end with the **same volumes, the same face names and the same errors**; (b) **fuzz**: scripted random actions at random moments of a load, checked for exceptions, for the history being the commands given, and for the end being the one-shot's; (c) **invariants in the code**: an operation asks for the level it needs and asserts it (a failed assertion is a bug, not a state); (d) the existing tests run in the progressive mode as well as the one-shot.

**5. Caching, in layers, each keyed by what it was made from (so a stale one cannot be taken):** the browser's own cache of the kernel's 23 MB file (a deployment setting; the name carries its hash); **the project's derived scene and the relationships** kept with it or beside it (the open then does no replay at all); **the kernel's answers kept now, but one body at a time**, by the body's own dependency chain and the kernel's stamp (`dec_description_cache` keeps whole answers: a project with one body changed finds none); **the meshes at each level** (coarse and full) per body, so that an open is the ghosts, then the cached coarse meshes at once, then the rest; the compiled code of the kernel (the browser's own, when it is served as it should be). A cache entry is only ever the answer to the same question.

**6. Layers and their order.** *L0 ghost* (page only: every body's box or prism from its sketch and depth, a gear as its disc) for all bodies at once, drawn as one batch; *L1 coarse* (a draft mesh, or a cached one); *L2 full*; *L3 named* (faces, edges, corners, only for the body looked at or selected, and for what a step in hand needs); *L4 exact* (on demand). **The order of work:** a body is made after the bodies it is joined to (dependency, from 1), and among bodies that are free of each other by the camera: selected, in view and near (by the size it has on the screen), in view and far, out of view; each time the camera or the selection moves the order is made again and what is no longer wanted is dropped. Two or three kernel threads could take free bodies side by side (four cores here; each has its own memory, so the total needs care).

**Recommended first steps (nothing built):** (1) write the table of operations and their levels from the code (a document, a day's reading); (2) the **differential test harness** with the kernel as it is (a project built in pieces in random orders must equal the one-shot: this tells how independent the bodies really are, before any screen is touched); (3) Phase 0 of the first section; (4) then the ghost layer and the gating, on a branch.

## First steps done (`in_992`)

1. **The table of what each thing needs** is written: `docs/progressive-levels.md` (every command, what the person does with the view, what a ghost needs, from the code).
2. **The differential harness** is built (`prototypes/progressive`) and run: groups of independent steps built alone and in other orders give the same bodies, volumes, boxes and face names (17 groups, 17 bodies, 6 other orders, no difference on five made assemblies; the two pattern grids among the 70 fixtures are one group once a pattern is seen to use its source: the first, wrong grouping built the pattern without its source and the harness found that at once: 162 differences). **Not covered yet:** groups with modifying steps, revolves, lofts and sweeps, a body that moves, edits in the middle of a load. Still open: the rest.

**Extended (`in_996`):** parts with fillets, holes and moves, and ten fixtures of several bodies, built in groups alone and in other orders: **no difference** (20 groups, 27 orders, 30 bodies) after a fix the test found (a body moved among others lost the names of the faces across its motion: `facenames.mjs` `carried`, `movenames.test.ts`). A page-side rule for what a step changes (by name, by place, by relation, by box) covered 52 of 65 steps (48 exactly, 4 by saying "every body so far"); the 13 it missed were one fixture in which a body is moved through another's place: **until the ghost keeps every body's box at every step, a project with moves of that kind is one group**, and the rule is a check of the harness rather than a promise. **Phase 0 is built** (`dec_open_and_survive`).

**Phase 1 prototyped (`in_998`, `prototypes/progressive-load`, Node, the real kernel, parts apart):** ghosts of every part **from the saved commands alone** after **6 ms (300 parts) and 22 ms (1000)**, where the replay of the commands into a scene is 1.3 s and 6.3 s; the nearest parts made in 0.1 to 0.3 s whatever the size; **piece by piece the cost is a line** (about 12 ms a part): 100 parts 1.6 s (the whole 2.6 s), 300 parts 4.3 s (the whole **21 s**), **1000 parts 12 s where the whole in one go is past the 60 s limit**; bodies and volumes are the whole's. Not yet measured: the page taking each answer into the scene, the memory over a thousand pieces, parts that touch (one group), the browser with the thread beside the page. **Recommendation (the AI's):** the numbers say Phase 1 is worth building; the next step is a decision entry and the smallest piece of it in the app (the ghost layer drawn from the commands at once, the kernel asked for one group at a time in the camera's order), measured in the browser first.

**Phase 1, the smallest slice, built (`in_999`, `dec_progressive_open`):** a model of 24 or more pushes of sketches of their own, apart, is made in pieces nearest the eye first with a box for each part not made yet and "Building N of M parts…"; in the built app 300 parts 4.4 s (12.9 to 17.5 s before), 1000 parts 14.6 s (past the limit before); an edit during the build stops it and is made. **Still open:** ghosts before the replay, touching parts, parts with modifying steps, gears, picking a ghost, the memory over thousands of pieces, lazy faces and edges (Phase 2), the kernel that does not hold everything (Phase 3).

## Rationale

The human asked how to make big models fast and tolerant of their size; this records the direction and its parts so that the order is theirs to choose. Nothing is built.
