# A Geneva drive: the slotted wheel and its crank, as one more shape of the cam tool

**Approved at `in_782`** as recommended (the geometry is fixed by the slot count, so it needs no unsourced standard). A Geneva drive turns a wheel one slot's step for each turn of a crank: a pin on the crank enters a slot, carries the wheel round, and leaves.

- **The geometry (exact, not a convention).** `n` slots; the crank's pin circle has radius `a`. The pin must enter each slot straight, so at the moment it enters, the line from the crank's centre to the pin is square to the slot: the centres are `C = a / sin(π / n)` apart, and the pin enters at `b = C cos(π / n)` from the wheel's centre. The wheel turns `360 / n` degrees while the crank turns `180 - 360 / n` either side of the line of centres; the pin's nearest approach to the wheel's centre is `C - a`. (`n` from 3 to 24.)
- **The wheel.** A disc of radius `b + pin diameter` with `n` radial slots, each of width pin + 2 × clearance, going in to a semicircular end where the pin is nearest the centre, and the bore. With a locking disc (the default) the rim between two slots has a **concave locking arc**, a circle about the disc's centre a clearance bigger than the disc, taking just over half of the room between the slots' mouths.
- **The locking disc** (built, `in_788`). It keeps the wheel from turning between the pin's visits: its rim sits in the wheel's concave arc, and it is **cut away where the wheel would meet it while the pin is in the slot**. The cut is not a formula but the envelope of the turning wheel (`discRadii` in `operation/geneva.ts`): for each of 720 directions from the disc's centre, how far the disc may reach before the wheel is met at any of 90 moments of the visit, less the clearance and half the most a point of the wheel's rim moves between two of those moments; each corner of the wheel is a circle of that margin the disc keeps out of, each side a line it keeps that margin from. The radii are then **smoothed without ever being made bigger** (`smoothUnder`: each is the average of the lowest values within 4 degrees of each place within 4 degrees of it, so the scallops that the wheel's separate positions leave in the notch's edge are gone, and the notch is a little wider, never narrower; found by the human, `in_798`). The disc is then as full as the wheel lets it be. Its outline is one closed spline through knots (`fitKnots`, within 0.02 mm), drawn beside the crank. **Lock** is the disc's radius (worked out if left; 0: no locking disc and a plain round rim). Refusals: a disc radius that does not meet the wheel's rim, or so big that its arc runs into a slot's mouth, or a notch that comes too near the shaft's hole. **Hole** left empty is worked out: as big as the room allows, up to a quarter of the crank radius (for few slots the room is small: the wheel's rim passes close to the disc's shaft). Checked by simulation (`geneva.test.ts`): over a whole turn of the crank the wheel and the disc never overlap, and a turn of the wheel either way while locked runs it into the disc.
- **A quick picture while typing** (`in_789`). Working out the notch takes most of a second (`discRadii`), so the box's picture uses `camShapesQuick` (`cams.ts`; `genevaShapes` with `quick`): the same wheel and crank, the locking disc a plain circle of its radius in the notch's place, and the hole worked out without the notch. A number typed costs a frame of a few milliseconds; Enter makes the real drive (and the refusals that need the notch, such as a hole too near it, come then, in the message line). The real shapes are kept by their numbers, so the picture uses them when they are already worked out.
- **The crank.** A plate with a hub (round, with the shaft's hole) and an arm of the pin's width out to the pin's circle. **Drawn beside the wheel, not on it**: in a working Geneva the crank is in a layer under the wheel, so drawn in place it would lie over the wheel; the drawing therefore puts it below the wheel, centred directly under the wheel's centre, to be moved to the centre distance `C` (shown in the box) from the wheel's centre when assembling.
- **Numbers (the box).** Slots (4), Crank (the pin circle's radius `a`, set by the pointer's distance from the wheel's shaft), Pin (diameter, a quarter of `a`), Clearance (mm each side of the pin in the slot, 0.3), Hole (the shaft's hole in both, a quarter of `a`). The defaults other than the geometry above are conventions, not sources; a slot that would reach the shaft's hole, or slots that would meet, is refused with the reason (few slots with a big pin).
- **Three layouts** (built, `in_799`; the Cam > **Geneva** submenu: **Apart**, **Assembled**, **3D model**; `CamSpec.layout`): **Apart** is the sketch above, the crank and the locking disc beside the wheel. **Assembled** is a sketch of the assembly in place: the wheel at the shaft, the locking disc and the crank on the second shaft a centre distance away along +x, the crank's pin pointing away from the wheel, the pose the wheel is locked in (the figures overlap on the plane, as the layers of a real drive do in plan). **3D model** is that, made into **three bodies** by steps the record owns (as a worm's are, `wormSteps`; `camSteps` in `camscene.ts`): the wheel and the locking disc each `Layer` mm up from the plane (6 by default), and under them the crank plate `Plate` mm down from it (3), with its pin (a circle at the end of the crank, `.k`) extruded up the layer's height and joined to the plate (`mode: "add"`), so it stands up through the notch into the wheel's slot. The box adds **Layer** and **Plate** for the model; Edit cam reads them back and a change of any number changes the bodies by the same steps; deleting the record takes them with it. Looked at in a browser: three bodies, no page errors; tested for the bodies' heights (`geneva.test.ts`).
- **Kept as a record**, as the cams and the ratchet are; Edit cam changes its numbers.
- **Not yet:** an internal Geneva, a Geneva with more than one pin, the model turned to any pose or played (a turn, a play button).

## Rationale

A Geneva drive is a shape of a few numbers whose one relation, the centre distance, is exact; the slice is the wheel and the crank, which a person can print and run (the wheel is held by a detent or by friction until the locking disc is built). Drawing the crank beside the wheel avoids a sketch whose figures lie over each other (they would be cut into pieces by every crossing), at the cost of one move when assembling.
