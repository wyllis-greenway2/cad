# The laser outline route: the first manufacturing route

`in_569` asked for the laser outline route, the first of `dec_manufacturing_prep`'s v1. **Built; the details are the AI's choices (provisional until the human accepts them).**

- **What it takes:** a flat drawing (a sketch, or a flat face of a body) as the contract's part (`interaction/partsnapshot.ts`, the snapshot builder: the one place that knows the scene and turns it into plain data). The drawing's lines keep their layer (*cut*, *score*, *engrave*, `in_563`).
- **The route** (`manufacturing/routes/laser-outline/`): `fit` (a flat drawing with lines in it; its size in its own plane against the machine's work area), `check` (findings) and `plan` (the toolpath). It has no `emit`: the file is written by the shared stage from a dialect (`in_567`). The geometry is worked out once, in `prepare.ts`, so `plan` and `check` cannot disagree.
- **Cut lines:** pieces that meet are joined into loops (`core/outline.ts`); a loop inside another is a hole (even-odd nesting); with a **kerf** the path of an outside edge is moved out by half of it and a hole's in by half (`core/offset.ts`: mitred corners, so the part's sharp corners stay sharp), so the part comes out the size it was drawn; with **no kerf and no tabs lines and arcs stay lines and arcs** (a circle is two half arcs); **tabs** are bridges left on each outside edge (a number and a width, evenly round it); everything is cut **inside before outside, nearest next**, a loop started at the vertex nearest the head (`core/order.ts`); **passes** repeat the cut.
- **Score and engrave lines** are followed on the line, once, at their own power and speed.
- **Findings (advice, never a block):** a line to cut that does not close; a feature too small for the kerf (cut on its drawn line instead); a wall between two cut lines thinner than the kerf; tabs that do not fit; tabs asked for with no closed outside edge; nothing marked to cut; a drawing bigger than the machine's work area (`fit`); settings out of their limits (the registry).
- **Settings** are declared as a schema (kerf, cut power, speed and passes, tabs and tab width, score and engrave power and speed, part to the corner). **The numbers are only places to start:** power and speed depend on the laser, the lens and the material, and the help says to test on a scrap.
- **In the app:** the ⋯ menu's **Laser cut…** row opens a dialog **made from the route's own schema** (so any later route gets one the same way), then runs the route on the selected sketch (or all that is drawn); the findings are read in a "Before you cut" step before the file is saved; the file is plain G-code.
- **The dialect is a generic starting point, not an official one; the machine is the user's own:** a work area and a power scale the user types and can keep, and plain GRBL-style words (G21 G90 G0 G1 G2 G3, `M4 S` power, M5, M2). Marked `user`, with the note that the first lines are to be read against the controller's manual. Official ones wait for a cited source (`dec_manufacturing_definitions` point 10).
- **Offsetting is our own code,** not Clipper2: `clipper2-js` (Boost) was tried and gave wrong offsets for a plain square (recorded in `docs/licenses.md`).

**The preview (`in_570`, built):** the toolpath is shown over the drawing in the 3D view, with the same camera and world: cut lines red, score blue and engrave orange (the colours of their marks), travel pale grey, what is done in full colour and what is still to come faint, a red dot where the head is, and a scrubber under it (a slider, how far along by length, the estimated time, a cross). It opens when the file is made (before the findings are read) and stays after the file is saved; Esc or the cross ends it. It is **shared, not the laser's:** the toolpath carries a `frame` (where its zero, x and normal are in the world), `interaction/toolpathview.ts` flattens any toolpath (arcs in five-degree steps) into lines by position, and `ui/previewbar.ts` and `view.setToolpath` draw them, so every later route gets its preview with no code of its own.

**Saved machines and settings (`in_571`, built):** the dialog asks which machine (the project's copy, the user's own, or a new one), starts from the machine's fields and the plan's settings for this drawing, and the project keeps a copy of the machine and the plan (`dec_manufacturing_definitions` point 11); the machine's name, work area, power scale and rapid speed are fields of the dialog.

**Nesting (`in_572`, `in_560`, built):** several parts on a sheet, in two ways. **Manual layout** (nesting *off*, the usual): the parts stay where they are drawn, the person moves them with the tools they have, and the toolpath preview draws **the sheet's edge** (the machine's work area, or a sheet size given, from the toolpath's zero: with *part to the corner* off that is the drawing's own zero, so the layout can be arranged against it) and the route **checks the layout**: a part outside the sheet, two parts that overlap (a plate wholly inside another is a hole in it, not an overlap; two that only cross overlap), two parts closer than the *gap* (advice). **Packing** (nesting *on*): the drawing is split into parts (a closed outline with the holes in it and the score and engrave lines inside it; a loop inside a hole is a part of its own), each part and each of its *copies* is taken as its **bounding box**, and the boxes are packed in rows on the sheet (`core/nest.ts`: tallest first, the row with the least width left over, a new row if none, a gap between, a margin at the edge), each box turned a quarter turn when allowed and when that is what lets it in; a part that does not fit is left out and said. Packing is the plan's, not the model's: the drawing is not changed, and the preview shows the packed layout over it (the toolpath's frame is at the drawing's corner). Settings: nesting (off or pack), copies, gap, margin, turn to fit, sheet width and height (0: the work area). **Not built:** true-shape nesting (the parts' own outlines, `in_560`: later); moving parts on the sheet in the preview with the pointer; a part packed into the holes of another; keeping the same part orientation (grain) when turning is allowed; the order of cutting across parts (each part's holes are cut before its outside, nearest next, but the parts are cut in the order of the nearest, not part by part).

**Not built:** the playing of the preview as a moving animation (only the slider); named settings presets; a power and speed table by material; lead-ins and lead-outs; cut order within a depth beyond nearest-next engrave fills and raster images; SVG or DXF written from the toolpath; a solid body or a body's face as the part's source for kerf (only a flat face's outline); a tool-radius offset for a router (the offset code is shared and ready); a check that a hole or a part is not inside a hole; features smaller than the offset on concave corners are caught only by a distance check, not repaired.

## Rationale

A laser outline is the cheapest toolpath (2D polygons with kerf, tabs and an order), it covers the largest hobbyist group the brainstorm found underserved, and it proves the whole chain at once: the snapshot builder, the contract, the registry, the shared Emit stage, findings and a settings dialog made from a schema. Keeping the geometry in `core/` (loops, offset, order) is the human's wish for generic reusable code (`in_566`): a router's profile, a plasma or a waterjet will use the same three files and add only their own compensation model and parameters.
