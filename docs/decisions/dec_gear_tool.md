# A gear tool: one generator, many tooth profiles

**Approved (`in_544`)** as proposed after `in_543`, except nothing per manufacturing route (`prin_manufacture_agnostic`).

- **One generator, many profiles.** A tooth profile gives one pitch of outline from a few numbers; the tool repeats it round the axis, and the outline is drawn as an ordinary figure of lines and arcs on a plane (a flat figure first). Thickness is the normal extrude; a bore, hub, spokes and keyway are the normal tools. New profiles are new generators, not new tools.
- **Inputs:** tooth count and module (metric tooth size); pitch diameter is module times teeth and is shown; the outside diameter can stand in for the module. Also pressure angle (20 degrees by default), profile shift, backlash (nominal 0), clearance (0.25 module).
- **Order:** involute spur gear first (external and internal), then rack; timing pulleys and roller-chain sprockets from published tables; cycloidal (clock) teeth; helical and herringbone by twisting the same outline (`dec_spiral_extrude`). Later and different in kind: bevel and worm gears, a planetary layout helper, differentials (assemblies).
- **No library:** the maths is public (ISO 53, DIN 867) and written here; gear libraries are GPL or LGPL.
- **Built (first slice, `in_544`):** `operation/gear.ts` makes the outline of an external or an internal (ring) involute gear as points and bulges (each side of a tooth is a short chain of arcs through points of the true involute, checked to a thousandth of the module; the volume of the extruded body agrees with the exact area). The **Gear** tool (Sketch ring): click the centre, the pointer sets the outside size or type the numbers; the outline is selected as a figure, ready to extrude.
- **Built (`in_545`): a gear keeps its numbers, and rings are in the tool.** The scene holds a record (`Scene.gears`: plane, centre, numbers; `operation/gearscene.ts`) and the gear's corners, sides (and a ring's wall circle) are made from it each time the scene is derived, as the copies of a repeat are: locked, named by the gear and their place in the outline (`d/g_003.s17`), not saved (the file holds the command). Commands `gear`, `setGear`, `deleteGear`. Changing the numbers changes the bodies made from the gear: when the count of sides changes, a step that names the whole outline (as its figure, a hole, a sibling) is given the new sides; a numbers change that would take away a corner or side a constraint stands on is refused with the reason; numbers that make no gear are refused with the reason and the gear stays. **Edit gear** (Sketch ring, enabled with a gear selected) opens the same number box on it: Teeth, Module, Pressure (degrees), Shift (a gear) or Wall (a ring, in modules), Backlash (mm), typed or Tab between; Enter applies and puts the tool away, Esc leaves it. **Ring gear** (Sketch ring) draws a ring: the pointer sets the outside of the wall. Delete on a selected gear takes the record and parts whole.
- **Built (`in_546`): a bore, and moving a gear.** The bore is a number of the gear (`spec.bore`, diameter in mm, a field of the box for an outside gear, none for a ring): a circle in the middle, made from the record, that an extrude takes as a hole. A bore put in or taken out after a body was made from the gear is added to or taken from that step's holes; a bore as wide as the root circle is refused. Moving: **Move** and **Rotate** on a selected gear change its record (`setGear` with a centre and a `turn`, one command, one undo, fixed constraints on its corners let go as for any figure); the bodies made from it follow. A gear stays in its plane: a move across the plane, or a turn about another axis, is not made.
- **Built (`in_547`): the rack and the meshing helper.** A **rack** (`spec.rack`; `operation/gear.ts` `rackOutline`) is a straight bar with straight-sided teeth at the pressure angle, `n` of them, π·m apart, the middle of its pitch line at the record's centre, teeth up, `rim` modules of bar under the roots (box: Teeth, Module, Pressure, Backlash, Base); it is made, edited, moved, turned, extruded and deleted as a gear is. **Mesh gear** and **Mesh rack** (Sketch ring, enabled with a gear, ring or rack selected; `operation/mesh.ts`, `meshWith`) make a partner that fits the selected one: a gear beside a gear (centre distance m·(z₁+z₂)/2), a gear inside a ring (m·(z_ring−z)/2, fewer teeth than the ring), a rack on a gear's pitch circle, a gear on a rack; the partner takes the module, pressure angle and backlash of the one it meshes with and is turned so a tooth of one is in a gap of the other (the phase rules are in the file; checked by outlines that do not run into each other, and that do when half a pitch out). The pointer says where it goes (the direction of the partner from the gear; how far along a rack), or type Teeth, Ratio (partner teeth over the gear's) and Angle (degrees) or Offset (mm). Refused: a gear with a profile shift (its centre distance is not the plain one), a rack against a rack or a ring, a gear as big as its ring.
- **Messages (`in_548`):** a gear, ring, rack or partner that is not made says why in the message line when Enter or a click makes nothing (`gearProblem`: too few teeth, a pressure angle that is no angle, teeth too thin at the tips, a gear as big as its ring, a rack against a ring or a rack, a shifted gear); the tool stays on so the number can be changed. A change to a gear that is already made is refused by the command with its reason, as before.
- **Built (`in_551`): GT2 pulleys.** `GearSpec.pulley` (`"2GT"` or `"3GT"`; `operation/pulley.ts`) makes the outline of a timing pulley for the Gates GT 2 mm or 3 mm belt: `teeth` grooves (12 to 400), the outside diameter pitch diameter less 2 × PLD (20 grooves of 2GT: 12.22 mm, as Pfeifer's and Gates' charts), each groove the belt tooth (three arcs, R3 tip, R2 sides, R1 corners, from the belt table in `q_timing_pulleys`) made `clearance` mm bigger all round (0 to 0.13), a bore. The **GT2 pulley** and **3GT pulley** tools (Sketch ring; box Teeth, Clearance, Bore) make it; it is edited, moved, turned, extruded and deleted like a gear; a gear is not meshed to it (a belt is). The groove is the belt's tooth, not the pulley makers' own groove drawing: it is to be checked against a real belt. HTD and the other belts are not built (`q_timing_pulleys`).
- **Built (`in_552`): roller chain sprockets.** `GearSpec.sprocket` (`{ chain?, pitch?, roller? }`; `operation/sprocket.ts`) makes the outline of a sprocket with the ANSI B29.1 tooth form for the chain's pitch P and roller diameter Dr and `teeth` N (7 to 400): pitch diameter P / sin(180/N), outside diameter P (0.6 + cot(180/N)); the gap is the seating curve (R = 0.5025 Dr + 0.0015 in, A = 35 + 60/N degrees either side of the bottom), the working curve (E = 1.3025 Dr + 0.0015 in about a point 0.8 Dr off, B = 18 - 56/N degrees, touching the seat on the inside), a flat of length Dr (1.4 sin C - 0.8 sin B) (C = 17 - 64/N) and the topping curve (F = Dr (0.8 cos B + 1.4 cos C - 1.3025) - 0.0015 in), the tooth cut at the blank's outside diameter or, when the pure tooth comes to a point below it (most counts), left pointed. **Sources:** the construction and constants are TNB Positioners' ANSI B29.1 generator page (https://tnbpositioners.com/tools/sprocket/), which says it reproduces the American Chain Association's worked example and a commercial #40 of 18 teeth (working radius 0.4079 in, topping 0.2582 in), and which agree with the Machinery's Handbook table quoted in a search (seating radius 0.5025 Dr + 0.0015, A = 35 + 60/N, B = 18 - 56/N); the code reproduces its numbers (the topping curve's centre is exactly 1.4 Dr from the roller's centre; E and F of the #40 / 18 teeth to four decimals). **Chains** (inches, Martin Sprocket's catalogue "Chain Data For All Sprockets", https://www.martinsprocket.com/docs/catalogs/engineering/engineering%20catalog/sprocket-engineering-data.pdf, who conform to the Type II tooth form of ANSI B29.1-1975): #25 .250 / .130, #35 .375 / .200, #41 .500 / .306, #40 .500 / .312, #50 .625 / .400, #60 .750 / .469, #80 1.000 / .625, #100 1.250 / .750, #120 1.500 / .875 (pitch / roller diameter); a chain of one's own is typed by its pitch and roller diameter (mm). The **Sprocket** tool (Sketch ring; box Teeth, Chain, Pitch, Roller, Bore) makes it; a chain number there is none of says so. Bicycle chains (1/2 x 1/8 in, 12.7 mm pitch) are not in the table (their roller diameter was not read from a source): type the pitch and roller diameter. The sprocket's width is the thickness it is extruded to; the table's single strand tooth width is in `operation/sprocket.ts`. Not made: ISO 606 tooth forms, the chordal rise.
- **Not built yet:** HTD, MXL, T-series pulleys, ISO 606 sprockets, a clearance field in the box, root fillet (the root is the root circle between the flanks), the undercut shape (only a warning is computed), snapping a bore to the gear's centre (no centre corner is kept: `dec_no_stray_corners`; a ring has its wall's centre), rack, pulleys, sprockets, cycloidal, helical.

## Rationale

The involute is the one profile of machined gears that keeps a constant speed ratio and forgives centre-distance error; the rest of the list are the profiles the human named. Drawing the outline as ordinary parts means export, extrude, hole cutting, mirroring and the flat SVG/DXF export all work on it without special cases.
