# A gear tool: one generator, many tooth profiles

**Approved (`in_576`)** as proposed after `in_575`, except nothing per manufacturing route (`prin_manufacture_agnostic`).

- **One generator, many profiles.** A tooth profile gives one pitch of outline from a few numbers; the tool repeats it round the axis, and the outline is drawn as an ordinary figure of lines and arcs on a plane (a flat figure first). Thickness is the normal extrude; a bore, hub, spokes and keyway are the normal tools. New profiles are new generators, not new tools.
- **Inputs:** tooth count and module (metric tooth size); pitch diameter is module times teeth and is shown; the outside diameter can stand in for the module. Also pressure angle (20 degrees by default), profile shift, backlash (nominal 0), clearance (0.25 module).
- **Order:** involute spur gear first (external and internal), then rack; timing pulleys and roller-chain sprockets from published tables; cycloidal (clock) teeth; helical and herringbone by twisting the same outline (`dec_spiral_extrude`). Later and different in kind: bevel and worm gears, a planetary layout helper, differentials (assemblies).
- **No library:** the maths is public (ISO 53, DIN 867) and written here; gear libraries are GPL or LGPL.
- **Built (first slice, `in_576`):** `operation/gear.ts` makes the outline of an external or an internal (ring) involute gear as points and bulges (each side of a tooth is a short chain of arcs through points of the true involute, checked to a thousandth of the module; the volume of the extruded body agrees with the exact area). The **Gear** tool (Sketch ring): click the centre, the pointer sets the outside size or type the numbers; the outline is selected as a figure, ready to extrude.
- **Built (`in_577`): a gear keeps its numbers, and rings are in the tool.** The scene holds a record (`Scene.gears`: plane, centre, numbers; `operation/gearscene.ts`) and the gear's corners, sides (and a ring's wall circle) are made from it each time the scene is derived, as the copies of a repeat are: locked, named by the gear and their place in the outline (`d/g_003.s17`), not saved (the file holds the command). Commands `gear`, `setGear`, `deleteGear`. Changing the numbers changes the bodies made from the gear: when the count of sides changes, a step that names the whole outline (as its figure, a hole, a sibling) is given the new sides; a numbers change that would take away a corner or side a constraint stands on is refused with the reason; numbers that make no gear are refused with the reason and the gear stays. **Edit gear** (Sketch ring, enabled with a gear selected) opens the same number box on it: Teeth, Module, Pressure (degrees), Shift (a gear) or Wall (a ring, in modules), Backlash (mm), typed or Tab between; Enter applies and puts the tool away, Esc leaves it. **Ring gear** (Sketch ring) draws a ring: the pointer sets the outside of the wall. Delete on a selected gear takes the record and parts whole.
- **Built (`in_578`): a bore, and moving a gear.** The bore is a number of the gear (`spec.bore`, diameter in mm, a field of the box for an outside gear, none for a ring): a circle in the middle, made from the record, that an extrude takes as a hole. A bore put in or taken out after a body was made from the gear is added to or taken from that step's holes; a bore as wide as the root circle is refused. Moving: **Move** and **Rotate** on a selected gear change its record (`setGear` with a centre and a `turn`, one command, one undo, fixed constraints on its corners let go as for any figure); the bodies made from it follow. A gear stays in its plane: a move across the plane, or a turn about another axis, is not made.
- **Built (`in_579`): the rack and the meshing helper.** A **rack** (`spec.rack`; `operation/gear.ts` `rackOutline`) is a straight bar with straight-sided teeth at the pressure angle, `n` of them, π·m apart, the middle of its pitch line at the record's centre, teeth up, `rim` modules of bar under the roots (box: Teeth, Module, Pressure, Backlash, Base); it is made, edited, moved, turned, extruded and deleted as a gear is. **Mesh gear** and **Mesh rack** (Sketch ring, enabled with a gear, ring or rack selected; `operation/mesh.ts`, `meshWith`) make a partner that fits the selected one: a gear beside a gear (centre distance m·(z₁+z₂)/2), a gear inside a ring (m·(z_ring−z)/2, fewer teeth than the ring), a rack on a gear's pitch circle, a gear on a rack; the partner takes the module, pressure angle and backlash of the one it meshes with and is turned so a tooth of one is in a gap of the other (the phase rules are in the file; checked by outlines that do not run into each other, and that do when half a pitch out). The pointer says where it goes (the direction of the partner from the gear; how far along a rack), or type Teeth, Ratio (partner teeth over the gear's) and Angle (degrees) or Offset (mm). Refused: a gear with a profile shift (its centre distance is not the plain one), a rack against a rack or a ring, a gear as big as its ring.
- **Messages (`in_580`):** a gear, ring, rack or partner that is not made says why in the message line when Enter or a click makes nothing (`gearProblem`: too few teeth, a pressure angle that is no angle, teeth too thin at the tips, a gear as big as its ring, a rack against a ring or a rack, a shifted gear); the tool stays on so the number can be changed. A change to a gear that is already made is refused by the command with its reason, as before.
- **Built (`in_583`): GT2 pulleys.** `GearSpec.pulley` (`"2GT"` or `"3GT"`; `operation/pulley.ts`) makes the outline of a timing pulley for the Gates GT 2 mm or 3 mm belt: `teeth` grooves (12 to 400), the outside diameter pitch diameter less 2 × PLD (20 grooves of 2GT: 12.22 mm, as Pfeifer's and Gates' charts), each groove the belt tooth (three arcs, R3 tip, R2 sides, R1 corners, from the belt table in `q_timing_pulleys`) made `clearance` mm bigger all round (0 to 0.13), a bore. The **GT2 pulley** and **3GT pulley** tools (Sketch ring; box Teeth, Clearance, Bore) make it; it is edited, moved, turned, extruded and deleted like a gear; a gear is not meshed to it (a belt is). The groove is the belt's tooth, not the pulley makers' own groove drawing: it is to be checked against a real belt. HTD and the other belts are not built (`q_timing_pulleys`).
- **Built (`in_584`): roller chain sprockets.** `GearSpec.sprocket` (`{ chain?, pitch?, roller? }`; `operation/sprocket.ts`) makes the outline of a sprocket with the ANSI B29.1 tooth form for the chain's pitch P and roller diameter Dr and `teeth` N (7 to 400): pitch diameter P / sin(180/N), outside diameter P (0.6 + cot(180/N)); the gap is the seating curve (R = 0.5025 Dr + 0.0015 in, A = 35 + 60/N degrees either side of the bottom), the working curve (E = 1.3025 Dr + 0.0015 in about a point 0.8 Dr off, B = 18 - 56/N degrees, touching the seat on the inside), a flat of length Dr (1.4 sin C - 0.8 sin B) (C = 17 - 64/N) and the topping curve (F = Dr (0.8 cos B + 1.4 cos C - 1.3025) - 0.0015 in), the tooth cut at the blank's outside diameter or, when the pure tooth comes to a point below it (most counts), left pointed. **Sources:** the construction and constants are TNB Positioners' ANSI B29.1 generator page (https://tnbpositioners.com/tools/sprocket/), which says it reproduces the American Chain Association's worked example and a commercial #40 of 18 teeth (working radius 0.4079 in, topping 0.2582 in), and which agree with the Machinery's Handbook table quoted in a search (seating radius 0.5025 Dr + 0.0015, A = 35 + 60/N, B = 18 - 56/N); the code reproduces its numbers (the topping curve's centre is exactly 1.4 Dr from the roller's centre; E and F of the #40 / 18 teeth to four decimals). **Chains** (inches, Martin Sprocket's catalogue "Chain Data For All Sprockets", https://www.martinsprocket.com/docs/catalogs/engineering/engineering%20catalog/sprocket-engineering-data.pdf, who conform to the Type II tooth form of ANSI B29.1-1975): #25 .250 / .130, #35 .375 / .200, #41 .500 / .306, #40 .500 / .312, #50 .625 / .400, #60 .750 / .469, #80 1.000 / .625, #100 1.250 / .750, #120 1.500 / .875 (pitch / roller diameter); a chain of one's own is typed by its pitch and roller diameter (mm). The **Sprocket** tool (Sketch ring; box Teeth, Chain, Pitch, Roller, Bore) makes it; a chain number there is none of says so. Bicycle chains (1/2 x 1/8 in, 12.7 mm pitch) are not in the table (their roller diameter was not read from a source): type the pitch and roller diameter. The sprocket's width is the thickness it is extruded to; the table's single strand tooth width is in `operation/sprocket.ts`. Not made: ISO 606 tooth forms, the chordal rise.
- **Built (`in_585`): cycloidal (clock) gears.** `GearSpec.cycloidal` (`{ circle?, addendum?, dedendum? }` in modules; `operation/cycloidal.ts`) makes a spur gear whose flank outside the pitch circle is an epicycloid and inside it a hypocycloid of the generating circle (radius 3 modules by default; addendum 1, dedendum 1.25), with the thickness on the pitch circle half the pitch less the backlash. The geometry is the textbook one (the curves a circle traces rolling on the outside and the inside of the pitch circle; the search results the AI read say the same, with the rule that mating gears share the generating circle, so one circle for a whole set makes any two mesh); at a generating circle half the pitch circle (a 12 tooth gear with the default) the dedendum is the straight radial flank of "clock toothing". **Checked** by outlines of gears that mesh (tooth counts 8 to 60 on both sides of 12, several directions and turns, never running into each other, touching, half a pitch out running into each other): this found that the hypocycloid must lean the other way from the epicycloid (the first version did not mesh a pinion under 12 teeth with a large wheel), and by the curves against their equations to half a percent of the module. **The defaults are conventions, not read from a standard** (the AI found no source for BS 978 or other clock addendum rules): the addendum, dedendum and circle are fields of the box (Teeth, Module, Backlash, Circle, Addendum, Dedendum, Bore) for the person to set. Refused with the reason: under 7 teeth, a generating circle as big as the pitch circle, an addendum or dedendum the circle cannot make, pointed teeth. **Clock gear** (Sketch ring) makes it, **Mesh gear** from a clock gear makes a clock gear partner of the same module and circle (the centre distance is m (z1 + z2) / 2 and the turn as for any gear); a **Clock rack** and a **Clock ring** (`in_586`, below) are the same tooth. A pinion under 12 teeth with the default circle is undercut (said as a warning).
- **Built (`in_586`): the cycloidal rack and ring.** `cycloidalRack` and `cycloidalRing` (`operation/cycloidal.ts`, reached by `spec.cycloidal` with `rack` or `internal`). The **rack** is a gear of endless radius: the generating circle rolls on the straight pitch line, from above for the addendum (the cycloid x = r (t - sin t), y = r (1 - cos t), leaning toward the middle of the tooth) and from below for the dedendum (leaning away), flat tips and roots; teeth, module, backlash, circle, addendum and dedendum as for a gear, `rim` modules of bar under the roots (box: ..., Base). The **ring** has its teeth pointing in: its addendum (inside the pitch circle) is a hypocycloid of the circle and its dedendum (outside) an epicycloid (the pair-of-circles argument: a pinion inside a ring has its addendum conjugate to the ring's dedendum and its dedendum to the ring's addendum), with a wall of `rim` modules round it (box: ..., Wall). Both are used by **Clock rack** and **Clock ring** (Sketch ring); **Mesh gear** and **Mesh rack** work from and onto them as for involute ones (a gear on a clock rack where the pointer is; a clock rack under a clock gear; a clock gear inside a clock ring). **Checked** by outlines that mesh (a gear on a rack at several places and turns, both ways round, penetration under 0.001 mm at module 2; gears inside a ring for five tooth counts) and half a pitch out collide; the sign of the ring's addendum lean was settled by that test (the other sign puts the pinion half a millimetre into the ring), and the dedendum's by a check of the curves themselves (a point of the pinion's addendum epicycloid, carried round with the pinion's turn, touches the ring's dedendum curve to a thousandth of a millimetre with the sign used and misses it by 0.14 mm with the other; the polygon test cannot tell the two apart, and an intuition that a ring tooth is wider at its root was wrong).
- **Built (`in_587`): the clearance field and an HTD 5M pulley.** **Clearance** is now a field of the box of an involute gear, ring and rack: the root clearance in modules (0.25 shown and by default; the root is the pitch circle less (1 + clearance - shift) modules); a number outside 0 to 1 is refused with the reason. (The pulleys already had a clearance field, in mm; the sprocket and the clock gears have their own numbers.) **HTD 5M** (`operation/pulley.ts`, `HTD_TOOTH`; tool **HTD 5M**, box Teeth, Clearance, Bore; 13 to 400 grooves): the belt tooth from SDP/SI's drawing, Chief Delphi's and Capolight's pages (pitch 5, tooth height 2.06, tooth radius R1 1.49, root radius R2 0.43) with straight sides between them, and the pitch line differential 0.572 from Pfeifer's charts; the groove is the tooth made `clearance` bigger all round (0 to 0.41), 0.07 mm by default (the difference between a machinist's forum's 1.56 mm groove radius for this belt, not read at its source, and the belt's 1.49; HTD is cut loose). **Not the pulley makers' groove drawing, and not verified against a real belt**; the 3.05 mm tooth width and 0.74 mm flat some of the pages give do not fit a tooth drawn from the radii. HTD 3M and 8M are not built (no radii read for them).
- **Built (`in_588`): ISO 606 sprockets, one Gear submenu, bores for meshed gears.** **ISO sprocket** (`operation/iso606.ts`; `sprocket.iso`): the ISO 606:1994 limits as the patent US 7128673 prints them (pitch diameter p / sin(180/z), seat radius ri from 0.505 d1 to 0.505 d1 + 0.069 d1^(1/3), flank radius re between 0.12 d1 (z + 2) and 0.008 d1 (z^2 + 180); the patent names the two flank limits the other way round, which cannot be, so the smaller is taken as the lower); the seating angle 120 - 90/z to 140 - 90/z degrees (the formulas of the AI's memory, checked at z = 20 against a calculator page: 115.5 to 135.5). A gap inside the limits is an ISO sprocket, so the form made is the seating arc and flank arcs touching it on the inside, `fit` (0 tight to 1 loose, 0.5 by default) putting ri, re and the angle that far along their limits; the outside circle is the ANSI one P (0.6 + cot(180/z)) (read from TNB's page), because ISO's own outside diameter limits (the AI remembers d + 1.25 p - d1 and d + p (1 - 1.6/z) - d1) were not found in a source; the value is between them for the chains tried. Chains: the B series 06B to 24B (pitch and roller diameter agree in three sources: planichapa, chainsprocketchina, Merley), the A series as the ANSI numbers 40 to 120, or one's own pitch and roller. 9 to 400 teeth. Box: Teeth, Chain (6 to 24 or 40 to 120; 8 by default), Pitch, Roller, Fit, Bore. **The Sketch ring now has one Gear icon** that opens a submenu: Spur gear, Ring gear, Rack, Clock (gear, ring, rack), Pulley (GT2, 3GT, HTD 5M), Sprocket (ANSI, ISO), Edit gear, Mesh gear, Mesh rack (`GEARS` in `control/pointer.ts`; the ring already took menus of menus). **Bores:** every gear that can have one (spur gear, clock gear, pulley, sprocket) already had a Bore field and Edit gear changes it; a gear made by **Mesh gear** (a gear partner, a gear on a rack) now has a Bore field too (after Angle or Offset), so it can have its bore at once; a ring has its wall and a rack its base instead.
- **Built (`in_589`): D flat and keyway bores.** `operation/bore.ts`; `GearSpec.flat` (mm cut into the bore's +x side), `key` (the slot's width, mm) and `keyDepth` (the hub depth t2, mm). The hole is a loop of an arc and straight sides (`d/g_001.bs0...`, corners `.bp0...`), turned with the gear, and is a hole of the steps that name the whole outline (`swapBore` handles round, D and keyway edits). The number box has **Flat**, **Key** and **Key depth** after Bore for every gear that has a bore (spur, clock gear, pulleys, sprockets, meshed partners). With no depth typed the keyway takes t2 from the DIN 6885 / ISO 773 hub table by bore diameter (over 6 to 8: key 2, t2 1.0; to 10: 3, 1.4; to 12: 4, 1.8; to 17: 5, 2.3; to 22: 6, 2.8; to 30: 8, 3.3; to 38: 10, 3.3); this table is from several secondary sources that agree (not the standard itself, which was not read) and the keyway's top is placed at r + t2 from the centre. Outside 6 to 38 mm a depth must be typed. Refused with a reason: a flat or key with no bore, both at once, a flat deeper than 1.8 r, a key as wide as the bore, a slot reaching the root. The key's width is not looked up (type it), and no fit tolerance is added: the shape is nominal, as everything here is (`prin_manufacture_agnostic`).
- **Built (`in_590`): HTD 8M pulleys** (`pulley: "HTD8M"`, `HTD_TOOTH.HTD8M` in `operation/pulley.ts`, "HTD 8M" in the Pulley submenu; the same groove construction as HTD 5M). Numbers read at their sources: ProTorque's HTD datasheet (www.protorque.net, 2026-11-16): 8M pitch 8, tooth height 3.38, head radius 2.46, foot radius 0.70, width 5.15, belt 6.00 thick, 14 degree flank (its 5M column agrees with the 5M numbers already used, foot radius 0.42 against 0.43 here); the pitch line differential 0.686 mm and the least count of 14 grooves from Pfeifer's 8 mm HTD chart (20 grooves: PD 50.93, OD 49.56). Like 5M it is the belt's tooth, not a pulley maker's groove drawing, so **check against a real belt**; no groove clearance default is known for 8M (5M's 0.07 comes from a forum), so it is 0 and the Clearance field changes it. **HTD 3M was not built**: only its tooth height (1.22 mm, belt 2.41 mm) and Pfeifer's chart (PLD 0.381, 10 grooves least) were found, no tooth radii at any allowed source (ProTorque lists 5M, 8M and 14M only); the radii are not guessed.
- **Built (`in_592`): root fillet and undercut shape for external involute gears** (`operation/gearroot.ts`; `GearSpec.fillet`, the **Fillet** field, in modules, after Clearance; none or 0 keeps the plain root circle). The root is what a rack cutter leaves: the cutter's tooth is the straight flank at the pressure angle with a corner of radius fillet x module at its tip, at the depth of the dedendum (1 + clearance - shift modules), its tooth half-width at its own pitch line pi m / 4 + backlash / 2. Rolling it on the pitch circle, the straight flank makes the involute (checked: the outline crosses the pitch circle and its neighbours at the same angles as the analytic flank) and the corner makes the trochoid of the root fillet, from the meshing condition (the cutter point's normal passes through the pitch point) in closed form. With few teeth the corner cuts below the involute's start: the loop the curve makes is cut away at the crossing, the joint is where the involute now starts, and the outline says "undercut". The fillet curve is three arcs through points at equal length along the curve, the involute part four (the existing flank arcs from the joint up); the arcs are within about 0.05 mm of the true curve for a 2 mm module (the sharpest case in the tests, a fillet of 0.01 with 12 teeth, is the loosest). A fillet is refused below zero, at or above the cutter's tip depth, too big for the cutter's tooth, or so big the teeth meet at the root. No fillet for rings, racks, pulleys, sprockets or cycloidal gears (their roots are made by their own constructions). The tip of the cutter is the ISO-style rounded corner; the standard's recommended 0.38 module is only a number to type, not a default.
- **Bore snapping to the centre: nothing to build.** A gear with a round bore has the centre of that circle as a corner (`d/g_001.bc`) that can be snapped to; with no bore, or a D or keyway bore, there is no circle, and a centre corner with nothing drawn on it would break `dec_no_stray_corners` (accepted), so none is made.
- **Built (`in_598`): helical gears** (`GearSpec.helix` and `width`; the **Helical gear** item of the Gear submenu; fields Helix and Width after Detail). The outline is the **transverse section**: module mn / cos(helix), pressure angle atan(tan(an) / cos(helix)); the normal module, pressure angle, shift (a distance), backlash and clearance are the ones the person types, converted so the tooth depth, the addendum (mn) and the thickness are right in the section (checked in `test/helical.test.ts`). The helix angle is the angle at the pitch circle, 1 to 45 degrees, **a minus sign is a left hand helix** (there is no Hand field); the width is along the axis (six modules if none is typed), the helix 15 degrees if none is typed. The gear command makes the outline **and** the body: a `revolve` step on the whole outline (and its bore) about the plane's normal through the centre, `angle` = width x tan(helix) / transverse pitch radius in degrees (`helixTwist`), `advance` = width, and a `gear` param naming the gear. `setGear` keeps that step's numbers, centre and holes in step with the gear (a gear that stops being helical loses it; `deleteGear` takes it away); selecting the body of a helical gear selects the gear (`gearOfPart`), so Edit gear works from the body. The preview (no kernel) is the outline, the outline again at the far end turned by the twist, and one edge of each tooth carried between them in four pieces (`dec_speed_rules` item 7). Mesh gear on a helical gear makes a partner of the opposite hand with the same normal module and width, at the centre distance of the transverse sections; a rack and a profile shift are refused. **Not built:** herringbone gears (the human: stack two gears of opposite hand), helical rings and racks, bevel and worm gears. Made in about 2 s for 40 teeth with the speed rules; the arcs of the flank are the plain gear's, so a helical gear is as exact as a spur gear is, not exact like a cutter.
- **Built (`in_770`, `in_771`): the gear's icon is the way to Edit, Helix and Mesh; the meshing gear shows orange.** (1) **Edit gear, Helix, Mesh gear, Mesh rack and Mesh ring are out of the Sketch ring's Gear submenu**; they are on the gear's own icon only (the gear button by a selected or pointed-at gear, `ui/gearchip.ts`). (2) **The icon and its items work on the body of a gear extruded from its own outline** (and a face of that body): `gearOfSelection` (`gearscene.ts`) finds the gear from the step that made the body, as `gearOfPart` did only for helical gears, worms and wheels' own bodies. Before, a plain extruded gear showed no icon and Mesh answered "select a gear first": that is why the Mesh box did not come up (a helical gear's body was found, so it did). `gearOfPart` is unchanged for delete, move and turn (a body made from an outline is a body there). (3) **Helix from the icon** puts 15 degrees in the Helix field of a gear that has none (typed, so Esc leaves the gear alone, Enter makes it) and the box opens with the keyboard in that field, scrolled to it (`Entry.focus`, `GearState.focus`); a gear that has a helix keeps its number. (4) **A gear sized next to a gear it would mesh with is drawn as the meshed gear in orange** between the first click and the second (`Preview.orange`; tooth count and place from `meshWith`, as the second click makes it), with the dashed line to the gear; the box says so. (5) **A double click on a body while the gear tool waits for the next gear's centre** (it stays on after a gear is made, `in_688`) puts the tool away and selects the body: the tool had been taking the double click as the centre and the size of a new gear, so bodies could not be double clicked until Esc.
- **Built (`in_783`): a helical rack and a worm wheel land.** Details (the land is toothed and cut back by the worm's envelope, `in_790`, at about four times the build time) are in `q_gear_finishing`. A rack takes a Helix and a Thickness (a sheared extrusion: the `extrude` step's `slantx`, `slanty`); a helical gear and a rack mesh in both directions (`meshWith`); the Helix item of the icon is enabled on a rack. A worm wheel has a `land` (Land field). **A tooth chamfer** (`in_791`, `chamfer` in modules, one turned cut) for spur, helical and ring gears: see `q_gear_finishing`.
- **Decided (`in_858`): the tooth flanks stay chains of arcs** (4 a flank by default, `detail` 2 to 8); a one-spline flank was prototyped (`prototypes/gear-spline-flank`) and is not built.
- **Decided (`in_862`): the helical gear's chamfer stays as it is** (4.4 s more on a 40 tooth gear); a faster chamfered tooth copied round is not built.
- **Built (`in_864`): defaults and the icon.** The planetary set's default module is 1 (was 2); a worm is 25 modules long (4 leads if more starts; was 15 and 2.5 leads); a new worm wheel has a bore as a gear has (`defaultBore`), unless 0 is typed, also from Mesh gear on a worm (a worm made for a wheel has none). The icon's items work on a gear that is only pointed at (the click selects that gear first: `runAction` takes `part`); a gear's number box is no longer pushed aside by the size bubble of a face that is selected (`frame.ts`: no automatic operation while a gear tool is on): with a face selected, Edit and Helix opened the tool with no box shown, and Helix did not take the keyboard.
- **Built (`in_866`): the edge chamfer.** `GearSpec.edge` (mm; the **Edge chamfer** field, last in the box of a spur gear and a ring; 0 to 3, under half the thickness; needs a thickness; refused with a reason with a helix, a flat or keyway bore or a root fillet): the whole top and bottom perimeter of both faces (every edge of the outline, a round bore's rim, a ring's wall), for printing. The body is one `chamferBody` step (kernel op in `interpreter.mjs`): a ruled loft through the outline inset by the chamfer at each face and the full outline a chamfer in, the loft's shells, the hole's turned inside out and the two flat ends sewn into one solid, no boolean on the gear. The inset outline is `gearOutline` with `inset` (`edgeOutlines` in `gear.ts`: an involute's offset is an involute of the same base circle: tip and root circles in by c, flanks thinner; for a ring out by c). It can be used with the tooth chamfer. **Cost, measured in the app (8 mm, module 2, 0.4 mm): 40 teeth 1.7 s plain and 3.2 s with it, 100 teeth 2.4 s and 7.3 s, a 40 tooth ring 1.3 s and 2.3 s**, against 45 s for the kernel's edge chamfer on a 40 tooth gear (`prototypes/perimeter-chamfer`). Checked: one body, the box, the volume against the kernel's chamfer (within 3 mm3 of 9608.1), a ring, with the tooth chamfer, on another plane and turned.
- **Decided (`in_868`): gears are picked and drawn like any regular part.** A click on a gear selects the face, a double click the whole body, as for every body (a click-selects-the-whole-gear change of `in_867` was built and taken out again at the human's word: "gears shouldn't be special"). What stays from it: a **selected body is filled blue all over, its teeth too, as any body**: a body among several was filled only by the faces that are parts, and a gear keeps few parts (`dec_gear_feature`), so its teeth were never blue; the frame now names the triangles of a selected or pointed-at body from the kernel's faces of it (`Frame.highlight.bodyTris`, `bodyTrisOf` in `frame.ts`, `fillTriangles` in `highlight.ts`). Hover lights the face under the pointer, as for any part.

- **Fixed (`in_872`): moving or turning a gear's body.** The human sent a bevel gear that was moved along Y by 11.2 (and said that turning a bevel gear sometimes fails and moving after a turn does odd things). ## Rationale

The involute is the one profile of machined gears that keeps a constant speed ratio and forgives centre-distance error; the rest of the list are the profiles the human named. Drawing the outline as ordinary parts means export, extrude, hole cutting, mirroring and the flat SVG/DXF export all work on it without special cases.
