# Revolve with turns and a pitch (a spiral extrude)

**Asked for (`in_427`):** a rotate-extrude arrow, a curved arrow that is a full circle so the turn can go past 360 degrees, and a second arrow at right angles to the turning direction that makes it advance, so the same operation revolves a figure (a ring, a bowl) or spirals it (a screw, a spring). Nothing is built; the choices below are the AI's proposal and are the human's to make.

**Proposal.**
1. One operation, **Revolve**, on a closed figure (or a selected face). Its numbers: **Angle** (degrees, any size; 360 is a full turn, 720 two) and **Advance** (mm along the axis for the whole angle, 0 for a plain revolve; the pitch is the advance divided by the turns).
2. **Arrows:** a circle arrow (a full ring, handle on it, drawn round the axis; its drag sets the angle, past one turn it keeps counting) and a straight arrow along the axis (its drag sets the advance). Both can be typed as the other operations are (Tab goes between them).
3. **Axis** (the human's choice, below). Default: the figure's own edge nearest the pointer or a world axis lying in the figure's plane.
4. **Kernel:** a plain revolve of up to 360 degrees is the kernel's own revolution. Past 360 with no advance the extra turns lie on the first (the same body), so the angle is limited to 360 until there is an advance. With an advance the figure is swept along a helix about the axis (the kernel's pipe shell on a helical spine, with the profile kept at the same angle to the axis); the result must not cross itself: an advance per turn smaller than the figure is wide along the axis is refused with the reason.
5. **As a step** `revolve` with `angle`, `advance` and the axis named by what it is (a line of the sketch, a world axis); a change of the number changes the step, as every other operation does (`dec_modifications_are_steps`).

**Decided (`in_428`):** the axis is a line **in** the figure's plane (A); a **circle arrow** sets the angle and a straight **arrow along the axis** sets the advance. The twist about the normal (B) is not built. **Choices still open:**
- Where the curved arrow lies (a ring round the axis, standing on the figure).
- Whether the turn past 360 with no advance should be allowed at all (it changes nothing).

**Built (`in_428`).** Select a closed figure (and, if you like, a line in its plane) and choose **Revolve / spiral** in the Shape menu. The figure turns about **a line in its plane**: the line selected with it, a world axis in its plane, or one of its own sides (a chip "Axis:" chooses; a line the figure crosses is not offered). Two numbers, typed or dragged, Tab between them: **Angle** (any size, either way; the ring round the axis keeps counting past one turn) and **Advance** (mm along the axis over the whole angle; the arrow along the axis; none for a plain revolve). The step is `revolve` (`angle`, `advance`, the axis as numbers); the kernel's `revolve` op makes a plain turn of up to 360 degrees by revolution and a spiral by sweeping the figure along a helix about the axis (exactly a screw motion: the volume equals area times the path of the figure's middle, to four figures). Refused with the reason: a turn past 360 degrees with no advance, an advance a turn that is less than the figure is wide along the axis (the turns would overlap), an axis outside the figure's plane or through the figure. Whether it adds, cuts or stands apart is decided when it is made, as for an extrusion. **Not built:** a figure with holes or several figures at once; the twist about the plane's normal (B).

**Simpler (`in_433`):** it begins as a full turn (360°) with no advance, so nothing has to be typed: pick the line to turn about and apply. The line is a line selected with the figure, a world axis in its plane, one of its own sides, or **the rotate tool**: a line through the sphere (the ball shown on the axis, dragged where it is wanted and brought to the figure's plane) along the figure's own X or Y. The twist axes keep an advance to begin with (10 mm), as a twist with none is nothing.

**`in_435` (replaces the axis chips and the tool axes above):** the revolve has no axis buttons and chooses no axis by itself: it begins at 360° with no advance and **waits for a click on the line to turn about** (a straight side of the figure, another straight line in its plane, or a world axis in it; a line selected with the figure counts as that click). Nothing shows and nothing is made until then; a click on something that cannot be the axis says why. The twist about the plane's normal is no longer offered from the screen (the step still takes it).

**`in_436` (replaces the waiting of `in_435`):** the revolve uses the mover's orb. It begins with the orb on one of the figure's own lines (the line selected with it, else its first side), pulsing blue as the mover's orb does in its orient mode, the part drawn as wireframe while it is decided, and a quarter turn (90°) as the preview; the ring (normal to the line: the axis runs along the line, slanted or not) and the advance arrow are there to change the angle and advance. Clicking another straight line of the figure, or any line in its plane the figure does not cross, moves the orb and the axis there (near a corner the line that is not the axis already is the one taken). The orb itself is not dragged.

**`in_438` (the orb is the mover's, for real):** the axis is the orb's place and one axis of its frame (`Op.pivot`, `Op.frame`, `Op.axis`), not a line chosen from a list (the list, its chips and the waiting are gone). It begins **on** the longest straight line of the first figure (a line selected with the figure is the one): the orb at the middle of that line, its X along it and its Z across the figure's plane (a figure with no straight side: its middle, the plane's own axes). A click on the orb selects it: the orient mode of the mover (`dec_mover_sphere`: its arrows put it anywhere in space, its rings turn its axes, numbers typed for place and turn, a click on a line, corner or face of anything sets it there, apply and cancel). A click on a line, a corner or a face sets the orb there even without the sphere selected, the axis along the line (X) or across the face (Z). The axis turned about is the frame's axis last chosen (X by default). About the plane's normal through the first figure's middle several figures each turn about their own middle (a twist), as before.

**Overlapping turns are allowed (`in_1335`, replaces the floor of `in_1194` and `in_1298`):** the human asked why not allow the turns to overlap and be merged into one body (`in_1334`: any advance under the figure's width along the axis was silently raised to it, so numbers below it seemed to do nothing). **Now** the advance is what is typed or dragged to, however small. When it is less than the figure is wide along the axis (a tight spring, coils pressed together) the kernel makes the spiral **in pieces of at most 180 degrees** (one sweep of that size cannot cross itself): the first piece is swept once, the others are it turned about the axis and moved along it by their share, each but the last runs 3 degrees past its share so two pieces share a little volume and not a face, and they are **fused two by two** with a tolerance of a thousandth of a millimetre and no merging of faces (`fuzzy`, `simplify: false`, `booleans.mjs`). Without those two settings the fuse failed or gave a wrong volume for most cases (the turns of a helix share the same helicoid surface exactly, which the kernel cannot join); with them seven different angle and pitch pairs gave the volume the ring they fill predicts, to the tenth of a mm cubed. The removed messages: "raised to" in the bar and the box. **Cost:** several seconds for many turns (1000 degrees: about 4 s; 1440 degrees with a pitch near the width: 8 s); a spiral that does not overlap is one sweep as before. **Not built:** a faster way for many turns; a progress sign while it is made.

## Rationale

Revolve is in the blueprint's operation list (`docs/origin/CAD_Blueprint.original.md`, "Revolve sketch around axis") and threads are a stated goal; a spiral is a revolve with an advance, so one operation with two numbers covers rings, bowls, screws and springs. Accepted with the axis and arrow layout chosen by the human (`in_428`).

**Rotate orb (`in_468`):** the revolve's handles are always the rotate orb: a ring about each of the three axes of the orb (`rev:turn:x|y|z`, about the frame's axes, wherever the orb stands and however it is turned), the ring taken (a click, or a drag that turns it) is the axis the figure turns about (`Op.axis`), the arrow through the middle of it, perpendicular to it (`rev:adv`), sets the spiral advance and follows the chosen ring; the orb itself is dragged to move it (it no longer turns the view; the sphere click still opens the orient mode). The *orb vanishing while it is moved* was not reproduced (the orb and rings stayed in the picture, in data and in the browser during a drag of the orb and of a ring): tell me the steps if it still does.

**Arrows on each axis (`in_529`):** the rotate orb has, on each of its three axes, two arrows in line as the linear repeat has (spacing and count): the first (`piv:x|y|z`, the same as the mover's) puts the orb along that axis (drag; up all the time, no click on the orb first), the second, past it (`rev:adv:x|y|z`), sets the spiral advance along that axis; taking it (a click or a drag) also makes that axis the one turned about. The single advance arrow through the ring (`rev:adv`) is gone. **Not reproduced:** "it doesn't spiral at all": the kernel, the step and a drag of the old arrow all gave a spiral in the tests (a 90 degree default turn with a small advance is a slight spiral); the clearer arrows are the answer built, not a found fault. **Number bubbles (`in_531`):** an arrow taken shows its number at its tip: a position arrow the orb's world coordinate along that axis (typed to put the orb there, field 10 + the axis, `revolveEntries`), a spiral arrow the advance (the chip's own second number). For a turned orb the number is the place along that arrow's own axis (the orb's distance from the origin measured along it; the world coordinate when not turned, `in_532`), and a number typed moves the orb along that axis only.

## Built: a first preview that always makes something, and why a spring failed (`in_1194`)
**Why it failed:** the advance is the distance over the *whole angle*, not a turn, and the kernel refuses a spiral whose turns would overlap (the figure's width along the axis must be no more than the advance per turn). A circle 9.51 mm wide with 1.6 mm typed over 90 degrees is 6.4 mm a turn: refused, with a message that talked of a turn the person had not set. **Now:** the advance is raised to the least that does not overlap (`advanceFloor`, `revolve.ts`: width times the turns, a hundredth over), the step is made with it, and the message says so ("the advance is the distance over the whole angle: the figure is W mm wide along the axis, so over A degrees it is raised to F mm"). **The first orb** for a figure with no straight side (a circle): out in the figure's plane beside it, twice the figure's reach from its middle, the axis along the plane's second direction in that plane, so the first preview (90 degrees) is a ring bent out of the plane; before, the orb stood at the middle with the axis across the figure, and nothing could be drawn. A figure with a straight side starts on its longest side as before. **Also:** a new icon for the tool (a profile beside its axis with the arc of the turn under it); the world axes' orb is not shown through the model. My choices, for review: twice the reach, the axis along the plane's second direction, raising the advance instead of refusing it.


## Changed: the advance is for each turn; the bubble is there from the start (`in_1298`)
The advance of a spiral was the distance over the whole angle, so the number typed meant nothing alone (a hundred degrees with 1.6 mm was 5.8 mm a turn). **Now it is the distance for each full turn** (the pitch, in mm or the system in use), whatever the angle; the step keeps the total (`advance` is that times the turns, so saved models do not change). The floor under which the turns would run into each other is now a pitch (the figure's width along the axis, raised to it with a note), no longer dependent on the angle. The Revolve operation shows the bubble of its angle and its advance (*Advance a turn*) as soon as it opens, nothing chosen first (`ui`: `numberShown` in `frame.ts`); the same for the advance arrow's own bubble. A hard-body stop at a part of the turn keeps the advance a turn as it was.
