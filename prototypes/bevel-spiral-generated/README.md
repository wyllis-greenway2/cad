# A spiral bevel tooth by the gearing law (a prototype, `in_793`)

**Meant to learn:** whether the tooth surfaces of a face-milled spiral bevel set can be derived from the sources the human sent (so they are built right, `in_785`) and checked against the one worked example that gives numbers.

**Sources read:** H. J. Stadtfeld, "Gear Mathematics for Bevel & Hypoid Gears", Chapter 2, *Gear Technology*, August 2015 (file `chapter2.pdf`, read in full: the example's inputs, blank data, cutter head radii, machine settings, the ratio of roll, the gearing law and how the flank grid is made); R. L. Huston and J. J. Coy, NASA TM 82622 (1981), and R. L. Huston, NASA CR 168226 (1983) (the first pages: the circular arc against the logarithmic spiral, the crown gear, the planes; the equations of the later pages not yet read); I. Zarebski, "The Intrinsic Pitch Cones in Hypoid Gears", *Gear Technology*, Sept / Oct 2025 (the first page: pitch cones of a hypoid are replaced by hyperboloids and depend on the offset and the tool; not yet read); Stadtfeld, "The Basics of Spiral Bevel Gears", *Gear Technology*, 2001 (the first page: the crown gear derives the flank form; the octoid is a straight lead with an octoid profile; the scan is poor, not yet read).

**The model (`generate.ts`):** a crown (generating) gear whose tooth is the band between two cones about the cutter's axis (the blade surfaces: radius `RW -+ tB/4 -+ y tan a` at the height `y`), the cutter's centre fixed by the mean spiral angle (`C = M - RW`, Stadtfeld eq. 33 to 37), the work gear's axis at 90 degrees + its pitch angle to the crown's, the ratio of roll the sine of the pitch angle; the work gear's flank is where the gearing law holds: `tan(psi) = -(x ny - y nx) / (z ny - y nz)` (derived here from the relative motion, a rotation about the contact line). Both members of a set come from the one crown tooth, the pinion from its tip side (`y < 0`) and the ring gear from its root side (`y > 0`), which is what the blade radii of the example say.

**Checked (`generate.test.ts`, passes):**
1. **A flat crown flank through the apex generates the spherical involute** (for four pitch angle and pressure angle pairs, the azimuth plus the involute function of the angle from the axis is constant to 4e-4 rad where it varies by 0.3 otherwise): the kinematics (the ratio, the signs, the turning back) are right, and the exact straight bevel tooth is the case of a flat crown flank.
2. **Stadtfeld's worked example (13 / 35 teeth, 30 degrees, cutter radius 76.2 mm, module 4, 20 degrees):** the mean cone distance 86.34, the cutter centre (65.99, 0, 48.24), the centre of roll 53.83 degrees, the radial distance 81.74, the four blade tip radii 74.80, 77.59, 81.09 and 71.31 (Tables 3 and 4, eq. 27 to 30) and the ratios of roll 0.348 and 0.937 all come out of the model.

**Built (`sections.ts`, `in_794`):** the pinion's surface (the envelope of the crown flanks), the ring gear's (the envelope of the pinion's, by the gearing law between the two gears: closed form `P0 + P1 cos t + P2 sin t = 0`, the ratio of roll `sin g1 / sin g2`), both in the pair's frame at the meshing place; the flank curves in planes across each axis (the grid contoured, filtered by radius and azimuth); the tip and root cones (Stadtfeld's: the pitch angle with the apexes moved by the addendum and dedendum over sin g); the outline of z teeth (two flanks, a tip arc, a root arc; below the lowest radius the flank reaches, straight down to the root: the 13 / 35 example's pinion root is inside its base cone, the paper says so, it motivates a profile shift).

**Measured in the kernel (`sections.test.ts`: 20 / 30 teeth, module 3, mean spiral angle 30 degrees, cutter radius 0.88 of the mean cone distance, face 0.3 of it, 20 degrees, shafts 90 degrees, flat ends, seven sections, a smooth loft, the pinion's slot 0.05 mm wider on each side; the volume of the two bodies' intersection):**

| the pair | overlap |
|---|---|
| at the meshing place | **0.5 mm3** (bodies 58220 and 88607 mm3) |
| rolled by 0.04 rad (the ring gear by the ratio of roll) | **0.6 mm3** |
| rolled by 0.08 rad | **0.5 mm3** |
| the ring gear half a pitch round | **352.9 mm3** |

**What it learned:** the pair made by the gearing law **is conjugate**: the overlap is a thousandth of a per cent of the bodies at the place and stays the same as the pair rolls, against 353 mm3 half a pitch off. (The 0.5 mm3 is the loft's and the polylines' error, below the 0.1 mm of backlash; the simple twisted section model of `prototypes/bevel-spiral` overlapped by 13 to 15 mm3 whichever the hands.) Earlier failures were mine: a face of a third of the cone distance made pointed teeth at the toe (the slot of a face milled gear is as wide at the toe as at the heel, the tooth is not), and the example's cone distance was the outer one where the paper's is the mean. **Not done:** the heel and toe cones (a blank to intersect with, as the straight gear's), the root fillet and the region below the base cone, crowning (a real set is crowned for a localised contact; this is the ideal conjugate set), a larger set of tooth counts and spiral angles, the 13 / 35 example itself (a profile shift), the hands, then the hypoid (the offset adds a vector to the machine, the paper's later chapters and Zarebski for the pitch hyperboloids). Nothing in `app/` changed.
