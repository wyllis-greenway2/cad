# round-limits: spike 7 (the pointed rod end, and a size that varies along an edge)

**Meant to learn** (`in_1489`): (A) can a round **past** the kernel's limit be built as a revolved profile, exactly and valid, and does it agree with the kernel's fillet where both exist; (B) does the kernel's radius law (a size that varies along an edge) work, and does a size that follows the wedge angle rescue the crossed-rod edge where a constant size fails. Throwaway (CLAUDE.md rule 9). Run `node spike.mjs`, `node spike_b.mjs`, `node spike_c.mjs` (uses the OCCT of `../lineage-occt`).

## What it learned
**A. The pointed ("gothic") rod end works** (`spike.mjs`). Rod r=10, h=30; an arc of radius `s` tangent to the wall and ending on the axis at the top, revolved and cut from the rod. For `s` from 10 (the dome) to 50 (where the point reaches the bottom): the solid is **valid at every size, 3 faces**, and the volume taken off agrees with the integral worked out by hand to **1e-11** (s = 12, 15, 20, 30, 45, 50). At s = 50 the wall vanishes (2 faces); above it the construction refuses (`t = sqrt(2 r s - r^2)` is longer than the rod). Where the kernel's own fillet exists (s <= r) it agrees with the same size built as a revolved region to three decimals (s = 3, 6, 9.9, 10); at s = r both give 1047.198, and s = 10.5 continues smoothly (1134.192). So the continuation past the kernel's limit needs no kernel fillet and does not fail.

**B1. The kernel's constant fillet on crossed rods mostly works; the failures are specific** (`spike_b.mjs`, junction edges chosen as the edges between the two cylinder faces; an earlier first try that picked the wrong edges, and a "90" that was a coaxial rod, was wrong and is discarded). A rod r=10 crossed by a rod of radius rb, axes at angle alpha (90 = a T), constant sizes 0.5 to 6:
- rb = 6: all fillets and chamfers valid, at alpha 90, 60 and 30, except **two results that are valid but took nothing** (fillet 1 and chamfer 0.5 at alpha 30: the solid is valid and the volume did not change: the in_514 trap again).
- rb = 9, 9.9 (close to the main rod's radius): chamfers **fail** from size 5 (rb 9) or 2 (rb 9.9); fillets work but **INVALID at size 6, alpha 30**.
- rb = 10 (equal radii, the junction has crossing points, 6 edges): fillets work at alpha 90 and 60, **fail at alpha 30 for every size from 1**; chamfers are INVALID or fail at every size.

**B2. The radius law works** (`spike_c.mjs`). On a straight block edge (40 long) sizes 1 -> 5 and 1 -> 5 -> 1 are valid, and the volume is 3.2% and 18.8% off the closed form for flat cross-sections; the second is **not explained** (the law is a smooth curve through the points and may bulge between them; the size actually made was not measured). A size that **follows the wedge angle** (size = min(S, W * tan(a/2)), a = air-side wedge angle from the normals of the two faces at nine places along the edge) **turns the constant failures into valid results** at rb 9 alpha 30 S 6 and rb 9.9 alpha 30 S 6 (both INVALID with a constant size), and equals the constant where the wedge is wide (alpha 90). It **does not rescue the equal-radius case** (rb 10, alpha 30, 6 edges): both fail, so crossing points and chains need splitting or our own construction. A chamfer has no radius law in the kernel.

**What it means for the spec** (E34 to E36): the revolved continuation is proven for the rod end (a rim of a revolved shape); a size that follows the wedge angle is a working first remedy for narrow wedges; a "valid but nothing changed" check is needed on every round (E27); the equal-radius junction and chamfers near it stay a tier-3 refusal (or need our own construction) until built; the middle-of-edge size should be measured before it is relied on.

**B3. What size the radius law really makes** (`spike_d.mjs`, the block edge sectioned at nine places, size = sqrt(area taken / (1 - pi/4))). It is **exact at the points given**; between **two** points it is **linear** (1 -> 5 over 40 mm: 4.98, 4.21, 3.00, 1.79, 1.02 at x = 0.5, 10, 20, 30, 39.5); between **three** it is a **smooth curve, not piecewise linear** (1 -> 5 -> 1: 1.08, 3.38, 5.00, 3.38, 1.08 at x = 0.5, 10, 20, 30, 39.5; linear interpolation would give 3.0 at x = 10) and **does not overshoot** the points; three collinear points (1 -> 3 -> 5) give the straight law. So the 19% gap of B2 was my closed form (piecewise linear), not a fault of the law. **The law's start (u = 0) was the x = 40 end of the edge** (the edge's own direction, not the left end): a size at "the start" must be looked up from the edge's direction, as the old app did (it marked the start end of each edge).

**B4. The equal-radius junction: what the kernel does, and what did not rescue it** (`spike_f.mjs`, `spike_g.mjs`, `spike_e.mjs`, `in_1500`). A rod of radius 10 crossed by a rod of radius 10 - delta, at angle alpha, junction rounded with sizes 0.5, 1, 2, 4 (ok = valid and changed; I invalid; f failed):
- **Fillet:** all four sizes work from alpha 90 down to 45 for delta 1, 0.1, 0.01 (and at delta 0 for 90 and 60); at **alpha 45 with delta 0.001 or 0 all four fail**; at **alpha 30 and 20 they fail for delta 0.1, 0.01, 0.001 and work for delta 0** (exactly equal radii) or fail in part at delta 1. So the failure is a **band** of near-degenerate geometry, not "equal radii": exactly equal works at a shallow angle and nearly equal does not.
- **Chamfer:** works at delta 1 for small sizes; at delta 0.1 only the smallest two sizes; at delta 0.01 and below it fails, and at delta 0 (exactly equal) it returns an **invalid solid** at every size and angle tried.
- **118 of the 200 cases fail** (a grid chosen to be hard, not a sample of real parts).
- **Cheap retries rescue nothing:** nudging the round's own size by up to 0.01 mm (the approved snap, E33) rescued **0 of 118**; rounding the edges one after the other rescued 0 of 118 (the edges were found again by their middle point: if that is faulty the zero is not proof). The failures do not depend on the size by hairs. So E15 holds: there is no cheap automatic rescue to offer.
- **The closing (two offsets) does not work in this build:** dilating a body works (the dilation of the fused rods equals the union of the two dilated rods, 40541.5), but the **erosion fails** (the offset inward with the arc join gives an empty invalid shape; with the intersection join it gives the body back, with no round). The erosion as the dilation of the complement fails as well ("offset failed").
- **A region built by hand got near:** fill = (dilation of rod 1 meets dilation of rod 2) minus the body minus a pipe of radius s round the spine (where the two dilated surfaces meet). On rb 6, alpha 90, s = 1 it gave 10.61 against the kernel's 8.59 (+23%), but one of the two pipes comes out invalid (the volume is right, the check says no) and the cut after it is invalid, and the run took 19 s. Not a method to ship as it is.

**B5. The hand-built region, taken further (`in_1501`, `spike_e.mjs`, `spike_h.mjs`): it does not give the right fillet.**
- **The tube:** the sweep of a circle along the spine is **valid when the sweep runs in Frenet mode** (`BRepOffsetAPI_MakePipeShell.SetMode(true)`); the default mode gave an invalid tube on one of the two curves. Both are now valid with the right volume.
- **The boolean with the tube is the next problem:** a tube of exactly the fillet's radius is **tangent** to both rods along the whole spine (that is what a rolling ball is), and cutting it from the air region gives garbage (negative volumes, 0.5 to 5 s per cut). That is the same class of tangent boolean that the kernel's own fillet fails on.
- **A tube a hair larger crosses the rods instead of touching them** (radius s + 0.01, 0.001, 0.0001 mm): the result is **valid**, but the added volume is 10.27, 12.77 and 12.85 against the kernel's 8.59 on rb 6, alpha 90, s = 1: **about 50% too much**, 31 faces, 7 to 8 s.
- **The formula is wrong for curved surfaces, not only fragile:** "the air within s of both surfaces, minus the pipe" is the right fill between two flat faces (the square minus the disc), but between curved faces it also takes air beyond the contact curves. The right fill is bounded by the two contact curves (where the ball touches each surface) and the surfaces ruled between them and the crease; building those is a second construction as hard as the first, and the faces would be approximated splines, not analytic.
- **Verdict:** this route is **not** lean or robust. Stopped. The equal-radius junction stays tier 3 (E34, E35).
