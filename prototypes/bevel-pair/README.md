# Straight bevel pair (a prototype, `in_719`)

**What it was meant to learn:** whether a straight bevel gear and its mate can be made from the loft the worm wheel already uses (two sections joined by ruled faces, scaled toward the pitch cone's apex), with the shafts at any angle, and whether the two teeth really fit.

**How the tooth is made (`bevel.ts`):** the Tredgold back cone tooth. The gear's equivalent spur gear has `z / cos(delta)` teeth, the same module and pressure angle; its involute tooth is developed on the back cone (the cone through the pitch circle, perpendicular to the pitch cone), carried round the axis (angle divided by `cos delta`) and projected through the pitch cone's apex onto a plane across the axis. The small end is the same outline scaled toward the apex, so the faces of the tooth are ruled by lines through the apex, as a straight bevel tooth's are. Pitch angle `tan delta = sin(sigma) / (mate / z + cos(sigma))` (`sigma` the angle between the shafts), cone distance `R = m z / (2 sin delta)`.

**What was read (and what was not):**
- **Autodesk Inventor 2022 Help, "Basic Geometric Calculation for Bevel Gears"** (the page as a PDF the human sent, `in_722`; the text is CC BY-NC-SA 3.0, the formulas are used, not the text; its units are ISO / DIN style): pitch angle `tan delta1 = sin(sigma) / (u + cos(sigma))`, `delta2 = sigma - delta1`; outside pitch diameter `m_et z`; outer cone distance `Re = d / (2 sin delta)`; face width ratio `b / Re`; equivalent teeth `z / cos(delta)`; dedendum reduction `k = 0.02 (17 - zn)` (when positive); addendum `h_ae = m_et (ha* + x - k)`; dedendum `h_fe = m_et (ha* + c* - x)`; outside diameter `d_e + 2 h_ae cos(delta)`; root diameter `d_e - 2 h_fe cos(delta)`; vertex distance `Re cos(delta) - h_ae sin(delta)`; face angle `delta + atan(h_ae / Re)`, root angle `delta - atan(h_fe / Re)`; tooth thickness `s = m_et (pi/2 + 2 x tan(alpha_t) + x_t)` (x = x1 = -x2 the unit correction, x_t the change of thickness); the correction without undercut. **Not on the page** (its sub-pages were not sent): the defaults of `ha*` and `c*` (used here, unverified: 1 and 0.2), the distribution of the unit corrections between pinion and gear (x = 0 here), and the face width design. The prototype now uses these formulas.
- ANSI/AGMA ISO 23509-B17 (= ISO 23509:2016) and AGMA 937-A12: **previews only** (the human sent them, `in_722`): contents, scope and nomenclature lists; no formulas. They show where the rest is: 23509 section 7 (tooth depth, root and face angles, pinion face width, tooth thickness) and 8 (the undercut check); AGMA 937 section 6.1.6 (face width), 6.1.9 (pressure angle), 6.2.8 (mean addendum factor, Table 6), 6.2.15 (Table 10, the straight, zerol and spiral bevel formulas) and Figure 8 (suggested numbers of teeth in the pinion).
- Boston Gear, *Engineering Information: Miter and Bevel Gears* (a maker's catalogue; https://documents.mrosupply.com/product_documents/SH/SH82-P/1930-bg_engineering-info-miter-and-bevel-gears.pdf; read 2026-11-17): 20 degree pressure angle; AGMA long and short addendum system for stock pinions and gears (named, its numbers not given); straight tooth formulas: whole depth `hT = 2.188/P + 0.002 in`, addendum `a = 1/P`, dedendum `b = hT - a`, clearance `c = hT - 2a`, circular tooth thickness `1.5708/P`, pitch angle of the pinion `tan^-1(Np/NG)` (the gear's `90 - that`), outside diameter `Dp + 2 a cos(pitch angle)`; spiral bevels 35 degrees, pinion left hand, gear right hand. These agree with the Autodesk formulas to within 0.012 m of the dedendum (`ha* = 1`, `c* = 0.2`).
- A search result (not a source read to the page): the virtual tooth count is `z / cos(delta)` and the cone distance `R = pitch radius / sin(delta)`; sources disagree on the face width: a maximum of `R / 3` and a minimum of `10 m` from lecture notes; `R / 3` is used here.
- **Not read in full: the standards** (AGMA 2005, ISO 23509, DIN 3971 are paywalled and were not reachable); the pages of KHK, Autodesk and Engineers Edge could not be fetched in this session (their hosts are not allowed). So the proportions are a maker's stock-gear numbers, **UNVERIFIED as the standard's**, the long-and-short addendum is not applied (both gears have `a = m`: right for a 1:1 miter pair, not the standard for other ratios), and a gear whose tooth is undercut or pointed is not checked.

**What it learned (`bevel.test.ts`, run in the app's kernel; re-run with the Autodesk formulas, the overlaps are the same to the second decimal):** the pair is built as two lofts, each a piece of its own. Fit is measured as the other worm test does it: the two bodies intersected, the volume left over is the overlap (module 2, face a third of the cone distance, no backlash):

| pair (shafts) | at the computed place | teeth in each other (turned half a pitch) | 0.3 mm closer | 0.3 mm further |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 / 20 (90) | no overlap | 120 mm3 | 2.1 mm3 | none |
| 20 / 30 (90) | no overlap | 164 mm3 | 3.7 mm3 | none |
| 24 / 24 (60) | no overlap | 217 mm3 | 2.4 mm3 | none |

So the teeth fit at the computed centre and the shaft angle can be anything (60 degrees was tried). Each body takes about 1 to 3 s to make. **Not tried:** a bore or a hub, a face width other than a third, tooth counts that undercut (under about 17 / cos delta), the long and short addendum, backlash, a gear that is a body with a thickness beyond its face.
