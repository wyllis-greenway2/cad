# wheel-fast (prototype, `in_704`, `dec_gear_speed`)

**Meant to learn:** where the time of a worm wheel goes, how much of it can be taken out without changing the wheel, and whether the wheel's loft is reliable.

**Files.** `sections.ts` is a copy of `app/src/operation/wheelsections.ts` with the changes below (each can be set back by an environment variable); `bench.test.ts` compares it with the app's (time, and the largest distance between corresponding points); `naming-quick-box.patch` is the change to `app/src/compiler/kernel/occt/facenames.mjs` (`git apply` it from `app/`). Run from `app/`: `ZS=20,40,80 node --test ../prototypes/wheel-fast/bench.test.ts` (`W='{"module":3,"diameter":30,"starts":3,"width":14}'` for another worm). The app itself is unchanged.

**Learned (2026-11-17, in a sandbox with few cores, one run each).**
1. The sections were **already one gap** (the contour is found over two pitches and then turned z times), so "one pitch only" is not what costs. The time is `material`: every grid node scans the worm's turns in 0.03 radian steps, about 800 steps each (more with more teeth), and every crossing is found by 24 bisections that each scan again.
2. Faster sections, same shapes: bisect 10 times instead of 24 (the cells are 0.05 mm: 0.0001 mm); work out exact crossings only for chains that can pass the filter; test every 4th node first and the cells next to the contour fully; scan the worm's turns in steps of 0.2 mm along its thread (at most 0.1 radian) instead of 0.03 radian. **3.4 s to 0.5 s for 40 teeth, 6.9 s to 0.9 s for 80 (5 to 7 times); the points differ from the app's by at most 0.003 mm** over four worms (module 1 to 3, one to three starts). That is a hundredth of the loft's own 0.05 mm (`worm-wheel-loft`).
3. **Naming the loft's faces** (`nameFaces`, `surfacesOf`) was 4.3 s of 10.3 s for 40 teeth, all of it `f.boundingBox` of the 760 freeform faces (5.5 ms each: 4143 ms). The quick box (`BRepBndLib.Add(.., false)`, as `bodies.mjs` already uses for a body) is 8 ms in all. Kernel time for the whole wheel: 40 teeth 10.3 s to 6.0 s, 20 teeth 7.1 s to 5.0 s, with the **same volume** (51093.555 and 12948.620 mm3).
4. **The loft is not reliable, with or without these changes.** The smooth loft through the 5 sections comes out as a solid of the right volume that the kernel's validity check (`BRepCheck_Analyzer`) refuses, so the step fails ("the loft could not be made"). Today: 12, 20, 40 teeth work, 30 and 60 fail, and with 24, 28, 32, 44, 48 only 36 works (module 2, 24 mm worm, one start). With the faster sections the set shifts (20, 24, 32, 44, 48 fail, 28 works): a change of a few thousandths of a millimetre in a section decides it. So about half the tooth counts do not make a wheel, which is as much "crashing" as the slowness. The kernel build has no `ShapeFix`, so the solid cannot be repaired in place.
5. A **ruled** loft through the same sections is valid every time tried, but rougher: volume 0.2 per cent over the smooth one with 5 sections (51203.9 against 51093.7 at 40 teeth), 0.05 per cent with 9, and 9 sections cost more than the smooth loft did (13.5 s against 5.9 s).

6. **Why the loft was refused (`in_705`), and the fix.** For 24 teeth the analyzer (`BRepCheck_Analyzer`, asked face by face and edge by edge) rejects 18 of 458 faces (16 B-spline side faces and both flat ends) and 24 of 1368 edges, in a regular pattern (the middle of the gap's bottom, two faces a tooth, in a group of three teeth); a wheel that works has none. The solid itself is whole: its volume is the same to every digit before and after the fix. What is wrong is the edges' *same parameter* property (each edge's 3D curve and its curves on the two faces must run at the same pace, within the edge's tolerance), which the smooth loft through the sections does not always leave. **`BRepLib.SameParameter(shape, 1e-5, false)` on the loft makes the check pass** (`loft-same-parameter.patch`: three lines in the loft op, for a smooth loft only). With it every tooth count tried builds, 12, 20, 24, 28, 30, 32, 36, 40, 44, 48 and 60 (module 2, 24 mm worm; before, 24, 28, 30, 32, 44, 48 and 60 failed), the volumes agree with the ones that worked before (51093.55 at 40 teeth, 41453.38 at 36), a wheel with an 8 mm bore subtracts exactly the bore's volume (502.65 mm3), and `worm.test.ts` and `loft.test.ts` (19 tests) pass. The kernel build has `ShapeFix_Solid` and `ShapeFix_Face` but no `ShapeFix_Shape`, and `BRepCheck_Result` is not exposed, so which of the check's statuses it was is not read directly; the cure is what shows it is this one.
7. **All three together** (the quick box, the faster sections, the same-parameter repair), the whole of making the wheel (the page's sections and the kernel's answer): 24 teeth 6.5 s (it failed), 40 teeth 7.0 s (it was 13.7 s: 3.4 s of sections and 10.3 s of kernel), 60 teeth 11.7 s (it failed). The rest is mostly the loft itself and its checks, and the mesh.

**Not tried:** a loft of one tooth turned and joined; a loft in two halves; a 200-tooth wheel; other worms (starts 2 and 3, other modules) through the kernel (the sections were compared for them, the loft was not); the whole test suite with the three changes; whether the larger tolerance a repaired edge may take matters to a later boolean (a bore cut and the volume were fine).

**Landed (`in_706`).** The three changes are in the app. The naming one is not exactly `naming-quick-box.patch`: the full suite showed that a loft's own sides are named by comparing their box with the box of the sketch's corners (`test/facenames.test.ts`, a bent loft), which needs the exact box, so the quick box is used only for a shape with more than 64 freeform faces (`QUICK_FROM`); an ordinary body is named as before. The patches here are what was measured, not what was landed.
